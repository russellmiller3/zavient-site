AI agent or AI assistant: which does your team need?

Bottom line
  • Pick an AI assistant (generative AI) if the work is drafting, summarizing or answering, and a person acts on it2,6.
  • Pick an AI agent if the work crosses several systems, needs judgment calls, and a named person approves risky steps6,10.
  • Pick neither if the steps never change; plain rules automation is cheaper and more predictable8,5.
  • Pick short, supervised agent tasks over long unattended ones; the best agent finished under a third of test office tasks14,7.
  • Ask every vendor who approves and what it bills; only four of nine we checked gate risky steps by default33,6.

What is the real difference?

An AI assistant is generative AI: it drafts, summarizes or answers, and a person acts on it2,3. An AI agent is agentic AI: given a goal, it plans steps and acts in your systems1,5. What makes or breaks an agent is what it may touch and who approves risky steps10,11.

Which one fits your team?

1. Start with an assistant (most teams, first 90 days)

An ops team of 10 to 30 whose work is drafting, summarizing and answering policy questions, with no system changes6,17.

  1. Ops leadPicks three repeat tasks, such as the weekly report, vendor emails and procedure lookups
  2. Staff memberAsks the assistant for a draft
  3. Staff memberChecks and edits it, then sends or files it
  4. Ops leadOnce a month, compares time per task against the starting baseline

Cost: Per seat: Microsoft 365 Copilot Business is $21 a user a month, so 15 people cost about $31521. Claude Team Standard read $25 monthly or $20 annual in our 22-26 Nov check; we didn't capture ChatGPT Business's figure26,25.

2. One narrow agent with an approval step (service team)

High-volume, repeatable requests with clear rules, such as order status, password resets and simple returns8,23.

  1. Head of serviceWrites the rules and lists the actions the agent may take
  2. AdminGrants read access plus only those actions, and turns on confirmation for refunds, cancels and account changes24,30
  3. AgentAnswers the customer and runs the allowed lookups
  4. Service repApproves any refund or account closure the agent proposes30
  5. Head of serviceReviews a sample of resolved conversations each week, and tracks resolution time against the starting baseline

Cost: Vendor list prices, read 26 Nov 2026: Agentforce is $0.10 an action, about $120 a month in Salesforce's own order-status example23. Intercom Fin is $0.99 per resolved outcome plus seats from $29; Zendesk charges $1.50 to $2.00 per automated resolution30,29.

3. A back-office agent with a role-owned gate (AP exceptions)

A finance or ops team with many exceptions a month across email, the ERP (finance system) and documents, each needing a judgment call6,17.

  1. ControllerRecords today's exception count and handling time as the baseline
  2. ControllerSets the decision rules and the approval threshold, such as any payment change or credit over a set amount
  3. IT or adminConnects the agent to the inbox and ERP with the narrowest rights, and no payment release10,11
  4. AgentReads the invoice, matches the PO, finds the gap, drafts the vendor email and proposes a fix
  5. AP specialistApproves, edits or rejects each proposal in an approval queue22,31
  6. ControllerSigns off anything over the threshold and reviews the log of agent actions monthly12

Cost: Microsoft Copilot Studio is $200 per 25,000 credits a month, or pay as you go; credits used per action vary21. UiPath and ServiceNow price agents by consumption units or per action, with no dollar price published31,28.

Where does your team stay in charge?

  • Ops lead or system owner decides what the agent may touch. Give it the fewest functions, permissions and autonomy the task needs10,11.
  • The role owner, such as the AP specialist, controller or service rep, approves high-impact or hard-to-reverse actions before they run10,11. ChatGPT Work and Claude in Chrome build this check in25,26.
  • A team lead reviews a sample of agent work on a schedule. NIST's risk framework asks that human oversight be defined, assessed and documented12.
  • Leadership decides which decisions agents never make, and matches controls to each agent's level of autonomy9,7. Most real deployments today run under significant human oversight3.

What does each look like in practice?

Illustrative, not a client case: a vendor emails an invoice that is $480 over its purchase order. With an assistant, the AP specialist pastes both in and gets the gap and a polite email draft2. How do you spot agent washing and other mistakes?

  • Buying a relabeled chatbot: Gartner calls this agent washing and counts only about 130 real agentic vendors6,8. Ask for a demo where it chooses its own steps on a case it hasn't seen.
  • Giving the agent more access than the task needs: coding agents at Replit and PocketOS deleted production databases19,20. Grant the narrowest permissions, and no delete or payment rights without a person10.
  • Treating an instruction as a control: Replit's code freeze was only an instruction, and NIST saw hijack success reach 81%19,13. Enforce limits in permissions and approval steps, not in the prompt11.
  • Expecting long tasks to finish unattended: the best agent fully finished 30.3% of simulated office tasks14,16. Keep each task short and add checkpoints where a person looks.
  • No baseline, then a surprise bill: Gartner expects over 40% of agentic projects canceled by 2027 over cost, unclear value or weak controls6. Measure time and error rate first, and model per-action costs from our check33.
  • The wrong person confirms: Agentforce's confirmation asks the user in the conversation, who may be your customer24. Route refunds and payment changes to a named role in an approval queue30,31,22.

Questions buyers ask before they choose

Which workflow should we give an agent first?

A high-volume, well-defined one with clear rules and a low cost of error, such as order status8,23. Keep money and account changes with a person10,25. Pilot it on one team, with one owner and one measured number.

Is "AI agent" just rebadged chatbot marketing?

Sometimes. Gartner calls it agent washing and estimates only about 130 real agentic vendors among thousands6. Ask the vendor to show the agent choosing its own steps on a new case8.

Is agentic AI safe to use in a business?

With limits. Give it the narrowest access and require a person to approve high-impact actions10,11. Gartner says fully autonomous agents are not ready for most enterprise uses7.

What's the difference between RPA and agentic AI?

RPA (robotic process automation) follows fixed rules; an agent decides its own steps5. Gartner's advice: automation for routine workflows, agents when decisions are needed6,8.

Is ChatGPT generative AI or agentic AI?

Both, depending on the feature. Chat is generative AI; ChatGPT Work runs tasks in a cloud browser and asks before hard-to-reverse steps2,25.

Is agentic AI the same as AI agents?

Close. An AI agent is one system doing a task; agentic AI often means several agents working toward one goal3,32. Both usually run on a generative model that plans and calls tools4,5.

Should you buy a tool or build your own?

Buy: Buy when the process is standard, such as service desk, order status or IT tickets, and you have no AI engineers17. MIT found bought tools and partnerships reached deployment about 67% of the time, against about 33% for internal builds (self-reported)17. Many agents now ship inside software you already pay for21,23.

Build: Build or configure when your own data and rules are the edge, and you need control of the approval gate and the activity log12,10. Start with a fixed workflow and add agent steps only where needed5. Platforms such as Copilot Studio, Agentforce and UiPath are a middle path: you configure, they host21,23,31.

When should you pick neither?

  • The steps never change: use rules automation, such as Power Automate or UiPath robots6,8. Agents trade cost and speed for flexibility you won't need5.
  • You need one smart step inside a fixed process: use a workflow with a single AI step to classify, extract or draft5. That runs on a predefined path, not an agent's own plan5.
  • The process itself is broken: fix the form, the handoff or the data first8. Gartner warns that agents bolted onto old processes cap the return8.
  • You need a score or forecast, not content or actions: that is predictive AI. See our generative AI vs predictive AI comparison.
