Zapier vs Make vs Workato: which automation tool fits your ops team?

Bottom line
  • Pick Zapier if non-technical ops staff will build and own the flows, and you want published prices1,40.
  • Pick Make if volume is high and budget is tight; its native approval step is still Enterprise closed beta18,21.
  • Pick Workato if you run an ERP and need dev, test and live stages; Vendr reports a $64,544 yearly median28,37.
  • Pick on billing unit, not sticker price: Zapier bills tasks; Make bills credits, including empty trigger checks2,13.

What is the real difference?

Zapier is easiest for non-technical staff and Make is cheapest at volume, while Workato adds the IT controls, such as separate test stages40,18,1,28. All three run multi-step workflows with AI steps; the choice turns on billing unit, approvals and testing before go-live20,23,2,13,21.

Which one fits your team?

A 50-person team with no IT fits Zapier, a 150-person team running cross-team handoffs fits Zapier or Make, and a 400-person firm with an ERP fits Workato4,20,28.

1. About 50 staff, no IT team: invoice intake to approval

One ops lead builds, the controller approves, and volume is in the low thousands a month1,40. Pick Zapier, because Make's native approval step is still closed beta4,21.

  1. Ops leadPilot one site or supplier group on a monthly plan you can stop
  2. Ops leadTime invoice handling for two weeks, so there is a before-and-after number
  3. AP (accounts payable) clerkForward each supplier invoice email to the intake address
  4. Ops leadSet an AI step to pull vendor, amount and due date
  5. ControllerApprove or reject in Zapier's Request Approval step; decisions are logged
  6. Ops leadPost approved bills to the accounting app
  7. Ops leadReview failed runs and task use each week

Cost: 2,000 invoices at five action steps uses 10,000 tasks: $129 a month on Pro, about 6.5 cents an invoice, or $169 on Team, billed yearly1,2. Approval needs Pro or above, and each reviewer needs a Zapier login4,5.

2. About 150 staff: closed deal to billing and onboarding, with an AI agent

Sales, finance and onboarding hand work across apps, and you want an agent to draft the setup3,20. Zapier or Make both fit; Make's agents show each decision in a reasoning panel20.

  1. Sales opsMark the deal closed-won in the CRM (customer relationship management system)
  2. AI agent, run by the ops leadDraft the customer record and onboarding checklist
  3. Finance leadApprove the billing setup before anything is created
  4. Onboarding leadTake the task list and assign owners
  5. Ops leadClear agent runs that stopped to ask for input

Cost: Zapier bills agents in 'activities', apart from tasks: 400 a month free, 1,500 on Pro1,3. Make bills agents in credits; with your own AI key, you also pay the AI provider12,14.

3. About 400 staff with an ERP: order-to-cash with change control

You run SAP, Oracle or Workday, change flows often, and auditors ask who changed what28,31. Gartner says Workato is mainly used by integration specialists, so plan for one38,41.

  1. Integration ownerBuild and change flows in DEV
  2. QA (quality assurance) leadTest each change in TEST
  3. ControllerApprove each deployment to PROD
  4. ITConnect the ERP (enterprise resource planning system) through Workato's on-site agent, which only dials out
  5. ControllerApprove invoices over a set amount in Slack or Teams
  6. Ops leadReview the audit log each month

Cost: Quote-only; Vendr reports a $64,544 yearly median and a $20,370 to $182,649 range, not Workato prices37,23. Vendr also reports $10,000 to $50,000+ to implement, and test-stage runs count as usage37,28.

Where does your team stay in charge?

People keep control: the controller approves payments above a set amount, a named ops lead owns each workflow, and agents stop to ask a person at set points4,33,3.

  • The controller or finance lead approves payments and invoices above a set amount. Zapier and Workato have built-in approval steps; Make's is Enterprise closed beta4,33,21.
  • A person reviews every item at first, then less as error rates fall, per Workato's own guidance. NIST says human roles in AI oversight must be clearly defined33,43.
  • A named ops lead owns each workflow and its failures. Make switches a scenario off when its failed-run store fills, so someone must watch it16.
  • Agents stop and ask a person: Zapier's at their run cap, Make's at stop points, Workato's at business approvals3,20,32. The EU AI Act asks that people can override and stop AI44.

What do teams get wrong when they choose?

Teams go wrong by missing overage and empty-trigger costs, editing live flows with no test stage, and assuming every platform has an approval step1,13,28,21.

  • Costs jump past the plan limit: Zapier overage runs 1.25× to 2.5× the base rate, and Make's extra credits cost 25% more1,18. Fix: buy headroom, and set Zapier usage alerts or Make's optional auto-purchase2,18.
  • Empty trigger checks burn Make credits: each check costs one, even when nothing new arrived13. Fix: check less often, and price the flow with checks included13.
  • Editing live flows: Zapier and Make have no separate test stage, though Zapier keeps version history1. Fix: test on a copy, or pick Workato if you need formal change control28,29.
  • Assuming every tool has an approval step: Make's is Enterprise closed beta, and Zapier's can't sit inside a loop21,5. Fix: prove the controller's approval step in a trial before you buy4.
  • Forgetting that connections hold the keys: in Feb 2025, an outsider reached Zapier code repositories holding some customer data9,10. Fix, on any platform: give each connection the least access it needs, use company sign-in (SSO) and rotate tokens10,7.
  • Agent runs add usage fast: Zapier allows up to 40 activities a run, Make bills AI tokens plus credits, Workato AI steps cost extra3,12,27. Fix: set run caps and per-team credit limits, and check agent use weekly3,17.

Questions buyers ask before they choose

Is Make cheaper than Zapier?

Yes, usually, per unit: Make's help example is $9 for 10,000 credits, against $129 for 10,000 Zapier tasks billed yearly18,1. But the units differ, and Make charges for every trigger check13. Price your real flow, as our table does.

Does Make charge for trigger checks that find nothing?

Yes: each trigger check counts once, whatever it returns13.

Why doesn't Workato publish its prices?

Its editions are sold by contract, so the pricing page says 'Get a quote'23. Self-service Pro is published at $75 to $1,275 a month24. Vendr reports a $64,544 yearly median for contracts37.

Can a person approve a step before the workflow continues?

Yes, on Zapier and Workato; on Make, only in an Enterprise closed beta4,34,21.

Who owns the workflow when it breaks?

Name one owner per workflow, usually the ops lead for that process. Give them error tools: Zapier has autoreplay, and Make has retry and rollback handlers1,15.

Do we need a developer to run Workato?

Not always, but plan for an integration specialist; Gartner says that's who mainly uses Workato38. Zapier is built for non-developers, and G2 reviewers praise its ease of use40.

Should you buy a tool or build your own?

Buy: Buy one of these when your flows join standard apps and no engineer has time to own custom code38,40. Most firms now buy AI tools: 76% of enterprise AI use cases in 2025, up from 53%45. MIT found bought or partnered tools reach deployment about twice as often as internal builds46,47.

Build: Build your own integration code only when the workflow is your edge and an engineer will own it46. Self-hosting means you own uptime and patches, a point Zapier stresses about self-hosted rivals8. MIT found only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production, so start narrow46,47.

When should you pick neither?

Pick neither when you need self-hosting (n8n), a full integration platform for a large ERP estate, or Power Automate because your firm lives in Microsoft 36548,38.

  • You have an engineer and data must stay on your own servers: n8n's free self-hosted Community Edition fits48. Its cloud plans bill per run, not per step48.
  • You run a large ERP estate and need a full integration platform: compare Gartner's other Leaders, such as Boomi, MuleSoft and SAP38.
  • Your firm lives in Microsoft 365: Microsoft, maker of Power Automate, is also a Gartner Leader38.
  • You run a few two-step flows, under 100 runs a month: Zapier's free plan covers it1.
