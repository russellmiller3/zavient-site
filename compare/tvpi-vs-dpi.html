What is the difference between TVPI and DPI?

Distributions to paid-in capital (DPI) counts only cash a fund has paid back, while total value to paid-in (TVPI) also counts what it still holds.1,2 The gap is residual value to paid-in (RVPI): TVPI equals DPI plus RVPI.2 RVPI is the manager's estimate, not cash.2,17

How do TVPI, DPI, RVPI, MOIC and IRR compare, side by side?

Three of these multiples share one denominator, paid-in capital, while IRR adds timing and multiple on invested capital (MOIC) usually measures deals, not your stake.2,1

Paid-in capital means money actually called from you, not your commitment.2 Calls for management fees count too, which is why young funds start below cost.20

How do you compare two funds fairly?

Compare net figures for funds of the same vintage and strategy, use IRR without the credit line, and add a public market equivalent.7,3,18

Family office guides list the same toolkit: IRR, DPI, TVPI, MOIC and PME, each read by vintage year and strategy.14 The checks below put that into practice.

How slow are distributions right now?

Distributions run at about half their long-run pace, so a low DPI on a 2017 to 2021 fund is common, not alarming on its own.9,8

Private credit pays back sooner than buyout or venture, because it collects contractual interest along the way.12 So judge each strategy against its own pace.

Which matters more, TVPI or DPI?

TVPI matters more while a fund is still investing, DPI matters more from about year six, and neither means much without the vintage beside it.7,1

1. Young fund, years 0 to 5: watch TVPI and question the marks

DPI sits near zero by design while the fund calls capital for fees and deals.20 TVPI and IRR are the only signals, and both lean on the manager's estimates.1,17

  1. Ops analystLog every call, distribution and NAV from each capital account statement
  2. Ops analystRecompute DPI, RVPI and TVPI from those cash flows, and flag gaps with the GP's figures
  3. Head of investment operationsAsk the GP for net IRR with and without the credit line3
  4. PrincipalHold off on ranking the manager before year six7

Cost: No new tools: the statements you already get, plus a spreadsheet. Ask managers for the ILPA Performance Template, which pairs each metric with its contributions and distributions.22

2. Maturing fund, year 6 on: lead with DPI and test the remaining NAV

Funds take six to eight years to settle into a final quartile, so later ranks mean more.6 By then, cash back is the test and unsold value is a question.2

  1. Ops analystTrack DPI against funds of the same vintage and strategy7
  2. Head of investment operationsAsk how the remaining NAV was valued and when the GP expects to sell13
  3. Ops analystRun a PME against an index that fits the fund's risk18
  4. PrincipalWeigh any re-up on cash returned, not on estimates alone

Cost: Staff time each quarter. Public pension tables, such as Oregon's quarterly list of every fund it holds, give a free check on peers.21

3. Mixed book of buyout, credit and venture: compare like with like

Private credit pays back sooner than buyout or venture, so one DPI bar for all three is unfair.12 Compare each fund with its own strategy and vintage.7

  1. Ops analystTag each fund by strategy and vintage, using your benchmark's vintage definition6
  2. Ops analystSet DPI and TVPI expectations per strategy from dated benchmark data12,11
  3. Head of investment operationsSet the family's cash needs beside expected distributions13
  4. PrincipalPace new commitments against that cash plan

Cost: Staff time, plus any benchmark data you license. Date every benchmark figure, because recent vintages move each quarter.11

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your team keeps every judgment: an operations lead signs off the numbers, an analyst challenges the valuations, and the principal decides peers, timing and re-ups.13,17,6

  • Head of investment operations or COO: owns the cash flow ledger and signs off each recomputed figure. Of 155 family offices in the Americas surveyed, 73% call investment reporting too manual.13
  • Analyst: challenges the valuation behind RVPI before any re-up. In one study of funds from 1988 to 2016, venture NAVs ran aggressive next to later cash flows, more than buyout, especially after 2000.17
  • Principal or investment committee: picks the peer group and decides when a fund is too young to judge. Funds take six to eight years to settle.6
  • Whoever owns the manager relationship: calls the general partner (GP) about each flagged mismatch. The Washington State Investment Board notes its IRR can differ from the GP's.20

When do TVPI and DPI mislead you?

TVPI and DPI mislead early in a fund's life, when estimates drive value, when credit lines delay calls, and when gross, net or recallable money gets mixed.20,3,2

  • Reading DPI too early: fees and early calls keep a young fund's multiples low, a pattern known as the J-curve.20 Hold rankings until year six or later.7
  • Treating RVPI as cash: NAV is the manager's estimate, and venture NAVs ran higher than buyout ones in a 1988 to 2016 study.17 Ask how each large holding was valued.2
  • Letting a credit line flatter IRR: in ILPA's example, a two-year line lifts IRR from 6.62% to 7.98% while TVPI falls.3 MSCI puts the median line's lift near 100 basis points for recent buyout and real estate funds.5
  • Mixing gross and net: deal-level MOIC is gross of fees, while fund TVPI should be net of fees and carry.2 Keep each comparison on one basis.
  • Counting recallable money as gone for good: recallable distributions sit in DPI's top line, so the manager can call that cash back.1 Track them separately.
  • Ranking on one quarter: 85% of funds by IRR pass through three or four quartiles before they settle.6 Compare with the same vintage, and expect ranks to move.7

Questions family offices ask about TVPI and DPI

What is DPI in private equity?

DPI is the cash a fund has distributed to its limited partners (LPs), divided by the capital they have paid in.1,2 A DPI of 0.5x means half your paid-in money has come back.

What is TVPI?

TVPI is cash distributed plus the fund's remaining net asset value, divided by paid-in capital.1,2 It adds the manager's estimate of unsold holdings to the cash already returned.

Can DPI be higher than TVPI?

No, not on one consistent basis, because TVPI is DPI plus RVPI.2 If a statement shows DPI above TVPI, check for mismatched dates or bases, or a negative NAV.

Is TVPI the same as MOIC?

No, not usually: TVPI is a net multiple of your paid-in capital, while MOIC usually measures deals, gross of fees.2 Firms use the terms loosely, so check the label and the denominator.

What is a good DPI for a private equity fund?

A good DPI depends on the fund's age and vintage, because recent funds are paying back slowly.8,12 In one vendor's 2026 data, the median 2021 private equity fund had returned 0.16x.12

Does paid-in capital include management fees?

Yes, capital called for fees counts as paid-in, which is one reason young funds show low multiples.20,2

How do subscription lines change TVPI and DPI?

Subscription lines delay capital calls, which lifts early IRR and trims the multiple once interest is paid.3,4 One study found IRR up 1.9 points and the multiple down 0.02 on average.4

What is a public market equivalent?

A public market equivalent (PME) asks what a fund's cash flows would have earned in a public index.18 A Kaplan-Schoar PME above 1 means the fund beat that index after fees.18

When can you trust a fund's quartile rank?

Trust a quartile rank only after six to eight years, when private equity funds typically settle into their final quartile.6 Before then, ranks move a lot.6

Do managers have to report TVPI and DPI the same way?

No, because a US appeals court vacated the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) private fund rules, quarterly statements included, on 5 June 2024.23 ILPA's Performance Template is industry guidance for funds starting on or after 1 January 2026.22

Should you buy fund analytics or build your own?

Buy extraction if you hold many funds and have no data team, and build the calculation layer if you need one method and an audit trail.16,15

Buy: Buy extraction when you hold dozens of funds and have no data team. Tools built for alternatives, such as Canoe, read capital account statements. Canoe reports accuracy consistently above 90%, its own claim.16

Build: Build the calculation layer when you need one method and a page reference for every figure. Even top models fail most complex spreadsheet calculations, so let AI extract and a plain calculator do the maths.15 ILPA's template gives the cash flow fields to build around.22

When should you look past both metrics?

Look past both metrics when a fund is too young, when the question is whether it beat stocks, or when the real issue is your family's cash plan.21,18,13

  • The fund is under three years old: neither multiple means much yet, and Oregon labels such figures not meaningful.21 Track calls against your plan instead.
  • You want to know whether private equity beat stocks: use a PME or direct alpha, not TVPI alone.18,19
  • The real question is your family's cash needs: model future calls and distributions instead of ranking funds.13
