What is the real difference?

A reporting platform rolls up investments across custodians and funds, while a ledger keeps each entity's books.9,4 Document, bill pay and customer relationship management (CRM) tools sit around both; the plumbing between them is what breaks.2,10 By our count, 15 of 16 products name AI features, with differing client-data terms.5,11

Which one fits your team?

Lean offices fit QuickBooks plus Kubera or Asora, multi-entity offices add FundCount or Eton, and adviser-led offices fit Addepar or Masttro.3,4,11

1. Lean office, one or two entities, mostly custodied assets

Pick a general ledger plus light reporting while entities are few, since QuickBooks covers one company per subscription.57,3 Kubera or Asora adds the net-worth view.22,12

  1. ControllerKeep the books in QuickBooks Online Advanced
  2. ControllerTie ledger cash to custodian statements before each report goes out
  3. Ops leadTrack net worth in Kubera, or Asora for fuller reporting
  4. ControllerCode and route bills in BILL
  5. Second approverRelease each payment and confirm any change to wire details
  6. COOGive staff one approved business AI plan, not personal accounts
  7. PrincipalReview the monthly net-worth report

Cost: QuickBooks Online Advanced is $340 a month, BILL Team $65 per user per month and Kubera Black $2,500 a year.3,29,22 With two BILL users that is about $8,140 a year by our math; Asora instead of Kubera starts at $900 a month.12,8

2. Office of 10 to 20 staff, many entities and private funds

Pick partnership accounting plus a reporting platform once partnerships, trusts and funds multiply.4,9 A UBS survey puts the average family office at 12 staff, and Deloitte's at 15.40,38

  1. Ops leadTime this quarter's report from close to the principal's desk, as the before number
  2. ControllerRun partnership accounting in FundCount or Eton
  3. Ops leadFeed custodian and ledger data into Asora, Aleta or Landytech
  4. Ops leadTrial the reporting platform on one entity before moving the rest
  5. Investment ops analystLoad capital calls and K-1 tax forms through Canoe or Arch
  6. ControllerCheck every extracted amount and wire detail before it posts
  7. COOApprove which tools may use AI, and on what data terms

Cost: FundCount starts at $35,899 a year for a single family office, Aleta at $1,000 a month, while Eton, Canoe and Arch publish no price.4,18,25,5 FundCount plus Aleta comes to about $47,899 a year by our math, before fees, document tools and onboarding.4,18

3. Adviser-led office with complex holdings

Pick an institutional reporting platform and outsource the books when the team is thin.1,9 Expertise is the reason to outsource for 87% of North American family offices surveyed.1

  1. COOHave an adviser run the selection, since Simple found that is when selection is structured
  2. Ops leadImplement Addepar or Masttro for reporting
  3. Outsourced accountantKeep the books and send monthly files
  4. Ops leadConnect an approved AI assistant through a read-only MCP link, such as Masttro's
  5. PrincipalSign off the quarterly report

Cost: Addepar publishes no price and quotes each deal, and Masttro quotes a flat annual fee.15,11 For scale, published reporting prices start at $900 a month for Asora and $1,000 for Aleta.8,18

Where does your team stay in charge?

People keep every payment, posting and data-access decision: a second approver releases payments, the controller checks extracted figures, and the COO sets AI data rules.41,2

  • A second person approves every payment and every change to payee or wire details. Business email compromise drew 24,768 complaints in 2025, and only investment fraud cost more.41,10
  • The controller checks extracted capital call and K-1 figures before they post. Arch builds a person into the loop, with 200+ validation checks.31,24
  • The COO sets an AI policy first, approves each tool and keeps sensitive data apart from AI, as offices with formal controls do.2 ISO/IEC 42001 is a standard to ask vendors about.42,25
  • The ops lead decides whether an AI assistant may only read or may also write. Masttro's MCP link is read-only, while Asora's proposes changes a user confirms.43,44

What do teams get wrong when they choose?

Offices go wrong by building their own system of record, trusting an FAQ over the contract, and skipping implementation cost and capacity.2,48

  • Building your own system of record: home-built views tend to work, but feeds, reconciliation and history break and hang on one person.2 Fix: buy the plumbing and build the view.9
  • Reading the FAQ, not the contract: Masttro's FAQ rules out AI training, but its privacy policy says models train on anonymized data.11,48 Fix: get data terms in writing, since Canoe's FAQ says client data fine-tunes its models.5
  • Underinvesting, then patching: of 354 single family offices surveyed, 34% underinvested and 38% only moderately invested in operations technology.38 Fix: budget for upgrades, which 51% of North American family offices surveyed call the top long-term risk.1
  • Skipping implementation cost: vendors typically quote two to four months, and Asora's implementation fee is 25% of the yearly subscription.2,8 Fix: price year one, not the subscription alone.
  • Underplanning capacity: capacity and implementation came up for 37% of offices and 28% of vendors in Simple's study.2 Fix: name an internal owner with time set aside before kickoff.
  • Personal AI accounts at work: 50% of North American family offices surveyed call AI essential, yet split 43% vs 35% on paying for enterprise-grade AI.1 Fix: approve a business plan that doesn't train on your data by default.52

Questions buyers ask before they choose

How much does family office software cost?

Published starting prices run from $250 a year for Kubera Essentials to $35,899 a year for FundCount's single family office plan.22,4 Addepar publishes no price and quotes each deal, while Asora starts at $900 a month and Aleta at $1,000.15,8,18 Most of 146 family offices surveyed spend $100,000 to $500,000 a year on all technology.39

What are the alternatives to Addepar?

Masttro, Asora, Aleta and Landytech Sesame are the reporting platforms we checked besides Addepar.11,12 Asora and Aleta publish prices from $900 and $1,000 a month, and Landytech from £20k a year plus custodial feeds.8,18,20

Can we stay on QuickBooks and spreadsheets?

Yes, while entities are few: QuickBooks Online Advanced is $340 a month for up to 25 users, but covers one company per subscription.57,3 Of 146 family offices surveyed, 42% still rely on spreadsheets.39 With many entities, partnership accounting such as FundCount fits better.4

Does the vendor's AI train on our data?

Not for most products we checked: by our count, 10 of 16 say client data never trains their models, but Canoe says it does.5,6 Five publish no statement, so ask for the training terms in the contract.15,10

How long does implementation take?

Two to four months is typical, per vendors' quotes in Simple's study, and longer with much history or many banks.2 Aleta says 4 to 8 weeks, and Masttro 12 to 14 weeks.18,11

Do we need a vendor's SOC 2 report?

Yes, ask for the System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 Type 2 report, because it tests whether controls worked over a set period.53 Addepar publishes a SOC 3 report covering 1 Sep 2025 to 31 Aug 2026.16,53

Should you buy a tool or build your own?

Buy the system of record and build only the view on top, because home-built data plumbing tends to break.9,2

Buy: Buy the system of record, because vendors warn against building your own and the pattern is 'buy the plumbing, build the view'.9,2 US enterprise AI decision-makers say 76% of their AI use cases were bought in 2025, up from 53% in 2024.54

Build: Build only the view: a dashboard or assistant on your own data, linked through the MCP connectors Asora, Aleta and Masttro offer.44,18,43 Keep it narrow: in MIT's study of 52 organizations, internal builds reached deployment about 33% of the time, against about 67% for external partnerships.55

When should you pick neither?

Pick neither when the gap is between systems you already own, when vendor data terms clash with your AI policy, or when no one has time to run a platform.7,2

  • Your systems work but don't talk to each other: wire the ledger, bill pay and reporting you have. Of single family offices, 54% keep reporting and 64% financial administration wholly in-house.7,2
  • Vendor terms clash with your AI policy: keep sensitive data in the ledger and use a business AI plan that doesn't train by default.52,2 Privacy came up for 39% of vendors and 32% of offices where AI came up, in Simple's study.2
  • No time to run a platform: outsourced accounting or a managed service fits, a topic 33% of vendors and 24% of offices raised.2
  • Few entities, simple assets: stay on QuickBooks Online Advanced at $340 a month, or Sage Intacct, which publishes no price and quotes each deal.3,56
