Generative AI vs predictive AI: which does your workflow need?

Bottom line
  • Use predictive AI if the job is a number from your own records, such as demand, cash or default risk.1,2
  • Use generative AI if the job is reading or writing: drafts, summaries, fields pulled from documents. A person checks it.1,3
  • Use both if the facts sit in documents but the decision is a score: generative extracts, predictive scores.4,5
  • Use neither if a spreadsheet forecast or a written rule already works. Make any AI beat it first.6,7
  • Either way, people decide: lenders must give specific denial reasons, and chatbots state wrong answers with confidence.8,9

What is the real difference?

Predictive AI turns your past records into a number, such as demand or default risk, while generative AI writes new content, such as drafts.10,11,9,1 The output decides it: a tested number, or words a person checks.1,12

How do predictive and generative AI differ in practice?

Predictive AI turns your own records into a tested number, while generative AI turns a prompt and documents into new text that a person checks.10,9

Generative AI here means large language models (LLMs), the engines behind ChatGPT and similar chatbots.9 SR 26-2 is the bank regulators' model risk guidance; it replaced SR 11-7 on 17 Apr 2026.13,14,15 Reg B is the US rule on credit decisions and the reasons for each denial.8

Which one fits your team?

A lending team scoring loans fits predictive AI, a planning team forecasting cash fits predictive first, and a document-heavy team fits both: generative reads, predictive scores.1,4

1. Lending or underwriting team: a credit scorecard (predictive)

A lender or insurer with 2+ years of decided files and known outcomes. Past about 40-80 labelled loans, trained models beat an LLM on this job.2,29

  1. Head of creditDefine the outcome, such as 90+ days past due within 24 months25
  2. Credit analystPull past applications and outcomes, and set aside the newest ones untouched13
  3. Credit analystFit a logistic regression and an explainable boosted model, then compare them on the set-aside loans29,24
  4. Independent reviewerChallenge the inputs, the logic and the result on the set-aside loans13
  5. Credit officerRun it in shadow on one loan product, beside today's decisions, before it decides anything
  6. UnderwriterDecide every decline and borderline case, and give the top reasons the model used8
  7. Model ownerCheck accuracy monthly and re-fit when the applicant mix shifts10,26

Cost: Open-source tools are free;36 no-code SageMaker Canvas is $1.90 per session-hour after a free tier, read 26 Nov 2026.21 Twenty build hours past the free tier is $38 (our arithmetic).

2. FP&A or planning team: a demand or cash forecast (predictive first)

A finance or planning team with 2-3 years of weekly or monthly history in a spreadsheet. Predictive methods make the number; generative AI only drafts the commentary.1,18

  1. FP&A analystBuild an Excel Forecast Sheet, which shows a 95% band and error stats6
  2. FP&A analystHide the last 6-12 months, forecast them, and record the error as the bar to beat18,7
  3. FP&A analystIf many items or drivers matter, test a boosted-tree model and keep it only if it beats the bar18
  4. FP&A leadOwn the number and log every manual override
  5. Generative AIDraft the variance commentary from the approved numbers1
  6. FP&A analystCheck every figure in the draft before it goes out9

Cost: Excel's forecast tools come with desktop Microsoft 365.34 Commentary for 20 reports a month is about $0.40 at third-party tracker prices read 26 Nov 2026 (our arithmetic).22,23

3. Document-heavy team: generative reads, predictive scores (both)

Small-business lending, commercial insurance or payables teams whose facts sit in bank statements, invoices or applications. In one study, a boosted model plus LLM-read text lifted credit-rating accuracy from 52.8% to 69.9%.4,5

  1. Ops leadList the 10-15 fields the decision needs
  2. Generative AIPull those fields from each document3
  3. Ops reviewerCheck amounts, bank numbers and low-confidence fields, where models err most3
  4. Predictive modelScore the case from the checked fields plus history4,5
  5. UnderwriterDecide, and sign off the reasons8

Cost: Extraction for 1,000 documents a month is about $11 on a mid-tier model, or $0.55 on a small one (our arithmetic).22,23 Prices are third-party, read 26 Nov 2026; your reviewers' time is the bigger cost.

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your team keeps every decision: an underwriter signs each decline, an independent reviewer challenges each model, a model owner watches accuracy, and a named person approves generated text.8,13

  • Underwriter or credit officer: makes every decline call and signs its reasons; the model only ranks. Reg B reasons must be specific and match the factors actually scored.8
  • Independent reviewer, not the builder: challenges each model's inputs, logic and results on held-back cases before use. SR 26-2 keeps this 'effective challenge' for bank models, vendor models included.13,14
  • Model owner: watches accuracy and calls the re-fit. The NAIC bulletin asks insurers to assess drift, and FICO scores lost predictive power from 2003 to 2006.10,26
  • A named person approves any generated text before a customer sees it. A tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong fare advice.28,9

What do teams get wrong when they choose?

Teams go wrong by asking a chatbot for a number a tested model should make, skipping tests on unseen data, and publishing generated text unchecked.1,18,28

  • Asking a chatbot for the forecast number: Gartner rates generative AI 'hardly useful' for forecasting.1,37 Use a forecasting method and test it on hidden past months; removing the LLM from LLM forecasters often improved accuracy.31
  • Testing once, or never: score every model on months or loans it never saw before go-live, as the M5 and M4 contests did.18,7 Then re-check monthly, because models decay as data shifts.13,26
  • Treating a chatbot's odds as a risk number: in one lending study it predicted 28-38% default against an actual 17%.2 For anything that sets price, limit or reserve, use a predictive model whose predicted rates match real ones.2,24
  • A black box where reasons are required: Reg B still demands specific reasons tied to the factors actually scored.8 The CFPB withdrew its two AI-related adverse-action circulars in May 2025, but the rule itself stands.38,39
  • Publishing generated answers unchecked: Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot's advice.28 Even purpose-built legal AI tools gave false answers 17-33% of the time, so a named person approves outbound text.19
  • Citing SR 11-7 as current: SR 26-2 replaced it on 17 Apr 2026 and leaves generative AI out of scope.13,14,15 It targets banks over $30B in assets, but it is the clearest public guide to good model practice for any lender.13,15

Questions buyers ask before they choose

Is ChatGPT generative AI or predictive AI?

Generative: it predicts the next word inside, but what it gives you is new content, not a tested forecast or score.9,11

Can generative AI do forecasting or predictive analytics?

Not well as a chatbot: Gartner rates it 'hardly useful' for forecasting, and removing the LLM from three forecasting methods did not hurt accuracy.1,31 Purpose-built time-series models are a different tool, and some do well.32

Is a linear regression AI?

Yes, for the rules: the NAIC bulletin calls it a predictive model, and SR 26-2 counts any method producing quantitative estimates as a model.10,13 On FICO credit data, a plain logistic regression matched a boosted-tree model (XGBoost) at 0.72 AUC.29

Is generative AI replacing predictive AI?

No: Gartner says nongenerative machine learning is superior for prediction, and that focusing only on generative AI is 'a guarantee to fail'.1 The best credit-rating result in one study combined both.4

Which type of AI is better for business?

Neither wins everywhere; the output decides. Numbers from your own records point to predictive AI, and words and documents point to generative AI.1,12

What is the difference between agentic, generative and predictive AI?

Predictive AI scores outcomes from past records, while generative AI writes content and agentic AI plans and acts with tools.11,40,20 One agent flow can call a predictive score and a generative model, per IBM's and SAP's explainers.40,20 Our agentic AI vs generative AI comparison covers the third kind.

Should you buy a tool or build your own?

Buy: Buy when the job is standard, such as document extraction, drafting or a forecast inside your planning tool, and you have no data team.41 In a Menlo Ventures survey of US enterprise decision-makers, 76% of AI use cases were bought in 2025, up from 53% in 2024.41 MIT NANDA found bought tools succeeded about twice as often as internal builds, though both studies cover generative AI only.42,43

Build: Build, or set up a no-code tool on your own data, when your history is the edge, such as a credit or claims score.17,21 No-code options start at $1.90 per session-hour, read 26 Nov 2026.21 Either way, validate a vendor's model as you would your own.13

When should you pick neither?

Pick neither when a spreadsheet forecast already works, the decision is a fixed policy rule, a plain scorecard is enough, or you have too little history.6,13,29,2

  • A spreadsheet forecast already works: Excel's Forecast Sheet gives a forecast, a 95% band and error stats.34,6 In the M4 contest, simple statistical combinations ranked among the most accurate methods.7
  • The decision is a fixed policy, such as declining above a set debt-to-income ratio: write the rule into your loan system. SR 26-2 doesn't count deterministic rules as models.13
  • A plain scorecard is enough: logistic regression matched a boosted-tree model on FICO credit data, and its reasons are easy to explain.29,25
  • Too little history: below about 40-80 decided loans, trained models had too little to learn from in one lending study.2 Keep deciding by policy and log clean outcomes until you have enough.29
