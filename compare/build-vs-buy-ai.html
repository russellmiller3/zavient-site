Build vs buy AI: which should your firm do for each workflow?

Bottom line
  • Buy if the job is common, like drafting or meeting notes: check the AI in tools you already pay for.21,24
  • Buy a platform and configure it if the job is common but the steps are yours, like staff policy questions.1,23
  • Build if your own data or process is the edge, no product fits, and someone owns the upkeep.6,25
  • Do neither yet if you can't name one measure, one owner and data that's ready.7,8
  • Whichever you pick, a named person signs off anything the tool tells customers or posts to the books.31,35

What is the real difference?

Buying gets a product, configuring adapts a platform to your steps, while building on a rented model gives control but leaves upkeep to you.6,23,25 Most firms now buy: 76% of enterprise AI use cases in 2025, per investor Menlo Ventures.3,4 People and process decide the result more than the model.13,12

How do buying, configuring and building compare?

Buying is quickest, at $20-31.50 a user a month. Configuring a platform takes weeks to a few months; building your own averages 8 months to production.19,18,22,1,9 Building also costs far more in upkeep than in model fees.25,26

Which one fits your team?

Buy for a common job, configure a platform when the steps are yours, and build only when your own data or process is the edge.21,1,6

1. Buy for a common job: drafting and summaries for 100 staff

Pick it when the job is the same at every firm and a plan you can buy already does it.21,24 Most enterprise AI decision-makers start here: 76% of their AI use cases were bought in 2025.3

  1. Ops leadPick one workflow and one measure, such as time to first draft8,12
  2. IT leadTurn on the business AI plan your firm already fits19,21,24
  3. Security leadCheck the no-training and data-retention terms in writing19,20,24
  4. Team leadsRun a 30-day trial and record the measure
  5. Business ownerKeep it, change it or drop it

Cost: By our math, 100 seats cost $24,000 a year on ChatGPT Business or $25,200 on the Copilot Business add-on at list.19,21 Prices read 26 Nov 2026, and Google Workspace plans already include Gemini.24

2. Configure a platform: an agent for staff policy questions

Pick it when the job is common but the steps, documents or rules are yours. MIT found successful buyers demanded tools fitted to their process.1

  1. Process ownerWrite down the steps and where the answers live7,12
  2. Data ownerFix access rights so each person sees only what they may see30
  3. IT leadBuild the agent in a platform you already license, such as Copilot Studio, and launch it to one team first23
  4. Named reviewerCheck a sample of answers weekly and own the corrections31,34
  5. Ops leadTrack the measure for 90 days, the pace MIT saw from mid-market leaders1

Cost: Copilot Studio is $200 a month per 25,000 credits, or pay-as-you-go, read 26 Nov 2026.23 Credits used vary per action, so pilot on real traffic before you commit.23

3. Build a narrow tool: supplier documents into your ERP (enterprise resource planning) system

Pick it when your own data or process is the edge and no product does the job.6,1 Get the data ready first: Gartner expects 60% of projects without AI-ready data to be dropped.7

  1. Business ownerConfirm no product fits and your data is the edge1,6
  2. Build team, in-house or outsideCall a rented model from your own tool16,17
  3. Build teamAdd tests, monitoring and a tested fallback model25,26
  4. AP (accounts payable) leadApprove anything the tool posts to the ERP above a set value35
  5. IT leadTrack the model's retirement date and re-test before the cut-off27,28

Cost: Model use for 2,000 documents a month: about $60 on a $2/$10 model, or $3 on a $0.10/$0.50 model (our arithmetic).16,17 Integration, testing and upkeep cost far more than tokens.25,26

Where does your team stay in charge?

A named person signs off anything the tool tells customers or posts to the books, and the ops lead defines those checks before launch.31,35,34 AI drafts; people decide.36

  • Ops lead or process owner: defines the human checks and signs them off before launch. NIST's AI framework asks that human oversight be defined, assessed and documented.34
  • A named approver signs off any high-impact action the tool proposes, such as an ERP posting or a refund. OWASP lists unchecked AI actions as a top risk.35
  • AI drafts, people decide: support agents with AI resolved 15% more issues an hour, and newer agents gained most.36 In a P&G study, human judgment kept its value in picking the best option.37
  • A named person owns what any chatbot tells customers. A tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong answer.31,32

How much notice do vendors give before a model or tool goes away?

OpenAI promises at least 6 months' notice for stable models, 3 months for specialized ones and as little as 2 weeks for previews.27 Anthropic retired Claude Sonnet 4.5 on 61 days' notice.28

What do teams get wrong when they choose?

Teams most often start with no business measure, build what their suite already does, or skip getting their data ready.5,21,7

  • Starting a pilot with no business measure: Gartner names unclear value as a top reason GenAI projects are dropped.5,8 Set one measure and one owner before day one.
  • Building for a common job: drafting and search are already inside suites firms pay for.21,24 Check what you license first; in MIT's interviews, bought tools reached deployment about twice as often.1,2
  • Data that isn't ready: Gartner expects 60% of projects without AI-ready data to be dropped through 2026.7 Test on your real documents before you sign or build.10
  • Forgetting the model will be retired: OpenAI gives as little as 2 weeks' notice on previews and 6 months on stable models.27 Track the dates and keep a tested fallback model.28
  • No access controls: 97% of firms with an AI breach lacked them, in an IBM-sponsored study.30 Give staff an approved tool, since workers at 90%+ of firms use personal AI anyway.1
  • Lenders citing old model rules: SR 26-2 replaced SR 11-7 on 17 Apr 2026, and it is aimed at banks over $30B in assets.33 Smaller lenders should still keep a list of every model, bought or built, and check each one.

Questions buyers ask before they choose

Do most companies buy AI tools or build their own?

Most buy: Menlo Ventures, an AI investor, found 76% of enterprise AI use cases were bought in 2025, up from 53% in 2024.3,4 Wharton found about a third of GenAI tech budgets still go to internal research and development.14

Should we build or buy AI agents?

Buy or configure first, unless your data or process is the edge.1,6 MIT found bought or partnered tools reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds. The split comes from interviews, as the report itself says.1,2

Should I learn to build it, pay someone, or buy?

For most owner-run firms, buy first and spend your time on the workflow. If you pay someone to build, budget for upkeep: Google engineers found maintaining machine learning (ML) systems is the hard, costly part.25,26

Is enterprise knowledge search worth building?

No, not from scratch: search across your files now comes in Microsoft 365 Copilot and Google Workspace plans.21,24 The hard part either way is access rights, so each person sees only what they may see.30

How long until we see value?

Weeks for a bought tool, months for a build. Gartner's survey averaged 8 months from prototype to production.9 MIT saw mid-market leaders go from pilot to full rollout in about 90 days.1

Should companies build AI, buy AI or assemble AI?

Buy or assemble most AI, and build only where your own data or process is the edge.1,6 Assembling means configuring a platform you license, like Copilot Studio, for steps that are yours.23 Gartner found embedding AI in apps you already use was the most common approach.9

Should you buy a tool or build your own?

Buy: Buy when the job is common, you have no build team, and the vendor's data terms meet your rules.6,20 Most firms do: 76% of enterprise AI use cases were bought in 2025, per Menlo Ventures, an AI investor.3 MIT found bought tools reached deployment about twice as often, from interviews rather than market data.1,2

Build: Build when your own data or process is the edge and you need control of the rules.6,1 Gartner's July 2024 figures, as CACM reports them, put training your own model at $8M-20M upfront.6 A firm your size builds a narrow tool on a rented model instead, and budgets for upkeep and model retirements.16,27

When should you pick neither?

Pick neither when your suite already does it, the process is broken, the job is a score or forecast from your records, or no one can check the output.21,10,8,5

  • The AI in your current suite already does the job: Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace plans include it.21,24 Turn it on for one team before you buy anything new.
  • The process itself is broken: fix the steps or the data first. RAND found misunderstood problems and missing data among the root causes of failed AI projects.10
  • The job is a score or forecast from your own records: use a predictive model, not a chatbot. Gartner advises defining where generative AI should not be used.8
  • No one can check the output and an error would be costly: keep the decision with people for now. Most failed projects lacked risk controls or clear value, per Gartner.5
