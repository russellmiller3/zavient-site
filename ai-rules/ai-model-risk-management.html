What replaced SR 11-7, and what does it change?

SR 26-2 replaced SR 11-7 on 17 Apr 2026 as the bank regulators' guidance on model risk.1,4,5 It keeps validation, monitoring and vendor checks, scaled to how much a model matters, and covers machine-learning models but not generative AI.2 Law firms read it as more principles-based.26,27

Does SR 26-2 apply to your bank, credit union or insurer?

Only if you're a bank over $30 billion in assets, or a smaller bank with significant model risk, because the agencies drew that line.1,5

IfThen
Your banking organization has over $30 billion in assetsYes: the Federal Reserve calls it "most relevant" above $30 billion.1 Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) banks of that size are in scope too.5
You're an FDIC-supervised bank with $30 billion or less in assetsMostly no. The FDIC says it "generally does not apply" unless you have "significant exposure to model risk".5
You're a community bank supervised by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)Mostly no: the bulletin covers community banks "subject to the limitations", and the OCC says their models are "generally excluded".4,6 It won't require them to validate every year either.7
You're a credit unionNo. The National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) has no rules specific to super intelligence (SI), its new word for AI, and thin model guidance.10,11
You're an insurerNo. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) AI Model Bulletin applies instead, in 30 jurisdictions, or your state's own rule.12
Your bank has a state charterYour state examiners now have the Conference of State Bank Supervisors (CSBS) AI framework, published 16 Sep 2026, alongside the federal guidance.14

What did SR 26-2 change, clause by clause?

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every call that matters: validators judge models, owners approve use, reviewers can halt AI, and vendor model risk stays with the bank.2,17

  • Validators judge whether a model works: SR 26-2 rates validation on "the rigor and effectiveness of the review rather than on organizational structure".2
  • Model owners decide when a model goes live: validation "generally occurs prior to a model's first use", with an exception for "an urgent business need".2
  • Reviewers can stop an AI system: OCC examiners expect authority to "reject, modify, pause, or halt" it.17 State examiners ask for human checkpoints on AI agents.15
  • AI drafts, people sign: the OCC's moderate-risk example is summarizing documents for credit memos, with a mandatory human in the loop.17 Vendor model risk stays with the bank.17,20

What is on your checklist?

Your checklist is an inventory, risk tiers, validation timed by tier, vendor evidence, outcome monitoring, an audit check and an exam folder.2,15

  1. Start one list of every model and AI tool, each with a named owner: use case, provider, permissions, status, monitoring and fail-over.17 SR 26-2 calls such a list "common industry practice".2
  2. Tier each model by exposure and purpose. For immaterial ones, the work "may consist of identifying those models and monitoring model performance".2
  3. Set validation timing by tier, not by calendar: SR 26-2 dropped SR 11-7's "at least annually" review.2,3
  4. For each vendor model, gather enough to understand its "conceptual soundness, design, development data, and performance": whether its logic, data and results hold up.2 Then monitor its outcomes and log your changes to it.2
  5. Put liability for model errors, and the vendor's use of your data for training, in the contract.17,15
  6. Check outcomes against predictions on a schedule: outcomes analysis and ongoing monitoring remain two of the three parts of validation.2
  7. Keep generative AI on the list even though SR 26-2 skips it: state examiners ask for generative AI policies and an approved-tools list.15
  8. Have internal audit judge whether the program is "rigorous and effective", not redo validations; SR 26-2 says audit "would generally not duplicate" that work.2
  9. Keep one folder for exams: policies, board reporting, inventory, tiers, vendors with embedded AI, validation and monitoring results, output samples and audit reports.15
  10. Credit unions: NCUA examiners look at internal controls, ongoing monitoring and vendor due diligence.10 Insurers: regulators may ask for inventories, drift records and vendor contracts.13

Where do firms go wrong with the new guidance?

Firms go wrong by over-building, by treating non-binding as optional, and by trusting a vendor's word on bias.7,22

  • Over-building: the OCC found small banks setting up in-house modeling teams "without regard to whether it is reasonable".7 A community bank with few, moderately complex models might do significantly less.7
  • Reading "non-binding" as "nothing to do": SR 26-2 still leaves room for action against unsafe or unsound practices.2 Keep the inventory and monitoring running.
  • Untested AI underwriting: Massachusetts reached a $2.5 million settlement with a student lender whose AI models allegedly lacked disparate impact testing.24 Test for bias before launch and after.
  • Unwatched algorithms: the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) imposed a $2.7 million penalty after Hello Digit's savings algorithm "routinely caused consumers' checking accounts to overdraft".25 Watch outcomes, not just inputs.
  • Taking a vendor's word: New York says an insurer "may not rely solely on a third-party's claim of non-discrimination".22 Ask the vendor for its test results.
  • Citing a retired document: SR 11-7 and the FDIC's 2017 adoption of it were both replaced on 17 Apr 2026.1,5 Point your policies at SR 26-2.

Questions risk officers ask about SR 26-2

Is SR 11-7 still in effect?

No, because SR 26-2 superseded it on 17 Apr 2026.1 The OCC rescinded its twin, Bulletin 2011-12, the same day.4

Does SR 26-2 still require annual model validation?

No, because SR 26-2 ties validation timing to model purpose and drops SR 11-7's "at least annually" review.2,3 The OCC also says it won't require community banks to validate every year.7

Does SR 26-2 cover generative AI?

No, because footnote 3 puts generative and agentic AI out of scope.2 The agencies plan a request for information on AI, including generative and agentic AI.4

Does SR 26-2 apply to credit unions?

No, because the NCUA did not issue it and has no AI-specific rules.10,1 GAO found NCUA's model risk guidance "limited in scope and detail".11

What if we only use vendor models?

You still own the risk: sound practice is understanding the vendor model's "conceptual soundness, design, development data, and performance", then monitoring it.2 Using a vendor "does not diminish banks' responsibility".20

Is a spreadsheet a model under SR 26-2?

Not usually, because SR 26-2 defines a model as a "complex quantitative method" and leaves out simple spreadsheets.2

Should you buy model risk software or do the work in-house?

Buy software for the records once you have many models, and keep the judgment in-house: who validates, which tier, when to pause.29,2

Buy: Buy model risk software once your inventory outgrows a spreadsheet. ValidMind lists a model tier at $5,000 for 12 months on AWS Marketplace (vendor's listing, read 28 Nov 2026).29 Yields publishes no price and quotes each deal.30

Build: Keep the judgment in-house: who validates, which tier, when to pause a model.2 The OCC warns against in-house modeling teams set up "without regard to whether it is reasonable".7 A surveyed community banker said building your own AI tools would be "very difficult".23

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your AI is generative or agentic, when you're an insurer, or when the real issue is the vendor.2,13

  • If your AI is a chat assistant or an agent, SR 26-2 doesn't reach it.2 Use the OCC's SI framework or the CSBS guide to set up AI governance instead.17,15
  • If you're an insurer, work from the NAIC bulletin and your state's rule; California, Colorado and New York have their own.12,13
  • If the worry is the vendor more than the math, start from the interagency third-party guidance, which has a replacement proposed.18,20
