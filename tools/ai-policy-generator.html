What does the AI policy generator do?

The AI policy generator turns 10 answers into a draft policy: which AI tools staff may use, what data goes in, who checks output.6,7 It adds clauses for your industry, data and locations. It's a starting draft for your adviser, not legal advice.

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your COO owns the policy, a qualified person reviews AI output before it goes out, and people make every decision about someone's job, credit or care.7,6,14 Your legal adviser reviews the draft before you adopt it.10

  • Your COO or head of operations owns the policy and names who approves new AI tools.7,19
  • A qualified person reviews AI output before it reaches customers, regulators or the public.6,1
  • People make every decision about someone's job, credit, insurance or care; AI only prepares the information.14,18
  • Your legal or compliance adviser reviews the draft before you adopt it, because state and EU rules keep changing.10,9

Questions leaders ask

Is there a free AI policy template we can use?

Yes: this generator is free, needs no email, and builds a full AI use policy from your answers. You edit it instead of adapting a fixed template.6,15

Should we ban ChatGPT at work?

No, usually not: in one global study, policy-breaking AI use was most common where AI was banned.4,5 Approved tools plus clear rules work better.

What should an AI acceptable use policy include?

Scope, an owner, approved tools, a rule for each kind of data, human review, approval for new tools, training, incident reporting and a yearly review.7,16

Who should own the AI policy at a mid-size company?

One named senior owner, often the COO, with input from IT, HR and legal. Larger firms often use a small working group instead.7,19

Do we need an AI policy if we have EU staff or customers?

Not by name, but the EU AI Act asks deployers to take steps to build staff AI literacy, supervised nationally from 2 August 2026. A written policy with a training record helps show you did.8,9

Do we need separate rules for AI in hiring?

Yes, because hiring AI carries extra duties. New York City requires a bias audit within a year of use, and federal anti-discrimination law still applies to AI-assisted decisions.11,14

How often should we update our AI policy?

At least every 12 months, and sooner when a law, a major tool or an incident changes things. Colorado rewrote its AI law in 2026, for example.7,10

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else, or add to this policy, if you are a broker-dealer or insurer, use AI in clinical decisions, or want a certified ISO 42001 system.20,21,22,19

  • You are a broker-dealer using AI in client communications → start from FINRA's notice and your supervisory procedures, then use this draft for staff use.20
  • You are an insurer → also write the AI program in the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) model bulletin. This policy covers how staff use AI.21
  • You use AI in clinical decisions → also review each tool's inputs under Section 1557; the generator adds the clause, not the review.22
  • You want a certified AI management system → use ISO 42001; an AI policy is one part of it.19
