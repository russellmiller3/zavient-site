AI training for employees: how many hours, and who goes first?

Bottom line
  • Plan 5 to 10 hours per person: BCG found 82% regular use there, against 63% below 5 hours.1,5
  • Train managers and champions first; only 8% of HR leaders say managers have the AI skills needed.6,17
  • Give every workflow a named person who checks; AI made consultants worse on tasks beyond its reach.11,24
  • In the EU, keep an internal training record; no certificate or knowledge test is required.13,14

What does the Team AI training plan do?

The Team AI training plan turns your team sizes into a free 90-day AI training plan. It shows who trains first, hours per person, cost of staff time and champions.1,5 Training works when it is hands-on, on real work, with a person checking the output.9,11

Where does your team stay in charge?

Managers pick the tasks and review AI-assisted work, and a named person checks each workflow's output before it is used or sent.6,24 Hiring, promotion and performance decisions stay with people.15

  • Managers pick the tasks, review AI-assisted work and name where saved hours go.6,4
  • Every workflow names the person who checks the output before it is used or sent.24,11
  • Hiring, promotion and performance decisions stay with people. The EU AI Act lists AI for them as high-risk.15
  • Champions coach and flag risky use. They are not the help desk.16,17

Questions leaders ask

How many hours of AI training does each employee need?

Plan 5 to 10 hours in the first 90 days; this tool defaults to 7.5 for staff and 10.5 for managers.1 Forrester's Microsoft studies budget 8 to 10 hours of formal training per user.5,26

Does the EU AI Act require us to train staff?

Yes, if you use AI: Article 4 asks you to take measures to support staff AI literacy, with no required level since July 2026.14,27 No certificate is needed, and an internal training record is enough.13 This plan supports your Article 4 record; it is not legal advice.

Why train managers before staff?

Managers decide which tasks use AI and check the output, yet only 8% of HR leaders think they have the skills.6 With strong leadership support, positive views of AI rose from 15% to 55%.2,3

How many AI champions do we need?

At least one per team, and one per 40 people, or per 25 with regulated data.16,22 Published ratios run from 1 per 25 to 50 in regulated firms to 1 per 50 to 100.18,25 Our ratio is a Zavient estimate you can change.

What happens to the hours people save?

Managers name where they go: more client time, faster turnaround or backlog cleared.28 66% of frontline AI users get little or no guidance on this, so the plan makes it a month-3 task.4

Should we test employees on AI?

No, skip a knowledge test; the EU AI Act sets no duty to measure staff knowledge.13 Track weekly use, workflows in use and time saved per task instead.2,8

What should AI training cover beyond prompting?

Checking the output, because on a task beyond AI's reach, consultants using it were 19 percentage points less likely to be right.11,23 Employers now list reviewing AI output before using it as a core skill.24

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else in three cases: under 40 people with no managers, low use past 10 hours each, or plans to use AI in hiring or reviews.10,2,15

  • Under 40 people with no manager layer? Run the basics as one group session and name one champion.10
  • Past 10 hours per person and use is still low? The gap is likely workflows and manager support, not more courses.2,28
  • Want AI in hiring or performance reviews? Get legal advice first, because the EU AI Act lists those uses as high-risk.15
