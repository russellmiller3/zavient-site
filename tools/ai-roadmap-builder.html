AI implementation roadmap: what should we do first, and when?

Bottom line
  • Start with two projects, not twenty: AI leaders pursue about half as many opportunities and scale more8,9.
  • Buy or set up a tool first; bought tools reached daily use about twice as often as internal builds1,2.
  • Write a one-page policy and approve one tool by day 15, since most staff lack AI guidance11,1.
  • Take a baseline before the pilot; tracking clear measures had the biggest bottom-line effect of any adoption practice6,7.
  • Judge year one on use, time and errors, because payback on a typical AI use case often takes years12,3.

What does the AI roadmap builder do?

The AI roadmap builder turns your answers into a dated plan: who owns it, which projects come first, and when to stop. AI drafts, sorts and suggests, while people check and decide7,16. What makes or breaks it is a baseline and a written review step before the pilot6,15.

Where does your team stay in charge?

The COO or owner-CEO sponsors the plan and makes each scale, fix or stop call, while each team's head owns its project7,9,1. People approve, send and decide; AI only drafts, sorts and suggests16.

  • The COO or owner-CEO sponsors the plan and makes each scale, fix or stop call. Senior ownership of AI work tracks with results7,9.
  • The head of each team owns its project, such as the head of customer service, the controller or the claims manager1,6.
  • People send, approve, sign and decide, while AI drafts, sorts and suggests. Firms using AI raised output per worker with no drop in jobs16,17.
  • Decisions about people's credit, insurance, health or jobs keep a person on every call, with a log and a second check7,15.

A 150-person insurance firm gets a one-page policy and an approved tool by day 15, two pilots by day 46, and a scale-or-stop check at day 90. Claim letters follow in months 4-6, with a person approving every letter.

Questions leaders ask

What should an AI roadmap include?

Two or three named projects, an owner for each, what comes first, the review step people keep, and dated checkpoints6,7. High performers are more likely to have a clearly defined AI road map7.

How long until we see results from AI?

Top mid-market firms went from pilot to full use in about 90 days; across firms it averages 8 months from prototype to production1,3. Payback takes longer, often 2 to 4 years in a survey of European and Middle East firms12. Productivity often dips before it rises13,14.

Who should own the AI plan at a company our size?

One executive sponsor, usually the COO or owner-CEO, plus the head of each team whose work changes9,1. Senior leaders who own AI work are far more common at high performers7.

How many AI projects should we start with?

Two in the first 90 days, and no more than three in year one. AI leaders pursue about half as many opportunities and align on three to four priorities8,9.

Do we need an AI policy and training before rollout?

Yes, and both fit in the first month. Only 40% of employees say their workplace has AI guidance, and 47% have had training11. At least 5 hours of training sharply raises regular use10.

Do we need clean data before we start with AI?

No, but data decides which projects go first. Start with bought tools on documents people already handle, and start one cleanup task early4,18.

Should we buy or build our first AI project?

Buy or set up first: bought or partner-built tools reached daily use about twice as often as internal builds, in a small self-reported sample1,2.

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you don't yet know which work to start with, already have one project to price, or keep your data in paper files and inboxes1,3,4. Start with the AI use case finder, the AI project cost estimator or the AI readiness check instead.

  • You don't know which work to start with yet: run the AI use case finder first, then come back1.
  • You have one project in mind and need its price: use the AI project cost estimator, then the AI ROI (return on investment) calculator3.
  • Your data sits in paper files and inboxes: take the AI readiness check first, since data gaps stop many projects4,18.
