How it works

  • Score = peer interest × strength of proof × size of gain × ease × risk, scaled to 100. The weights are a Zavient estimate, shown in full below.
  • Proof: a randomized trial counts 1.0, a field study 0.9, a survey 0.75, a vendor claim 0.55 and no published measure 0.4. Trial gains often shrink in day-to-day work.4,5
  • Ease: buy 1.0, set up 0.8, build 0.55. Bought tools reached daily use about twice as often as internal builds.6,7
  • Peer interest: US job ads plus a tenth of monthly searches for the work, from our 25 Nov 2026 count. The count is rough and includes AI vendors hiring.
  • Size: under 100 people, builds count 0.8; at 250 or more, set-up and builds count 1.05. AI use among US employer businesses rises with firm size, from 33.2% at 50-99 staff to 44.1% at 250 or more, in October 2026.8,9
  • Risk: low 1.0, medium 0.8, high 0.6. A use regulators name as high risk is never ranked first.10,12

What does the AI use case finder do?

An AI use case finder ranks where AI can help a team first. AI drafts, reads and sorts, while people check, decide and sign off.1,2 What makes or breaks it is picking frequent, well-defined work, then measuring one number for 30 days.3,7

Where does your team stay in charge?

The team lead owns every AI output that leaves the team: AI drafts, and a person checks and sends it.2,14 Decisions on jobs, credit or insurance stay with people.10,13

  • The team lead, such as the controller, claims manager or ops lead, owns each AI output that leaves the team. AI drafts; a person checks and sends.2,14
  • Decisions about people's jobs, credit or insurance stay with people. Regulators name these uses as high risk and expect human oversight.10,13
  • Newer staff gain the most from AI help, so experienced people move to coaching, exceptions and judgment calls.1,5
  • The COO or division head signs off the pilot and the one number it must move. Many AI projects stop after the proof of concept for lack of that proof.7,6

Questions leaders ask

Where should a company our size start with AI?

Start with frequent, well-defined work that a person checks, like drafting, document answers or support replies.1,2 Buy or set up a tool before you build. Bought tools reached daily use about twice as often as internal builds.6

How much time does AI really save?

On single tasks, trials show 15% to 40%: support agents resolved 15% more issues per hour, and writing took 40% less time.1,2 Across all US work hours, the saving was about 1.6% in 2025, so plan task by task.4

What happens to the time AI saves our people?

Most saved time goes to other work. In a large Danish study, 85% of users moved saved time to other tasks, and hours and earnings held steady.5 Newer staff gained the most from AI help.1

Should we buy, set up or build an AI tool?

At 40 to 500 staff, buy or set up a tool in most cases. Internal builds reached daily use about half as often as bought tools.6 At least 30% of gen AI projects were expected to be dropped after proof of concept.7

Which AI uses carry legal risk?

Screening job applicants, scoring credit and pricing life or health insurance are high risk under the EU AI Act, with duties from 2 Dec 2027.10,11 New York City requires a yearly bias audit for hiring tools.12 About 30 US states expect insurers to keep a written AI program.13

What data do we need to start?

Usually examples of work you already do: past invoices, tickets, contracts or estimates. Each card in the finder lists the data that use needs. Poor data quality is a top reason gen AI projects stop after a pilot.7

How does the finder decide which use comes first?

Each use gets a score from peer interest, strength of proof, size of gain, ease and risk. Proof from a randomized trial counts most, because trial gains often shrink in daily work.4,5 A use regulators name as high risk is never ranked first.10

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else if you already know your use, your data sits in paper files or many spreadsheets, or the work decides on jobs, credit or insurance.6,7,10

  • You know the use you want: price it with the AI project cost estimator, then check payback with the AI ROI (return on investment) calculator.6
  • Your data sits in paper files or many spreadsheets: take the AI readiness check first, since poor data stops many pilots.7
  • The work decides on people's jobs, credit or insurance: get legal review before any tool, since regulators name these uses as high risk.10,12
