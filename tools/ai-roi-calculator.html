How it works

  • Minutes moved per AI item = minutes today, minus review minutes, minus redo rate times minutes today. Review time is the step most calculators skip21,22.
  • Money per month counts 56% of freed hours: Gartner's example takes a 20% productivity leak, then a 70% harvesting ratio1.
  • Value ramps up over 3 months while run cost starts in month 1, to show the early dip15,16,25.
  • Hourly cost is BLS pay times 1.28 to 1.31, the BLS loading for benefits other than paid leave, because pay already includes leave5,6. Where no single job fits, we use BLS total pay and benefits per hour6.
  • Minutes per item come from MetricNet, APQC and Loopio benchmarks7,9,11. Volumes and costs start where a 40 to 500 person firm usually starts, from APQC, IOFM, Loopio and list prices9,26,28,30,32.
  • Adoption starts at 80%, the active-use rate in the UK government's Copilot trial12.
  • Low and high cases change AI share, review minutes and adoption; payback is not discounted. Above 40% time saved, a reality check appears, since surveyed AI users saved 5.4% of work hours13.

What does the AI ROI calculator do?

An AI ROI calculator asks whether one workflow earns back its setup and running cost. AI drafts the first pass, and people still check each draft2,12. The answer turns on volume, review minutes and how much freed time becomes real output1,19.

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your team stays in charge of reviewing each AI draft, handling exceptions, deciding where freed hours go, and the go or no-go call on spend12,17,23.

  • A named reviewer checks each AI draft before it goes out, such as the AP clerk, support agent or accountant. The calculator counts that time as a cost12,19.
  • Exceptions stay with people. Teams where AI took routine items and people reviewed exceptions reported 71% median gains, against 30% where people approved every output17.
  • People decide where freed hours go: faster turnaround, fewer errors, or work that waits today. Early Copilot users spent saved time mostly on focus work23.
  • The ops lead or division head owns the go or no-go call and signs off the spend. Test on timed items first, because many AI projects stop after the proof of concept24,14.

How many items a month does each workflow need to pay back in a year?

On the default path, workflows need 110 to 2,200 items a month to pay back in a year, from 110 meetings to 2,200 support tickets. A custom build at $80k needs far more, from 830 meetings to 9,700 invoices a month.

Questions leaders ask

Why is my result lower than vendor ROI calculators show?

We subtract the minutes people spend checking AI drafts, the drafts that get redone, and the slow first months21,15. Some vendor calculators skip these, and one defaults to a 76% resolution rate22. We also count only part of the freed time as money1.

Why does it show hours moved to better work, not jobs saved?

Measured gains are on tasks, not whole roles: 14% more issues resolved per hour in support, and 40% less time on writing tasks2,3. The hours are real capacity. Your team decides where they go23.

Where do the default numbers come from?

Minutes per item come from APQC, MetricNet and Loopio benchmarks, and volumes and costs from what a 40 to 500 person firm typically has9,7,11,26,30. Hourly cost is BLS pay plus benefits other than paid leave, which pay already covers5,6. AI share and review minutes are our estimates, because no neutral benchmark exists.

Why does the first month lose money?

You pay to run it from day one while people learn the new step16. Research on new technology finds a dip before the gain, and DORA's calculator models one too15,25.

How accurate is an AI ROI estimate?

Only roughly: an AI ROI estimate is for planning, so the tool shows low, expected and high cases. Self-reported savings run high: developers who felt 20% faster were 19% slower when measured14. Time 20 real items before you decide12.

What costs should I include?

Include one-time setup and monthly running costs: licences or usage fees, upkeep, and the staff time spent checking AI output.36,21 Setup covers configuration, connecting your systems, data clean-up and training, about $14,000 in one Microsoft-funded small-firm study.33 AI seats run $21 to $50 a user a month, and most AI projects reviewed on Clutch cost $10,000 to $49,999.36,30,20

What if it says the workflow doesn't pay back?

A no-payback result is still useful: the tool names the most likely lever, such as a cheaper set-up path, more volume, or less review time17. In MIT's study, bought tools succeeded about 67% of the time and internal builds one-third as often18. Bring the numbers to the free 30-minute diagnostic and we will find which lever fits.

Why are IT tickets, reconciliations and document intake not in the list?

At a firm of 40 to 500 staff they move too few hours to pay for a priced tool. A 150-person firm logs about 200 IT tickets a month, at 1.3 per person34,8, and has about four bank accounts, at 4 hours each35,10. If yours are bigger, use the blank workflow with your own counts.

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you run software delivery, draft at low volume, have few IT tickets or reconciliations, face thin-data judgment calls, or haven't priced the build yet25,12,34,4,20.

  • Software delivery teams: DORA's free calculator models engineering metrics and the early dip in more detail25.
  • Low-volume drafting and meeting notes: turn on AI in tools you already pay for before you price a build12,23.
  • IT tickets and bank reconciliations at a firm this size: turn on what your software already includes. Too few hours move to justify a project34,35.
  • Judgment calls with thin data: keep them by hand for now. On tasks outside what AI does well, users were 19 points less likely to be right4.
  • Build cost unknown: price the project first with our AI project cost estimator, then bring the numbers back here20.
