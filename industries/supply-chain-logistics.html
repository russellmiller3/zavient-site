What does AI actually do in supply chain and logistics?

AI in supply chain and logistics drafts routine work: forecasts, order entry, check calls, freight-bill matching and route plans.6,31 McKinsey reports distributors cutting inventory 20-30% and logistics costs 5-20%: consultancy ranges, not promises.31 Success hinges on clean master data (core product and supplier records) and a named owner per decision.4,30

Where does your team stay in charge?

  • Demand planners sign off the plan. AI drafts the baseline; planners flag promotions, new customers and lost accounts, where people add the most.12,14
  • Buyers award the business, and trade body ASCM keeps sourcing under human oversight while AI drafts orders and spend reports.11 A person verifies any change to a supplier's bank details by calling a number on file, as the FBI advises.44
  • Dispatchers and carrier reps keep final routes, carrier vetting and rate calls. Fake-carrier pickups are about a quarter of cargo thefts, so a person verifies every carrier.26,25
  • Warehouse managers set quotas and own safety. Several states treat an algorithm-set pick rate as a quota that must be disclosed in writing.15,16

Where does AI pay first in supply chain and logistics?

  1. Keying emailed and PDF orders into your ERP (enterprise resource planning system)

    Benchmarking body APQC puts the median cost to process one purchase order at $55, across 4,620 firms.5 APQC also finds ordering is the costliest procurement process and takes the most staff time.45

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per order, keying errors and orders handled per rep.

  2. Demand forecasting and reorder suggestions

    Machine-learning forecasts beat the best statistical method by over 14% in the M5 contest.8 McKinsey reports 20-50% fewer forecast errors, if your sales history is clean.9,10

    Payoff to measure: Forecast error by SKU (stock-keeping unit), fill rate and inventory turns.

  3. Check calls, tracking updates and quotes (for 3PLs, or third-party logistics firms, and brokers)

    At one freight firm, 50 AI agents automate 60% of check calls (calls asking carriers where a load is).6 Transport planning leads AI use among logistics providers, at 64%.1

    Payoff to measure: Check calls per rep per day, quote turnaround and loads per rep.

  4. Freight invoice audit and AP (accounts payable) matching

    The same firm's agents handle 80% of its paper invoice payments.6 MIT found the biggest generative AI return in back-office work like this.7

    Payoff to measure: Invoices matched with no touch, overcharges caught and days to pay.

  5. Procurement: spend analysis, contract review, supplier screening

    Hackett finds 43% of procurement teams pursuing AI but only 12% at scale, in a mostly large-firm sample.32 Most (69%) use AI already built into their procurement software.32

    Payoff to measure: Spend under contract, days per sourcing event and contract review hours.

  6. Route and load planning (if you run your own fleet)

    UPS's ORION routing saves about 100 million miles a year, but it's a custom build at UPS scale.41 Every route must fit federal hours-of-service limits.20

    Payoff to measure: Miles per stop, on-time delivery and driver hours used.

  7. Warehouse slotting and labor planning

    APQC finds 40% of firms have no plan for AI in warehouse automation.34 It needs a WMS (warehouse management system) and clean data first, and quota laws apply.4,15

    Payoff to measure: Travel per pick, picks per labor hour and injury rate.

Where does supply chain AI go wrong?

  • Lab wins can fail on the floor. Starbucks dropped an AI shelf-counting tool after about nine months when reflections doubled milk counts; pilot on one real site first.28
  • Bad master data sinks the forecast. Nike's 2000-01 planning rollout overbought about $90M of product, so clean your SKU data and launch in phases.29,4
  • Some 'AI agents' aren't. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by 2027; ask vendors to prove claims, which the FTC also polices.27,24
  • Email-reading agents are a fraud target. Cyber-enabled cargo theft cost about $725M in 2025, so any agent that books carriers or changes payment details needs a person's check.25,26
  • Planner corrections can hurt as well as help. Small, upward tweaks make forecasts worse, so track whether each touch improved accuracy.12,13
  • Pilots stall without an owner. Name who owns the data and the decision before you start; that's where most predictive pilots break.30,11

Questions supply chain leaders ask about AI

Is AI actually useful for demand forecasting, or is it mostly hype?

Useful, if you have clean sales history. Machine learning beat statistical methods by over 14% in the M5 contest.8,9 A 2026 study found data access mattered more than the algorithm.10

Are AI purchase order recommendations useful yet for a midsize distributor?

Often, but benchmark them against a plain reorder point first. Mid-size planning tools start around $900 a month.36 Most procurement teams use AI already built into their software.32

What exactly has AI improved for 3PL providers?

Transport planning and the paperwork around it. It leads providers' AI use at 64%, and one firm's agents automate 60% of check calls.1,6 Only 13% of providers report measurable value so far.1

Is AI for supply chain only for large companies?

No, but upfront cost weighs more on small firms: 44% cite it, against 25% of large firms.1 MIT and Mecalux find a typical payback of 2-3 years.4

How is AI changing logistics and supply chain jobs?

It adds skills to jobs more than it removes them. About half of logistics providers expect reskilling, and logistician jobs are projected to grow 18% by 2035.1,35 In our job-ad count above, demand planner ads mention AI most often.

Management wants AI in our operations. Where do we start?

Start with one paperwork workflow you can measure, such as order entry or freight-bill matching. APQC's $55 median cost per purchase order gives you a baseline.5 Bought tools succeed about twice as often as internal builds.7

Should you buy supply chain AI or build it?

Buy: Buy for forecasting, routing, visibility and freight audit, where tools are mature. About 76% of AI uses are now bought, up from 53% in 2024.33 Bought or partnered tools succeed about twice as often as internal builds.7

Build: Build only the glue: email-to-ERP order intake, data clean-up and approval steps. Hackett's leaders first check where AI pays, then pick built-in features, AI-native tools or custom builds.32 Turn on what your ERP or WMS already has before you buy more.

When should you pick something else?

  • Lumpy, slow-moving demand such as spare parts: try a simple method like Croston's first. Croston-type methods beat a seasonal baseline on low-volume items in M5.8
  • One depot or a regional fleet: buy off-the-shelf routing software. A custom optimizer like UPS's ORION pays only at UPS scale.41
