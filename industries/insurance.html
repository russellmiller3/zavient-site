What is AI in insurance, and what does it change?

AI in insurance mostly reads and drafts: it pulls facts from submissions, claim files and policy forms so underwriters and adjusters decide faster.3,1,11 People keep the decision: state regulators apply the same standards whatever tool you use.12,8 Clean data and a real human check make or break it.13,9

Where does your team stay in charge?

  • Underwriters keep accept, decline and price. New York expects each adverse decision to state the information used and its source.14,15
  • Adjusters keep every claim denial. In the regulators' auto survey, 9 of 193 insurers used AI for claim approvals and none for denials.2
  • MGA underwriters keep the bind decision within their authority. Carriers' AI programs should cover AI from outside firms, so expect their questions.8
  • At large agencies, a licensed person checks anything a client sees. Verify limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements against the policy forms.16
  • Senior management and the board own the AI program, with a committee from underwriting, claims, compliance and legal.12,8 The bulletin lists checking AI against expert review as one way to validate it.8

Where does AI pay first in insurance?

  1. Submission intake and triage (carriers and MGAs)

    Underwriters spend up to 40% of their time on admin and other non-core work.3 Only 9% of underwriting leaders use AI to read and prepare submission data to a large extent; 68% expect to in three years.17

    Payoff to measure: Hours from submission received to quote, and the share of submissions quoted.

  2. Claim file summaries for adjusters (carriers)

    Claims is the top AI area for auto insurers, and the main use is informing adjusters.2,18 In a McKinsey case study, Aviva runs 80+ claims models and assesses complex liability 23 days faster.4

    Payoff to measure: Adjuster minutes per file review, and days to final payment (40.7 on average for US home claims in 2026).19

  3. AI inventory and examiner file (carriers)

    Examiners can ask for your written AI program, model inventory, validation work and vendor contracts.8 Only 24% of insurance leaders are very confident they could pass an independent AI governance review within 90 days.20

    Payoff to measure: Days to produce the examiner file, and the share of AI uses inventoried.

  4. Bordereaux and carrier reporting (MGAs)

    AM Best says consistent, transparent portfolio data is becoming a condition for MGAs' access to capital.5 It also reports added scrutiny of MGAs as the market matures.21

    Payoff to measure: Days to close each bordereau (the premium and claims report to a carrier), and carrier data queries per month.

  5. Fraud screening (carriers)

    Fraud costs US consumers $308.6 billion a year, a coalition figure the NAIC cites.22 Among insurers the regulators surveyed, 58% in auto and 55% in home use or plan AI for it.18

    Payoff to measure: Hit rate of referrals to your special investigations unit, checked by customer group.

  6. Policy checking and coverage comparison (large agencies)

    Coverage form analysis is agencies' second most common AI use, at 43%.23 A qualified person still verifies limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements against the policy.16

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per policy check, and errors caught before the client sees the policy.

Where does AI in insurance go wrong?

  • Rubber-stamp review: a lawsuit alleges Cigna doctors denied over 300,000 payment requests at about 1.2 seconds each, and a court let it proceed.9,36 Nothing is proven yet; still, log review time, sample decisions and show reviewers the evidence, not only the score.
  • Unequal outcomes: Black homeowners allege State Farm's claim-screening algorithms slowed and questioned their claims more; the claim survived dismissal in 2023.37 Test outcomes by group before and after go-live; Colorado and New York expect it for covered lines.30,14
  • Vendor AI treated as someone else's problem. The NAIC bulletin and New York keep you responsible for vendor models, so put audit rights and regulator cooperation in the contract.8,14
  • Controls you can't prove: 68% of insurance leaders say their AI controls exist, but the evidence is scattered across teams and tools.20 Keep one inventory entry and one evidence file per AI use, because examiners can ask for both.8
  • Trusting a vendor's accuracy figure: score it on your own files against what your experts decided, a check the bulletin names.8 Data quality is insurers' second biggest AI barrier, named by 45%.13
  • Pilots that never reach the desk: only 7% of carriers in a 2026 claims study report scalable AI success.38 Pilot one workflow on one team with one owner and one measure; few task-specific AI tools reach production.39

Questions buyers ask about AI in insurance

How is AI used in insurance?

Mostly to read and draft for the people who decide.1,11 In regulators' surveys, claims and fraud lead in auto and home, and underwriting leads in life.18 Conning names AI and data-driven underwriting among the drivers of MGA growth.6

Where should a carrier or MGA start with AI?

With one reading-heavy workflow, such as submission intake or complex claim files.3,17 Pick one with a clear before-and-after number. Add it to your AI inventory from day one, since examiners can ask for it.8

What will the examiner ask about our AI?

Expect requests for your written AI program, your inventory of models and AI systems, and how you validate and monitor them.8 For vendor AI, expect requests for your due diligence, contracts and audits.8,12

Does the NAIC AI bulletin apply to MGAs?

It is addressed to insurers.12 It asks insurers to cover AI they rely on from outside firms, so carrier partners may ask an MGA about its AI.8 AM Best describes added scrutiny of MGAs as the market matures.21

Will AI replace insurance underwriters or adjusters?

Nothing in the data points that way. 75% of claims professionals say AI needs human oversight.38 Regulators hold insurers to the same standards whatever tool helped make a decision.12,8

Which AI rules apply to insurers in my state?

Thirty jurisdictions have issued the NAIC AI bulletin, per its 13 Nov 2026 map.7 California, Colorado and New York have their own guidance or rules.7 The NAIC says its map doesn't confirm each state kept every element, so read your state's own text.7

What does an AI platform for a carrier or MGA cost?

The platforms in our table publish no list price.25 Hi Marley, for example, sells by private offer priced per user.26 Budget from a scoped quote for one workflow.

Should you buy AI tools or build your own?

Buy: Buy when the job is standard, such as submission intake or claims texting, and you have no data team.40 Most enterprise AI is now bought: 76% of use cases, up from 53% in 2024.40 MIT found vendor partnerships reached production about twice as often as internal builds.39,41

Build: Build when your guidelines, appetite and loss history are your edge, or you must explain every step to an examiner.14,8 A thin build on your own rules also avoids lock-in while the NAIC vendor registry is still a draft.33 A common middle path: buy the reading step, and keep the rules and the decision log yourself.

When should you pick something else?

  • Your core systems can't take the AI output: fix the integration first. Legacy integration is insurers' top barrier to scaling AI, named by 50%.13
  • You already run Guidewire or Applied: check the AI they now own, such as hx and Cytora, before adding a separate tool.27,28
  • Your team only needs to write and summarise faster: an enterprise plan of ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft 365 Copilot will do. Bring it into your AI program, which covers generative AI too.8
  • Simple, high-volume claims: straight-through rules in your claims system may already do the job.42
