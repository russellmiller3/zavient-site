AI in insurance: what are carriers, MGAs and TPAs using it for?

Bottom line
  • AI informs; people decide.1 Of 193 auto insurers in 2022, 96 used AI to inform adjusters; none to deny claims.2
  • Start where reading piles up: submission intake for underwriters and complex claim files for adjusters.3,4
  • For managing general agents (MGAs), clean portfolio data is becoming a condition for capacity.5 MGA premium grew 12% in 2025.6
  • Thirty jurisdictions use the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) AI bulletin.7 A new supplement lists regulators' AI questions.45
  • Keep a named person on every claim denial. Lawsuits claiming algorithms, not reviewers, drove health denials have partly survived dismissal.9,10

What is AI in insurance, and what does it change?

AI in insurance reads and drafts for underwriters and adjusters, and scores prices, renewals and claims.3,11,57 People keep the decision: state regulators apply the same standards whatever tool you use.12,8 Clean data and a real human check make or break it.13,9

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every decision: underwriters accept, decline and price, MGA underwriters bind, and adjusters own every claim denial.14,2,8 Senior management and the board own the AI program, and a licensed person checks anything a client sees.12,8,16

  • Underwriters keep accept, decline and price. New York expects each adverse decision to state the information used and its source.14,15
  • Adjusters keep every claim denial, at carriers and third-party administrators (TPAs) alike. In the regulators' auto survey, 9 of 193 insurers used AI for claim approvals and none for denials.2
  • MGA underwriters keep the bind decision within their authority. Carriers' AI programs should cover AI from outside firms, so expect their questions.8
  • At large agencies, a licensed person checks anything a client sees. Verify limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements against the policy forms.16
  • Senior management and the board own the AI program, with a committee from underwriting, claims, compliance and legal.12,8 The bulletin lists checking AI against expert review as one way to validate it.8
  • Reviewers need the authority to reverse, change or escalate AI output, and their overrides should be logged. The NAIC's new AI supplement asks about both.46,44

Where does AI pay first in insurance?

AI pays first in submission intake for underwriters, then claim file summaries and claims analytics for adjusters.3,17,4,53 For MGAs the early win is bordereaux and carrier reporting; for large agencies, policy checking.5,23

  1. Submission intake and triage (carriers and MGAs)

    Underwriters spend up to 40% of their time on admin and other non-core work.3 Only 9% of underwriting leaders use AI to read and prepare submission data to a large extent; 68% expect to in three years.17

    Payoff to measure: Hours from submission received to quote, and the share of submissions quoted.

  2. Claim file summaries for adjusters (carriers)

    Claims is the top AI area for auto insurers, and the main use is informing adjusters.2,18 Aviva, a large UK carrier, reports over £90m of claims savings65; McKinsey says it assesses complex liability 23 days faster.4

    Payoff to measure: Adjuster minutes per file review, and days to final payment (40.7 on average for US home claims in 2026).19

  3. Claims analytics: severity, litigation and recovery (carriers and TPAs)

    Sedgwick reports litigated general liability claims cost over 5.5 times others, and 47% had an attorney on day one.53 Attorney involvement in auto injury claims rose from 40% to nearly 50% by 2022.52

    Payoff to measure: Days from claim report to first adjuster contact on high-risk claims, and dollars recovered from at-fault parties (subrogation).

  4. Pricing, renewal and loss-ratio analytics (carriers and MGAs)

    In WTW's survey of property and casualty (P&C) insurers, analytics leaders spent about 6 cents less per premium dollar on claims and costs.64 That's a link, not proof; models can also flag the riskiest submissions for underwriters.64,61

    Payoff to measure: Loss ratio (claims per premium dollar) by class, territory and agent, and retention of your best renewals.

  5. AI inventory and examiner file (carriers)

    Examiners can ask for your written AI program, model inventory, validation work and vendor contracts.8 Only 24% of insurance leaders are very confident they could pass an independent AI governance review within 90 days.20

    Payoff to measure: Days to produce the examiner file, and the share of AI uses inventoried.

  6. Bordereaux and carrier reporting (MGAs)

    AM Best says consistent, transparent portfolio data is becoming a condition for MGAs' access to capital.5 It also reports added scrutiny of MGAs as the market matures.21

    Payoff to measure: Days to close each bordereau (the premium and claims report to a carrier), and carrier data queries per month.

  7. Fraud screening (carriers)

    Fraud costs US consumers $308.6 billion a year, a coalition figure the NAIC cites.22 Among insurers the regulators surveyed, 58% in auto and 55% in home use or plan AI for it.18

    Payoff to measure: Hit rate of referrals to your special investigations unit, checked by customer group.

  8. Policy checking and coverage comparison (large agencies)

    Coverage form analysis is agencies' second most common AI use, at 43%.23 A qualified person still verifies limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements against the policy.16

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per policy check, and errors caught before the client sees the policy.

What do policy and claims models predict, and what does the person decide?

Models predict a number, such as a price factor, lapse risk, claim size or fraud score.57,11 A named person still decides: the actuary sets prices, the underwriter selects risks and the adjuster owns each claim.57,61,11

Policy and portfolio analytics cover pricing, risk selection, renewals and loss-ratio drivers.57,61 Claims analytics cover severity, litigation, fraud and recovery.11,64 Ireland's central bank found insurers' AI mostly assisted, with none fully autonomous.66

Which rules apply to AI in insurance?

The NAIC AI model bulletin applies in 30 jurisdictions, per the NAIC map of 13 Nov 2026. California, Colorado and New York have their own rules.7 A new NAIC supplement sets out what regulators can ask, and the EU AI Act covers life and health pricing from Dec 2027.45,49,50 The NAIC vendor framework is still a draft.47

Where does AI in insurance go wrong?

AI in insurance goes wrong when review becomes a rubber stamp, outcomes differ by group, or vendor models go unchecked.9,37,8 Scattered evidence of controls and pilots that never reach the desk are the other common failures.20,38

  • Rubber-stamp review: a lawsuit alleges Cigna doctors denied over 300,000 payment requests at about 1.2 seconds each, and a court let it proceed.9,36 Nothing is proven yet; still, log review time, sample decisions and show reviewers the evidence, not only the score.
  • Unequal outcomes: Black homeowners allege State Farm's claim-screening algorithms slowed and questioned their claims more; the claim survived dismissal in 2023.37 Test outcomes by group before and after go-live; Colorado and New York expect it for covered lines.30,14
  • Vendor AI treated as someone else's problem. The NAIC bulletin and New York keep you responsible for vendor models, so put audit rights and regulator cooperation in the contract.8,14
  • Controls you can't prove: 68% of insurance leaders say their AI controls exist, but the evidence is scattered across teams and tools.20 Keep one inventory entry and one evidence file per AI use, because examiners can ask for both.8
  • Trusting a vendor's accuracy figure: score it on your own files against what your experts decided, a check the bulletin names.8 Data quality is insurers' second biggest AI barrier, named by 45%.13
  • Pilots that never reach the desk: only 7% of carriers in a 2026 claims study report scalable AI success.38 Pilot one workflow on one team with one owner and one measure; few task-specific AI tools reach production.39

Questions buyers ask about AI in insurance

How is AI used in insurance?

Insurers use AI mostly to read and draft for the people who decide.1,11 In regulators' surveys, claims and fraud lead in auto and home, and underwriting leads in life.18 Conning names AI and data-driven underwriting among the drivers of MGA growth.6

What is a predictive model in insurance?

A predictive model in insurance is a statistical or machine-learning model that estimates claim frequency, severity, lapse or fraud from past data.57,63 State rate reviewers check both generalized linear models (GLMs) and gradient boosting machines in filings.57 Studies find gradient boosting often wins on claim frequency, but not always on severity.58,59

What is insurance analytics, and how do insurers analyze claims data?

Insurance analytics means using policy and claims data to find what drives losses, then acting on it.61,11 For claims, models flag likely severity, attorney involvement, fraud and recovery chances so adjusters act early.11,52,54 Early action matters: represented auto injury claimants waited a median of about 440 days.52

Where should a carrier or MGA start with AI?

A carrier or MGA should start with one reading-heavy workflow, such as submission intake or complex claim files.3,17 Pick one with a clear before-and-after number. Add it to your AI inventory from day one, since examiners can ask for it.8

What is the best AI for insurance?

No single AI is best for insurance: pick by job, such as submission intake, claim summaries, pricing or fraud.28,25,64 Check what your core system vendor already owns, then test any tool on your own files against your experts' calls.27,8

What will the examiner ask about our AI?

Examiners ask for your written AI program, your inventory of models and AI systems, and how you validate and monitor them.8 The NAIC's new AI Risk Evaluation Supplement, for voluntary state use from 2027, adds four exhibits: AI counts, your program, high-risk models and their data.45,46,69 For vendor AI, expect requests for your due diligence, contracts and audits.8,12

Does the NAIC AI bulletin apply to MGAs?

Not directly: the NAIC AI bulletin is addressed to insurers.12 Insurers must cover AI they rely on from outside firms, so carrier partners may ask an MGA about its AI.8 AM Best describes added scrutiny of MGAs as the market matures.21

Is AI going to replace insurance jobs?

No: regulators find insurers' AI use mostly assisted, and underwriters and adjusters keep the decisions.66,2 The NAIC says AI is more likely to support human workers than replace them.11 Only 16% of P&C insurers use AI to augment underwriters today; 60% plan to by 2028, WTW found.64

Which AI rules apply to insurers in my state?

The NAIC AI bulletin applies in 30 jurisdictions (28 states, DC and Puerto Rico), per the NAIC map of 13 Nov 2026.7 California, Colorado and New York have their own guidance or rules.7 The map doesn't confirm each state kept every element, so read your state's own text.7

What does an AI platform for a carrier or MGA cost?

One published list price runs from $20,000 a year for a 5-user MGA underwriting tool to $98,000 for its enterprise tier.70 Most carrier platforms, such as Hi Marley and Federato, publish no price, so budget from a scoped quote for one workflow.25,26

Should you buy AI tools or build your own?

Buy the standard reading step, such as submission intake or claims texting, and build only where your guidelines, appetite and loss history are your edge.40,14,8 Bought tools reached production about twice as often as internal builds in MIT's study.39,41

Buy: Buy when the job is standard, such as submission intake or claims texting, and you have no data team.40 Most enterprise AI is now bought: 76% of use cases, up from 53% in 2024.40 MIT found vendor partnerships reached production about twice as often as internal builds.39,41

Build: Build when your guidelines, appetite and loss history are your edge, or you must explain every step to an examiner.14,8 A thin build on your own rules also avoids lock-in while the NAIC vendor registry is still a draft.33 A common middle path: buy the reading step, and keep the rules and the decision log yourself.

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your core systems can't take AI output, your Guidewire or Applied stack already includes AI, or simple claims run fine on rules.13,27,28,42 For plain writing and summaries, an enterprise plan of ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft 365 Copilot will do, inside your AI program.8

  • Your core systems can't take the AI output: fix the integration first. Legacy integration is insurers' top barrier to scaling AI, named by 50%.13
  • You already run Guidewire or Applied: check the AI they now own, such as hx and Cytora, before adding a separate tool.27,28
  • Your team only needs to write and summarise faster: an enterprise plan of ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft 365 Copilot will do. Bring it into your AI program, which covers generative AI too.8
  • Simple, high-volume claims: straight-through rules in your claims system may already do the job.42
