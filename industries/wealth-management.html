AI for wealth management: what are RIAs actually using it for?

Bottom line
  • Most advisers now use AI, mainly for meeting notes, follow-up and drafting client emails.1,2
  • No AI-specific Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) rule exists; examiners apply existing duties on marketing, records and privacy.3,4
  • AI meeting notes can be required records, so check each tool's retention against the five-year rule.5,6
  • Advisors keep the client and the sign-off; most registered investment advisers (RIAs) plan to add advisors as AI frees time.7,8
  • Deepfake voice and video requests to move money are now a named SEC exam focus.9,3

What is AI for wealth management, and what does it change?

AI for wealth management drafts and summarizes advisor work: meeting notes, follow-up emails and review prep.2,10 Advisors still own the advice and the final work product.7 The record makes or breaks it, because AI notes can be required books and records.5,6

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your team stays in charge of every recommendation and anything a client sees: AI drafts, and the advisor edits and approves.7,12

  • The advisor edits AI notes and approves each follow-up before it goes.7 Vendors such as Jump build that approval step in (vendor claim).11
  • The advisor makes every recommendation; AI gathers data and drafts options, and the fiduciary duty stays with the adviser.12 Planners check each figure, since tested models slip on multi-step financial math.13,14
  • The chief compliance officer (CCO) sets retention, reviews AI vendors and keeps the AI inventory.5,15 Most firms already keep that inventory: 86% in the Investment Adviser Association (IAA) survey.16
  • Operations releases money or changes an address only after calling the client back on a number already on file.17,18 Deepfake voice and video requests are a named SEC exam focus.9

Where does AI pay first at an RIA or wealth firm?

AI pays first in the hours around each client meeting: prep, notes and follow-up. Forms and client emails come next.

  1. Meeting prep, notes and follow-up

    Kitces found 5.3 hours of prep and 6 of follow-through a week.19 Notetakers are the top AI tool, used by 42.86% of advisors in T3's survey.2

    Payoff to measure: Advisor hours a week on prep and follow-up, before and after.

  2. Customer relationship management (CRM) entry, forms and account opening

    Vendors say 20-40% of paper account forms come back not in good order (NIGO).20,21 One firm reports its NIGO rate fell from 35% to 11.5% after moving to digital forms.22

    Payoff to measure: NIGO rate on your own forms, and days from signed paperwork to a funded account.

  3. Drafting client emails and letters

    Among RIAs, 49% already use AI to draft client communications, per AssetMark's survey of 400 advisors.10 It is the top use worldwide too, at 51% in the Financial Planning Standards Board (FPSB) survey.23

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per client email, and compliance edits per draft.

  4. Tax return and plan document review

    A new financial plan takes about 18 hours, per the Kitces Report.24 Tools now read returns into checklists; Hazel lists a tax agent at $160 a month (vendor price).25

    Payoff to measure: Planner hours per new plan, and errors the planner catches.

  5. Compliance surveillance and archive review

    In the IAA survey, 60% of CCOs hold more than one role, and 72% of firms stepped up testing of AI use.16,26 The SEC lists compliance monitoring and surveillance among the AI uses it is studying.3

    Payoff to measure: Share of captured messages reviewed each month, and hours per review.

  6. Fraud checks on money movement

    The SEC's 2027 exam priorities ask how firms prepare for AI phishing and deepfake voice and video.9,3 Global regulators (IOSCO) and state regulators (NASAA) both warned about AI-enabled scams in 2026.27,28

    Payoff to measure: Share of wire and address-change requests verified by callback.

Which rules apply to AI at an RIA?

Your existing rules apply to AI, since the SEC has issued no AI-specific rule: Advisers Act duties, marketing, records and privacy.3,4

Where does AI go wrong at RIAs and wealth firms?

AI goes wrong at RIAs in false claims, missing records, deepfake fraud, unapproved tools, wrong math and agents with too much reach.

  • False AI claims drew fines first.44,45 The SEC fined Delphia $225,000 and Global Predictions $175,000; say only what your tools really do.46,54
  • Notes that never reach your archive: FinMate's page says it keeps transcripts 6 months, and required records are kept 5 years.31,5 Check each tool's retention against Rule 204-2, and set exports to your archive before go-live.6
  • Deepfake requests to move money or change an address, a named SEC exam focus.9,27 Call the client back on a number already on file before acting, as the SEC warns of impersonators.17,18
  • Shadow AI: 82% of advisors who use AI experiment on their own, per Schwab.1 NASAA warns about client data in public tools; an acceptable use policy and an AI inventory are the fix.53,16
  • Wrong numbers in plans: in one benchmark, accuracy fell from over 90% on basic tasks to about 40% on complex ones.13 Leading models scored about 70% on personal finance questions, so a planner checks every figure.55
  • Agents with too much reach: FINRA names autonomy, scope and auditability as agent risks.56,50 Limit what an agent can touch, and log its prompts and outputs.50

Questions RIA leaders ask about AI

Can advisors use ChatGPT with client data?

Not on personal accounts; use a business plan whose terms bar training on your data and that your vendor review covers.15,53 One r/CFP advisor said archiving needed an enterprise plan's compliance tools.57

Do AI meeting notes have to be archived?

Yes, often: notes that record advice or client communications can be required records under Rule 204-2, kept 5 years.5 Cooley suggests deciding by category, with counsel.6

Do we have to record client meetings to use an AI notetaker?

Not in one vendor's reading: Jump says neither Rule 204-2 nor FINRA Rule 4511(b) requires a recording.33 Advisors on r/CFP say some compliance teams approved tools because no audio is kept.57

Will AI replace financial advisors?

No sign of it yet: 73% of RIAs in a Cerulli study plan to add junior advisors as AI expands capacity.8 McKinsey projects a shortfall of about 100,000 advisors by 2034.58

Where should an RIA start with AI?

Start with meeting notes for one team of advisors, since notetakers are the most-used AI tool.2 Set retention and archive exports first, then compare prep and follow-up hours before and after.6,19

How much time does AI save financial advisors?

Over half of AI adopters in AssetMark's survey of 400 advisors save 4 or more hours a week.10 McKinsey estimates generative AI could save 6-12% of advisor time.58

Should an RIA buy AI tools or build its own?

Buy: Buy first: tools for notes, CRM entry and drafting already list $39 to $184 per advisor a month.29,30 Jump and Zocks name archive connections to Global Relay, Smarsh or Proofpoint.30,32 Zavient's view: check the AI already in your CRM or custodian first.35,25

Build: Zavient's view: build only where your data and workflow are the edge, such as onboarding across CRM, custodian and planning tools. Few firms have the data base for it: T3 found 3.27% use a data warehouse.2 Governance is the build every firm needs, and 86% of surveyed firms already have an AI policy.16

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your CRM or custodian already sells the job, staff only need drafting, or advisors need support staff more than software.59

  • Your CRM already sells the job: Wealthbox's AI Notetaker is a $49 add-on that keeps notes in the CRM (vendor claim).35,36
  • Your custodian already has AI: Hazel runs on Altruist, from $60 a month (vendor price).25
  • Staff only need internal drafting and summaries: Microsoft 365 Copilot is a $21 a user add-on (standard price, read 26 Nov 2026).39
  • Advisors are buried in admin and have no support staff: Kitces found support staff cut admin time more than technology did.59
