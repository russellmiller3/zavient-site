What is AI for insurance agencies, and what does it change?

AI for insurance agencies reads carrier documents, checks policies and matches commission statements, so account managers and producers key less.2,10 Per the Big "I" 2026 study release, 46% of agencies use AI.1 Clean data in the agency management system (AMS) and a person checking client-facing output make or break it.5,7

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every client-facing call: producers own coverage advice and binding, account managers approve policy checks, and the principal owns the AI policy.6,7,2

  • Licensed producer: signs off on any coverage advice, proposal or bind request. Under the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) licensing model, advising on coverage terms needs a license.6,22
  • Account manager: approves each policy-check flag and endorsement (policy change) before it reaches the client or carrier. Law-firm guidance treats every AI output as a draft for review.7,23
  • Accounting lead: clears every direct bill line the AI can't match, and posts the result. One agency cut this reconciliation from two days to five minutes.3,10
  • Principal or COO: owns the written AI policy, vendor contracts and the security program. Only 21% of agencies have a written AI policy.2,16

Where does AI pay first at an independent agency?

AI pays first in direct bill reconciliation and policy checking, then commercial submission intake, because each saves measured time or guards against errors and omissions (E&O) claims.3,4,5

  1. Direct bill and commission reconciliation (accounting team)

    Direct bill means carriers bill clients and send you commission statements to match.3 One agency cut that reconciliation from two full days to five minutes, and another matched about 80% of statements automatically in month one.3,4

    Payoff to measure: Days to close the month, and the share of statement lines matched without a touch.

  2. Policy checking (account managers and service staff)

    Failure to procure, not getting the coverage the client asked for, causes about 30% of agency errors and omissions (E&O) claims.24,25 In the 2025 Best Practices Study, an AI policy-checking pilot saved each support staffer 30 minutes a day.4

    Payoff to measure: Policies checked per account manager each week, and errors caught before the policy reaches the client.

  3. Commercial submission intake and prefill (account managers, producers)

    In Ivans' 2026 survey of 702 agents, 74% named re-keying their top workflow pain.5 Asked for one AI feature, 51% of agents chose prefill: carrier forms filled from data you already hold.5

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per new commercial submission, and markets approached per account.

  4. Loss runs, quote comparison and proposals (producers)

    In the 2025 Best Practices Study, one agency's AI for loss runs (claims histories) and coverage analysis saved staff 7.5 hours a week.4 Among agency staff saving the most time in Liberty Mutual's study, 34% use AI to compare policy details.27

    Payoff to measure: Proposals sent per producer each month, and the share of quotes that win.

  5. Service email triage and endorsements (account managers, service staff)

    Vertafore says its email agent saves 80% of handling time at 98% accuracy (vendor claim).10 Change requests also teach new account managers the business, so keep a review step.28

    Payoff to measure: First-response time, and endorsements done per person per day without rework.

  6. Meeting notes and client recaps (producers, account managers)

    AI users in Liberty Mutual's 2026 carrier study save about 4 hours a week.27,29 Among agency staff saving the most time, meeting notes was the top use, at 44%.27

    Payoff to measure: Hours a week returned per producer, and follow-ups sent within 24 hours.

  7. Clean-up of client data after an acquisition (operations)

    In the 2025 Best Practices Study, AI-assisted data clean-up cut one agency's integration from 180 days to 45.4 About one agency in eight (13%) did a merger or acquisition in the past two years.30

    Payoff to measure: Days to convert an acquired book, and duplicate client records left.

Which rules apply to AI for insurance agencies?

Producer licensing, data security and robocall rules bind agencies directly, while the NAIC AI bulletin binds the insurers you place business with.6,16,17,18 None of this is legal advice.

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act (#218), as adopted by statesIn force through state licensing lawsOnly licensed people may sell, solicit or negotiate insurance, and negotiating includes advising on coverage terms.6 Unlicensed staff are exempt only for clerical work paid no commission.6,22 A chatbot that advises on coverage or binds needs a licensed person behind it.6,7
NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (#668)Adopted in 28 jurisdictions (NAIC map, 3 Nov 2026)It applies to licensees, agencies included, and requires a risk-based security program that oversees outside vendors.16,40 Only licensees with fewer than 10 employees are exempt from the program, so put your AI vendors in yours.16 Notify the commissioner of certain cybersecurity events.16
New York Department of Financial Services (DFS) cybersecurity rule, 23 NYCRR Part 500In force; final phase took effect 1 Nov 2025It covers DFS-licensed businesses, insurance agents and brokers included, and the final phase requires multi-factor authentication for every user.42,41 The limited exemption needs under 20 staff and contractors, under $7.5M revenue, or under $15M assets.42 An agency of 40 or more fails the headcount test, so check the other two.42
New York DFS industry letter on AI cybersecurity risksGuidance issued 16 Oct 2024; adds no new requirementsIt tells covered firms to address AI in risk assessments and vendor due diligence.43 It also asks for deepfake-resistant multi-factor authentication and keeping less client data.43
Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Declaratory Ruling 24-17, Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA)In force; adopted 2 Feb 2024AI-generated voices count as artificial or prerecorded voices.17 Calls need prior express consent, and telemarketing calls need prior express written consent.17,44 Have consent records before an AI voice agent calls about renewals or cross-selling.44
NAIC Model Bulletin on the Use of AI Systems by InsurersAdopted in 30 jurisdictions (NAIC map, 13 Nov 2026)The bulletin binds insurers, not agencies.18 The carriers you place business with may still ask how your agency uses AI with their customers. Our insurance page covers the bulletin in depth.

Where does AI go wrong at an agency?

AI goes wrong at agencies when it invents facts in submissions, loses its human check, leaks client data or calls clients without consent records.19,20,15,17

  • Made-up facts in submissions: an E&O underwriter reports a broker presented an AI-invented nil claims history though the attached claims run showed losses.19 Placement and clerical errors already drive E&O claims, so check every material field against the source documents.45
  • Authority creep: staff come to trust AI that is usually right, and review fades until it disappears.20 Write down what AI may do alone, and keep advice, limit changes and binding with a licensed producer.7,6
  • Client data in public chat tools: 45% of agents use ChatGPT or other public tools.15 Gallagher and WTW name staff data leaks into outside AI as a risk, so approve tools and block client data in public ones.33,32
  • AI voice calls without consent records: the FCC treats AI voices as artificial voices under the TCPA.17 Check prior express consent, written for telemarketing, before any AI outbound call.44
  • Assuming your E&O policy covers AI mistakes: most agent and broker policies still respond, but insurers elsewhere in professional liability have begun proposing AI exclusions.45,46 Read your renewal wording for AI exclusions or sublimits.46
  • Cutting the work juniors learn on: change requests and data entry teach new account managers the business.28 Move juniors to reviewing AI drafts and client work, and keep a teaching loop.28

Questions agency leaders ask about AI

Will AI replace account managers and service staff?

No, the evidence points to AI taking keying and checking work while people keep advice and sign-off.47,7 The 2025 Best Practices Study says AI should augment, not replace, human expertise.4

Is it safe to put client data into ChatGPT?

Not in an unapproved public tool, so use only the tools your written AI policy approves.7,43 Add each AI vendor to the vendor oversight in your security program.16

Can a chatbot quote or bind coverage?

Only with a licensed producer behind it, because advising on coverage terms counts as negotiating under producer licensing law.6,7 Unlicensed staff are exempt only for administrative or clerical work paid no commission, and Big "I" advises asking your state insurance department when unsure.6,22,20

How much time does AI save an agency?

About 4 hours a week per AI user, in Liberty Mutual's 2026 carrier survey of agency principals and staff.27,29 In the 2025 Best Practices Study, one agency's policy-checking pilot saved each support staffer 30 minutes a day.4

Where should a mid-size agency start with AI?

Start with direct bill reconciliation and policy checking, where agencies report days and hours saved.3,4 Policy checking also guards against failure to procure, the top cause of agency E&O claims.24

Does our E&O policy cover mistakes made with AI?

Yes, usually, unless the policy carries a specific AI exclusion.46,45 Some professional liability insurers have begun proposing AI exclusions, so read your renewal wording.45

Should you buy AI from your agency software vendor or build it?

Buy for standard work inside your agency management system, and build only where your own process or carrier mix is the edge.21,10

Buy: Buy when the work runs inside your agency management system and a vendor module reads your data in place, as with reconciliation and policy checks.10,12 Most enterprise AI is now bought: 76% of use cases, up from 53% in 2024.21 MIT found outside partnerships reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds.48,49

Build: Build or configure when your own process is the edge, such as commercial submissions across niche carriers or data clean-up after acquisitions.5,4 Build also when you need firm control over what a tool may say to clients.6 A Big "I" AI Labs deck warns that the vendor's contract leaves your agency responsible for what a tool may legally do.20

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when carrier feeds are the pain, steps never change, you need volume moved out, or your agency software already ships it.5,2,14,10

  • Your pain is carrier connectivity, not reading documents: use carrier download and the insurance data standards from ACORD, the standards body, before AI.50,5 With 74% of agents re-keying data as standard practice, the feed comes first.5
  • The screens and steps never change: use robotic process automation or your agency system's own workflow rules. Only 6% of agencies use RPA today.2
  • You need the work off your desks, not just done faster: an outsourced policy-checking service, such as Patra's full-service model, moves the volume.14
  • Your agency software already ships the feature: turn on Vertafore's agents or Applied Epic's AI before any custom project.10,12
