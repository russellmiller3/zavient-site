What is AI for family offices, and what does it change?

AI for family offices is software that reads documents, drafts reports and summarizes meetings inside the office's daily operations.13,5 For most offices the aim is a leaner office, not better returns, and privacy comes first.5,12 Clean data plumbing and a person who signs off make or break it.6,13

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every decision: the controller signs off what reaches the books, a person releases every payment, and professionals own investment, tax and family calls.16,14,12

  • Your controller signs off before anything AI extracts reaches the general ledger or the reporting system. Review AI output before it enters a system of record.6,16,13
  • A person reviews, authorizes and releases every payment, even when AI queues the bill.14 In one vendor's statement workflow (Risclarity, writing for FOX), an analyst reviews and maps AI-read data before import.16
  • Investment, tax, legal and family decisions stay with qualified professionals. AI output informs them and a professional reviews it.12,5
  • The COO owns the AI policy and the list of approved tools. Offices Simple interviewed put policy first, then approved tools, data segmentation and human review.6,26
  • Keep a decision log when AI informs a trust or investment call, with the source behind each point. AI prompts and logs may be discoverable records.15,17

Where does AI pay first in a family office?

AI pays first in investment reporting, then capital call and K-1 intake with bill pay and the close, then meeting notes and knowledge work.1,6,13

  1. Investment reporting and aggregation

    72% of offices use or pilot AI for reporting automation.1 54% of single family offices keep aggregation and reporting wholly in-house, so the hours sit with your staff.9

    Payoff to measure: Days from month end to the family's report, and hours of manual entry per report.

  2. Capital calls, K-1s, bill pay and the close

    Document intake is a common first target, and RSM names capital call notices, bill pay and ledger coding.6,13 Controllers are the most-posted role in our sample, at a median posted pay of $137,000 by our math.30

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per notice from inbox to booked entry, and days to close each entity.

  3. Meeting notes, knowledge and office admin

    38% of family offices use AI for meeting notes and 32% for knowledge management.3 Heidrick & Struggles finds technology and AI are widening the operating models open to family offices.29

    Payoff to measure: Hours spent on minutes, follow-ups and finding past decisions.

  4. Choosing the software stack AI runs on

    The average family office tech stack costs $100,000 to $500,000 a year, per Campden Wealth and AlTi Tiedemann.11 Most AI features are narrow and tied to one platform, so the stack decides what AI can reach.13

    Payoff to measure: Manual re-keys between systems each month, and reports built from one data set.

  5. Research and diligence summaries

    72% of family offices use or pilot AI for investment research and 71% for market summaries.1 61% of family offices say AI improved investment outcomes.1

    Payoff to measure: Hours per investment memo, with every figure traced to its source.

  6. The operating companies the family owns

    77% of families UBS surveyed have an active operating business.8 67% hold controlling stakes, so the office often sets the companies' reporting and close.3

    Payoff to measure: Days to roll the companies into the family's balance sheet.

Which rules shape AI in a family office?

No AI-specific rule applies, but the SEC family office exclusion, Regulation S-P for registered offices and Form 13F shape what AI may touch.18,20,22

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
SEC family office rule, 17 CFR 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1In force since 29 Aug 2011 (amended 2016)An office is not an investment adviser if it serves only family clients, is family-owned and controlled, and doesn't hold itself out as one.18,19 The rule says nothing about AI, so your own policy sets the bar.
Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Regulation S-P amendmentsCompliance from 3 Dec 2025 (larger firms) and 3 Jun 2026 (smaller)These apply to SEC-registered advisers, such as a multi-family office that registers.20 You need an incident response program, customer notice within 30 days, and vendors who report breaches within 72 hours.20,21 Any AI vendor that touches client data falls under that oversight.
Form 13FIn force; due within 45 days of each quarter endAn office with $100 million or more in Section 13(f) securities files Form 13F, family office or not.22,23 AI can draft the holdings list, and a person checks it before filing.
FinCEN beneficial ownership reportingUS companies exempt; final rule effective 14 Aug 2026US entities no longer file beneficial ownership reports with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN).24,25 Keep your own entity register current anyway, because AI that books by entity is only as good as that list.
NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkVoluntary; version 1.0 from Jan 2023, under revisionA free, voluntary frame for writing your AI policy, with a separate profile for generative AI.26 It is guidance, not law, so adopt what fits an office of your size.

Not legal advice. Check with your counsel.

Where does AI go wrong in a family office?

AI goes wrong in a family office when family data leaks into public tools, extraction errors reach the books, or fraudsters clone a voice to move money.13,16,28

  • Family data in public chatbots: RSM warns against putting confidential reports into public ChatGPT.13 Give staff one approved tool, and ask each vendor whether it trains on your data.6,13
  • Voice-cloned wire requests: business email compromise drew $3,046,598,558 in reported losses in 2025.27 Agree a secret phrase with the family, and call back on a known number before any wire.28
  • Extraction errors that reach the books: an AI misread on a capital call or K-1 flows into every report after it. Risclarity, a vendor writing for FOX, has analysts review and map output before import into the system of record.16
  • Logs nobody planned for: AI prompts and logs may be discoverable records.15 Set retention rules and keep a decision log before staff use AI on trust or investment work.
  • Builds only one person understands: Simple's 2026 report finds in-house builds tend to break on data plumbing and on dependence on one key person.6 Document each build and give it a second owner.
  • No measured payoff: 24% of offices see no measurable impact from AI, and 31% of smaller offices.3 Pick one workflow and one before-and-after number before you start.

Questions operators ask about AI in a family office

Is it safe to put family data into AI tools?

Only in approved tools that keep your data out of model training and inside your office's controls.13 Offices Simple interviewed name approved tools, data segmentation and running models locally as their controls.6

Where should a family office start with AI?

Start with one document-heavy workflow, such as capital call intake or monthly reporting.6,1 PwC sets guidelines and training in the first 3 months, then scales proven workflows over 3 to 12 months.12

Do we need to hire an AI person?

Not usually, because offices add AI duties to finance and operations roles: 10 of 26 postings we read ask for it.30 Only one was an AI engineer.33 Lack of internal expertise is still the top barrier, named by 57% of family offices.4

Does the SEC regulate how a family office uses AI?

No, not with an AI-specific rule: an office that meets the family office exclusion is not an investment adviser.18 A multi-family office that registers as an adviser falls under Regulation S-P's data rules.20

Do AI chats and notes become records we may have to produce?

Yes, they can: Northern Trust warns that AI prompts and logs may be discoverable records.15 Set retention rules before staff use AI for trust or investment work.

What does AI cost a family office?

No standard price exists: the average technology stack AI runs on costs $100,000 to $500,000 a year, per Campden Wealth and AlTi Tiedemann.11 For scale, IT is 7% of the average office's pure cost.7

Should a family office buy AI or build it?

Buy the data platform and build the office-specific views on top, the split Simple's 2026 report calls buy the plumbing, build the view.6

Buy: Buy the plumbing: reporting, aggregation and accounting platforms are standard, and vendors warn offices against building their own system of record.6 About four in ten offices already use third-party consolidated reporting software.4

Build: Build the views and workflows that are yours, such as your entities, reports and privacy rules; Simple calls it buy the plumbing, build the view.6 Offices keep work in-house for expertise (67%), privacy (63%) and control (63%).7 RSM describes three shapes: a data warehouse, agents, or a hybrid.13

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your platform already has the AI feature, the gap is cybersecurity, or the problem is data plumbing rather than judgment.13,9,6

  • Your reporting platform, such as Addepar or Canoe, already has the AI feature you need: use it first. Most AI features are tied to one platform.13
  • The gap is cybersecurity, not workflow: buy a managed security service. 38% of single family offices fully outsource cybersecurity.9
  • The problem is data plumbing, not judgment: a vendor's managed data operations may fit. Offices already hand IT to outside providers.6
  • You run a registered multi-family office: start from our wealth management guide, since Regulation S-P applies to registered advisers.20
