AI in construction: what are contractors actually using it for?

Bottom line
  • Contractors mostly use AI for office paperwork so far; almost none use it in field production.1,2
  • Your project software probably already ships AI features, so check your plan before buying anything new.3,4
  • AI drafts the request for information (RFI), log or submittal check; a licensed or accountable person reads and signs it.5,6
  • General chatbots misread contract clauses and cameras miss small safety gear, so start with narrow, checked jobs.7,8
  • Ask your broker whether the new Insurance Services Office (ISO) generative AI exclusions are on your liability policy.9,10

What is AI for construction companies, and what does it change?

AI for construction companies is software that reads and drafts the job's paperwork: RFIs, submittals, logs, minutes and spec searches.1 What changes: PMs review drafts instead of writing them, and search project files instead of hunting, which took 5.5 hours a week.11,15 Licensed people still decide and sign.5,6

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every sign-off: project managers approve AI drafts, engineers keep the seal, the competent person stops unsafe work, and estimators own the bid.11,5,6

  • Your team stays in charge of every sign-off: AI drafts, and the project manager, superintendent, engineer or estimator approves.11 Procore builds its agents to draft for a person to approve (vendor).11
  • Licensed engineers keep judgement and the seal on delegated design; ASCE says AI cannot stand in for a professional engineer's (PE) judgement.5,12 North Carolina's board says the licensee must be able to reproduce AI output independently.13
  • Your competent person still stops the work: the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) defines that role as someone with authority to correct hazards promptly.6 A camera can flag a missing harness; only that person can act on it.
  • Estimators keep the bid number they sign. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) still projects about 17,600 estimator openings a year.14

Where does AI pay first at a contractor?

AI pays first in project search, since staff lose about 5.5 hours a week looking for project data. RFI drafting and submittal checks come next.15,19,16

  1. Project search pays first: answers from drawings, specs and files

    AI pays first in project search, because staff lose about 5.5 hours a week looking for project data.15 Procore, Autodesk and Fieldwire now ship this search inside their platforms (vendor claims).16,17,18

    Payoff to measure: Minutes to answer a field question from the drawings and specs, sampled before and after.

  2. Drafting and sorting requests for information (RFIs)

    A 2013 study of 1,362 projects put each RFI at about 8 hours and $1,080 to process.19 AI can draft the question, but a person must shape each RFI, says AIA Contract Documents.20

    Payoff to measure: Hours from field issue to RFI sent, and the share sent back as unclear.

  3. First-pass submittal checks against the spec

    Procore and Buildertrend now sell submittal review features (vendor claims).16,21

    Payoff to measure: Days from submittal received to first comments, counting misses found later.

  4. Daily logs, meeting minutes and owner updates

    AI drafts daily reports, client updates and meeting minutes for a person to review before sending. Raken and Buildertrend sell these features (vendor claims).22,21

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per daily log or owner update, and the edits a superintendent makes before sending.

  5. Estimating support: scope gaps and bid leveling

    Among Associated General Contractors (AGC) survey respondents, 23% already use AI in estimating.1 Takeoff tools, their prices and their accuracy are in our estimating guide.

    Payoff to measure: Scope gaps caught before bid day, and estimator hours per bid.

  6. Contract and subcontract risk review

    Procore sells a contract review agent; Trimble lists contract risk review as still in development (vendor claims).16,23 A general chat model scored 0.33 accuracy on contract clauses in one study.7

    Payoff to measure: Risky clauses flagged per contract, checked against your project manager's own markup.

  7. Back-office vendor invoice coding

    Office and administrative work is the top AI use among AGC contractors surveyed, at 45%.1 Buildertrend sells AI bill capture (vendor claim).21

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per vendor invoice coded, and errors caught before payment.

  8. Safety observations and protective gear flags: useful, but checked

    Construction had 1,034 private-industry deaths in 2024, with falls a leading cause.24 Detection is uneven: one 2026 model scored below 0.40 on goggles and boots.8

    Payoff to measure: Hazards flagged and closed each week, with false alarms counted.

Where does AI go wrong at contractors?

AI goes wrong most when nobody checks a confident answer, when pilots add steps crews skip, and when uploads or extra logins put drawings and systems at risk.7,20,49

  • AI goes wrong most when nobody checks a confident answer: general chatbots misread contract clauses and miss coordination problems.7 Have the project manager check every flag against the document itself.
  • Pilots fade when the tool adds steps. Pilot one job on one live project, count hours before and after, and drop any step crews skip.47
  • Fake change-of-bank emails hit subs and suppliers; the FBI logged $3.05B in business email compromise losses in 2025.48 Never change bank details without a call-back to a known number.
  • Ransomware hit 131 construction firms in early 2026, up 44% on a year earlier.49 Each connected AI tool is one more login to lock down.
  • Uploading owner drawings to tools that train on them can breach the architect's license.20 Read the training terms first; Bluebeam's AI beta terms, for one, allow training on your files.30
  • AI-written scope sheets sent to subs unedited. Keep a person editing anything that goes out, since AI output can't stand in for professional judgment, and keep juniors doing the work themselves.5,20

Questions contractors ask about AI

How is AI used in construction?

Mostly for office paperwork: 45% of AGC contractors surveyed use it there, and 23% in estimating.1 Field use is rare; a Census survey found 0.7% of construction firms using AI in production work.2

What is the best AI tool for a construction company?

Usually the one already inside your project platform: Procore, Autodesk, Fieldwire and Bluebeam all ship AI features now.3,17,18,25 Add a separate tool only for a job your platform can't do.

Has any AI construction software survived a pilot?

Not often: MIT research found most generative AI pilots show no profit-and-loss impact.47

Can AI review submittals?

Yes, as a first pass against the spec, with a person signing off; Procore and Buildertrend sell this (vendor claims).16,21

Is it safe to upload project documents to AI?

Only to tools whose terms rule out training on your files, as Fieldwire's do without your permission.18 Raken keeps anonymized training rights, and owner drawings may carry license limits.27,20

Will AI replace construction workers, project managers or estimators?

No sign of it: BLS projects construction manager jobs to grow 9% from 2025 to 2035.50 In an AGC survey, 87% of respondents at construction firms had open craft jobs, and BLS counted 298,000 construction openings in September 2026.51,52 Estimator jobs may dip 3%, yet about 17,600 open each year.14

Should a contractor buy AI or build it?

Buy: Buy first: switch on what your project platform already includes, then add one narrow tool.3,35 Most enterprises now buy AI: US enterprise decision-makers say 76% of use cases are bought, up from 53% in 2024.53

Build: Build only the glue that is your edge, such as your RFI template, spec library and approval step. MIT found bought or partnered tools succeed about 67% of the time; internal builds about a third as often.47

When should a contractor pick something else?

Pick something else when your platform already has the feature, the job is quantity takeoff or vendor invoices, or the work is sealed design.3,21 Dedicated takeoff and invoice tools fit those jobs better, and sealed design belongs to the engineer or architect of record.5,13

  • Pick something else when your platform already has the feature: turn on Procore's agents, Autodesk Assistant or Fieldwire Field Intelligence first.3,17,18
  • The job is quantity takeoff: use a dedicated takeoff tool from our estimating guide. General chat models test weak even on construction contract questions.7,38
  • The work is sealed design: the engineer or architect of record owns it, not the contractor's AI.5,13
  • The job is vendor invoices: an accounts-payable capture tool, such as Buildertrend Bill Capture, fits better; see our invoice guide.21
