AI in banking: what are community banks actually using it for?

Bottom line
  • Generative AI is spreading fast at banks and credit unions, but few run AI across the whole institution.1,2
  • Fraud detection leads AI use at community banks; credit unions also use AI to help contact-center staff.2,3
  • Your core provider is the gate: three in four banks need it to reach their own data.4,5
  • The April 2026 model risk guidance mostly targets banks over $30B and leaves out generative AI.6,7
  • Fair lending still applies: you owe specific denial reasons, and some states kept disparate impact.8,9

What is AI in community banking, and what does it change?

AI in banking, at a community bank or credit union, mostly sorts, drafts and answers: fraud alerts, credit memos and staff questions.2,3,10 People still decide, and you stay responsible for AI you buy from vendors.11,12 Getting your data out of the core makes or breaks it.4,5

Where does your team stay in charge?

  • Your people keep every credit decision, every BSA filing and the member conversation, and management owns oversight of every AI vendor.11,12
  • Credit officers keep each credit decision and sign every memo AI drafts; TD sends its AI drafts to expert review.10 The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) rates AI risk partly by whether a person approves each output.12,13
  • Your Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) officer decides what gets filed; AI only sorts alerts first, as Fiserv's triage agent does (vendor).14 Most alerts are false positives, so that sorting is where analyst time goes.15
  • Member-service staff keep the conversation; AI suggests answers from your own procedures.3 In a 5,172-agent study, AI help raised issues resolved per hour about 15%, most for newer staff.16
  • Management owns vendor oversight: using a vendor does not reduce your responsibility.11,17 Census data shows 66% of firms where AI changed tasks use it only to help staff; 2% report employment decreases.18

Where does AI pay first at a community bank or credit union?

  1. Fraud and anti-money-laundering (AML) alert triage

    Of community banks using AI, 77% use it for fraud detection and security.2 About 90-95% of anti-money-laundering alerts are false positives, so analysts spend most of their time clearing noise.15

    Payoff to measure: Analyst minutes per alert, and the share of alerts closed as false positives.

  2. Commercial credit spreading and memo drafts

    At TD, an AI tool cut first-draft credit memos from up to 3 days to under 30 minutes.10 Experts passed 95% of drafts on 220 deals; TD is a large bank, so treat that as a ceiling.10

    Payoff to measure: Hours from complete financials to a signed credit memo.

  3. Staff knowledge search and contact-center agent assist

    Among credit unions using AI in the contact center, 80% run generative AI agent assist.3 In a large published study, AI help raised issues resolved per hour about 15%.16

    Payoff to measure: Handle time and first-contact resolution, checked against a weekly sample of answers.

  4. Loan files and back-office documents

    APQC's median cost to process one invoice is $6.00, a fair yardstick for any paper-heavy step.19 Machine reading costs $1.50 per 1,000 pages on AWS Textract's price list.20

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per loan file or exception cleared, and re-keying errors caught.

  5. Consumer credit decisioning

    Two in three credit unions plan AI credit decisioning, per America's Credit Unions.21 It takes the most work: fair lending tests and specific reasons for every denial.8,22

    Payoff to measure: Share of applications auto-decided, with approval and delinquency rates by group.

  6. Member chatbots and voice AI

    One credit union told Congress its voice AI handles over 60% of business-hours calls.23 The CFPB found chatbots giving wrong answers and trapping people in loops.24

    Payoff to measure: Share of calls and chats resolved without a hand-off, and complaints about the bot.

  7. Share of wallet and next products for existing customers

    In a McKinsey study, relationship managers with analytics tools grew portfolios 9%, against 5% for a control group.25 Many banks have not yet linked accounts into households, which comes first.26

    Payoff to measure: Products per household, and new accounts opened by existing customers.

  8. CECL (current expected credit loss) estimates and loan monitoring: watch, don't lead

    The Fed's free SCALE tool already serves banks under $1B, in a spreadsheet.27 We found no independent evidence that machine learning beats it at this size.

    Payoff to measure: Hours per quarter spent on the allowance estimate.

Where does AI go wrong at community banks and credit unions?

  • It goes wrong through member data pasted into personal AI, wrong chatbot answers, vendor models without denial reasons and core contracts that block your data.39,24,8,5 Each fix is below.
  • Staff paste member data into personal ChatGPT or Gemini accounts, as ICBA warns.39 Approve one business tool and write down that member data stays out of anything else, as CU*Answers does.57
  • Chatbots give wrong answers or trap members in loops, which the CFPB flagged.24 Keep a fast path to a person, and test fees and rates, which the OCC names as hallucination risks.12
  • A vendor's credit model can't give the specific reasons a denial needs.8,58 Before you sign, have the vendor show the reasons its model gives on a sample of your own denials.
  • Treating the April 2026 Reg B rule as the end of fair lending risk.22 State law can still reach disparate impact, so keep testing outcomes by group.9,56
  • Core contracts that block integration or charge for your own data.5,41 Read the contract before you pick an AI vendor, and cite the 2026 joint statement at renewal.
  • Deepfake video and voice used to open accounts or fool call centers, per Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network.59 Add a callback or second check before changing account details or moving money.

Questions bankers ask about AI

Where should a community bank or credit union start with AI?

Most start with drafting tools and the fraud tools their vendors already sell.4,2 Pilot one workflow on one team or branch, with a before-and-after number such as analyst minutes per alert.

Can AI make credit decisions at a community bank or credit union?

Yes, but you owe specific reasons for every denial and must keep application records for 25 months, 12 for business credit.8,54 The OCC also rescinded its 1997 credit scoring exam guidance in April 2026.31

What does NCUA expect from credit unions using AI?

NCUA has no AI-specific rules; its existing rules and vendor due diligence letters apply.30 It can't examine your vendors directly, so that work falls on you.33

Did the April 2026 Reg B rule end fair lending risk for AI models?

No. Federal disparate impact is gone, but intentional discrimination, the Fair Housing Act and state law still apply.22,55,9 Adverse action notices are unchanged.54

What does AI cost a community bank or credit union?

Drafting tools run about $20 to $21 per user a month, prices read 26 Nov 2026.60,61 Alkami lists data and marketing tiers from $23K a year (vendor).62 Core providers' AI, such as Fiserv's credit pricing, isn't published, so get a scoped quote.14

Will examiners criticize us for using AI?

Not for using AI by itself: the OCC says its AI framework sets no new expectations and can be tailored to community banks.12,49 The FDIC says not following its model guidance does not, by itself, bring criticism.7

Should you buy AI from your core and vendors, or build it?

Buy: Mostly buy, when your core or an established vendor already sells the job, such as fraud triage or memo drafting.14,44 ICBA told the OCC that partnering with a core provider is often the only practical path for community banks.46 Most enterprise AI is now bought: 76% of use cases, up from 53% in 2024.63

Build: Build only thin layers where your own procedures are the edge, such as a staff knowledge base. MIT research found bought tools succeed about 67% of the time; internal builds about a third as often.64,63 For credit models, buy and validate; you still owe specific denial reasons.8

When should you pick something else?

  • When your core already sells the AI job: check Fiserv agentOS, Jack Henry's Google Cloud tools or FIS before adding a separate vendor.14,44,46
  • Staff only need drafting and summaries: Microsoft 365 Copilot ($21 per user a month) or ChatGPT Business ($20), prices read 26 Nov 2026.60,61 Copilot is what 95% of AI-using banks report.4
  • You are under $1B and need a CECL estimate: the Fed's free SCALE tool runs in a spreadsheet.27
  • Consumer credit decisions: buy from a specialist such as Zest AI, which claims 25% higher approvals, then validate it yourself.65,8
