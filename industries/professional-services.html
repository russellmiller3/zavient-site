What are professional services firms actually using AI for?

Bottom line
  • Professional services firms are among the heaviest AI users: 39.1% in mid-2026, against 21.5% of all businesses.1,2
  • The main uses are research, summaries, meeting notes and proposals: drafting work a professional reviews before it goes out.3,4
  • Saving time is common, turning it into revenue is not: in a vendor survey 84.8% save time, 29.9% earn more.5,3
  • On hourly contracts a saved hour shrinks the bill unless resold; on fixed fees it becomes margin or capacity.6,7
  • Licensed engineers keep the seal: boards say they must check, document and be able to reproduce AI output.8,9

What is AI for professional services firms, and what does it change?

AI for professional services firms means tools that draft, summarize and search the written work that fills a professional's week.3,4 It makes people faster and better on tasks it handles well, and worse on others.14,17 Whether saved hours become revenue decides the payoff.5

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your professionals stay in charge of every judgment and signature: AI drafts, and the licensee, engagement lead or recruiter decides.8,10

  • Licensed engineers keep responsible charge: the engineering licensing council (NCEES) says they validate AI output and document methods.8,9 American Institute of Architects (AIA) Rule 4.102 bars sealing documents not prepared under your responsible control.10
  • The engagement lead, partner or principal reviews every client-facing output before it leaves.14 AI-invented citations reached government clients in published cases in Australia and Canada.13,18
  • Your COO or managing partner decides where saved hours go: resold, moved to fixed fee or passed on.16,7 Only 29.9% of firms say AI raises revenue so far (vendor-sponsored survey).5
  • Recruiters keep the shortlist and the hiring call.19 Illinois requires notice when AI is used in employment decisions, and NYC requires a bias audit first.12,20

Where does AI pay first at a professional services firm?

AI pays first on the unbilled writing around client work: proposals, notes and status reports, then research summaries and time capture.11,4

  1. Proposals and request for proposal (RFP) responses

    Proposals eat unbilled senior time, and Bentley found proposal-style work in AI production at 86.4% of 103 design and engineering firms.11,21 A Loopio vendor survey puts one RFP at 25 hours, down from 30.22

    Payoff to measure: Senior hours per proposal, and win rate, before and after.

  2. Meeting notes, status reports and client updates

    In a vendor-sponsored study, 28% of client-facing staff spend 30% or more of their week on admin.23 Among US agencies, 74% already use AI to summarize documents.4

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per status report, and the edits the engagement lead makes before sending.

  3. Research and document summaries

    In a field study of 758 consultants, AI users finished 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, on tasks AI handles well.14,24 Professionals took 40% less time on writing tasks in a Science study.17

    Payoff to measure: Hours from question to checked summary, with every source verified by a person.

  4. Time capture, timesheets and invoicing

    Missed time is lost revenue on hourly work, and 44% of professionals in a vendor survey lose 4+ hours a week to admin.25,23 Productive fills timesheets from calendar events, and BigTime sells an AI assistant (vendor features).26,15

    Payoff to measure: Hours logged per person each week, and time written off at billing.

  5. Searching firm knowledge: past proposals, specs and deliverables

    Every firm holds years of past work that is hard to find, and engineering firms name data quality as the first barrier to AI.27,11 We found no sound payoff figure, so measure your own.

    Payoff to measure: Minutes to find a past proposal or spec, sampled before and after.

  6. First drafts of routine reports, specs, briefs and code

    At Procter & Gamble, one professional with AI matched a two-person team without it.28 Developers with AI completed 26.08% more tasks; a 2025 trial found experienced developers slower, but its authors now think they are likely faster.29,30

    Payoff to measure: Reviewer edit time per draft, and defects found after delivery.

  7. Staffing and search: screening and interview scheduling

    In Bullhorn's vendor survey, 46% of recruitment firms say AI halved screening time, but only 10% use it across the workflow.19 Hiring-AI laws apply, so read the rules below first.12,20

    Payoff to measure: Days from job order to shortlist, with each adverse-impact check logged.

Where does a saved hour go under each kind of contract?

A saved hour goes to the client on hourly work and to the firm on fixed-fee work, unless you change the contract.7,16 Hourly billing is sticky: 78% of tax firms still bill by the hour.16

Where does AI go wrong at professional services firms?

AI goes wrong when unchecked output reaches a client: invented citations, wrong answers on hard tasks, and sloppy drafts colleagues must fix.13,14

  • Invented citations reached clients: an Australian government report was partly refunded, and Newfoundland and Labrador now requires AI-use disclosure in bids.13,48,18 Have a person check every source before delivery.
  • On a task outside AI's range, consultants using it were 19 points less likely to be right.14,24 Test AI on your own past work before trusting it on a new kind of task.
  • Sloppy AI drafts pass the work to colleagues: 40% of US desk workers got one in a month, at about two hours each to fix.49,50 Make the sender check the draft, not the receiver.
  • Client data in the wrong tool: an engineering ethics board found putting client data into AI without permission partly unethical.41,39 Get client consent, and use a business plan with training switched off.
  • Saving time without capturing it: only 18% of professionals say their organization tracks AI return.3,5 Set the pricing rule for saved hours before rollout, not after.

Questions firm leaders ask about AI

Will AI cut our billable hours and revenue?

Only on hourly work where saved hours aren't resold: one hour a week per person at a 100-person firm puts $573,000-$700,000 of revenue at stake.6 Professionals expect AI to free about five hours a week, which multiplies that.51 Fixed fees turn the same hours into margin.7

Are clients asking for AI discounts?

Yes, some are: 42% of leaders at large firms say clients now question pricing.35 In a self-selected poll of 218 agency owners, 53% had a client ask for a lower price because of AI.52

Do we have to tell clients we use AI?

Not under any general US law we found, but contracts, engineering ethics and public bids can require it.41,18 An engineering ethics board says get permission before client data goes into AI.41

Can an engineer seal AI-generated work?

Only if the engineer kept responsible charge, checked the output and documented the method.8 North Carolina adds that the engineer must be able to reproduce it independently.9

Will AI replace junior staff?

No study we found shows that; the evidence so far shows tasks changing, as one professional with AI matched a two-person team.28,27 Keep juniors drafting some work themselves, so they still learn it.

Which AI tools do mid-sized firms actually use?

Mostly one general assistant plus the AI features inside tools they already own.15,26 General plans list at $20-$25 a user a month, read 26 Nov 2026.36,37

Should a professional services firm buy AI or build it?

Buy: Buy first: switch on the AI in your timesheet and proposal tools for one practice, then add one general assistant.36,15,53 Most companies buy: 76% of enterprise AI use cases are bought, not built.54

Build: Build or connect only where a job crosses your systems, such as proposal library to customer database to billing.55,27 MIT found partnered tools reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds, in a small sample.55

When should a firm pick something else?

Pick something else when software you already own does the job, or when the contract, not the tool, is the problem.15,16

  • Your timesheet or project tool already has AI: turn on BigTime's AI Assistant or Productive's AI time tracking first (vendor features).15,26
  • Proposals are the bottleneck: dedicated RFP software such as Loopio ships generative AI in its base tier, priced by quote.53,22
  • Hourly contracts eat your savings: change the contract before the tool, moving suitable scopes to fixed fee.7,16
  • You only need a chat assistant with admin controls: a business plan such as ChatGPT Business, Copilot or Claude Team fits.36,37
