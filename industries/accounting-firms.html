What is AI for accounting and CPA firms, and what does it change?

AI in a certified public accountant (CPA) firm reads client documents, drafts research and workpapers, and flags exceptions; accountants review and sign.1,9 In a 2026 field study, accountants using more AI served more clients and closed books faster.10 Review discipline and client-data handling make or break it.1,21

Where does your team stay in charge?

People keep every judgment that carries a signature: the audit opinion, the return, the advice and the decision to share client data.1,3

  • The engagement partner signs the audit opinion and must understand and approve any AI tool used.3,4 The American Institute of CPAs (AICPA) says the auditor stays responsible for the audit and the opinion.3
  • Preparers and reviewers check every AI-filled field and citation before a return or memo goes out, under Circular 230 due diligence.2,1 The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) warns that blind reliance on AI output may be unreasonable.1
  • The managing partner owns the firm's AI procedures and tool approvals, under Circular 230 section 10.36 and the quality standard SQMS 1.1,32 The IRS Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) lists training, secure data handling and vendor contracts.1
  • A tax partner decides client notice and consent before client data reaches an AI vendor.5,23 The AICPA says AI should support, not supplant, professional judgment.7

Where does AI pay first at a CPA firm?

AI pays first in client document intake and tax research, then in audit first-pass review and month-end close for client accounting work.11,9,10 The intake and research time savings are vendor claims, so measure your own.18

  1. Client document intake and chasing

    In a 2024 survey reported by a vendor, 49% of 240 professionals said gathering documents takes 1-2 days per engagement.17 SurePrep's own study claims 1.59 fewer hours per return (vendor claim).18

    Payoff to measure: Measure your own: days from first upload to ready-for-prep, and preparer hours per return.

  2. Tax research and memo drafting

    Firms using AI tax research rose to 60% from 33% in a year, and 84% say it saves time (vendor survey).11 Fake citations are the risk; the IRS says to check citations and read the cases.1,39

    Payoff to measure: Research hours per question and the share of citations that pass a read-through, not the vendor's time claim.

  3. Audit first-pass review: extraction, vouching and tie-out

    One 11-person firm cut first-pass document review from about a week to 2-3 hours with a closed AI system, by its own account.9 The AICPA's October 2026 practice aid covers AI in the audit and its risks.3

    Payoff to measure: Fieldwork hours per engagement and review notes per file.

  4. Client accounting services: bank feeds, reconciliation and close

    A 2026 field study of 79 small-business clients tied heavier AI use to about 19% more clients a week and a close 7.5 days faster.10 Accountants stepped in when the AI's confidence was low, and time moved to client communication and quality checks.10

    Payoff to measure: Days to close per client, clients per accountant and low-confidence items per month.

  5. Workpaper review assist, with AI as the first reviewer

    At one firm in a Journal of Accountancy report, AI is the mandatory first reviewer before partner review.9 Effort is higher: it needs a firm-approved tool, and Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) staff offer no safe harbor for AI.37

    Payoff to measure: Review notes per engagement and partner review hours.

  6. Client emails, engagement letters and practice admin

    Karbon survey users report saving 60 minutes a day each (self-reported vendor survey, 593 respondents).12 Karbon and TaxDome already build AI into practice management (vendor pages).50,51

    Payoff to measure: Response time to client emails and admin hours per week.

  7. Advisory and planning drafts

    Advisory is the top AI tax research use, at 44%, and 69% expect value-based, hybrid or fixed fees (vendor survey).11 The partner's judgment carries the advice, so the payoff is harder to measure.7

    Payoff to measure: Advisory hours sold per partner and turnaround on planning memos.

Which rules apply to AI at a CPA firm?

No law bans AI in a CPA firm, but tax privacy law, Circular 230, the AICPA code and quality standards all govern how you use it.1,5 None of this is legal advice.

Where does AI go wrong at CPA firms?

AI goes wrong at CPA firms when nobody reads its output closely: fake citations, client data in consumer tools and unchecked client reports are the documented failures.39,1

  • Fake citations reach filings: a 2026 Tax Court filing cited three AI-invented cases, and a Minnesota court referred a county's fake citations for discipline.39,40 Read every cited case before anything is filed, as the IRS directs.1
  • Client data pasted into a consumer AI tool risks unauthorized disclosure under Sections 7216 and 6713.1,21 Use an enterprise tool under contract with no training on your data, and list it in your WISP.29,5
  • Vendors are asking firms to sell data for AI training, and redaction does not make client information nonconfidential.6 Get specific written client consent and run a separate Section 7216 check.6,23
  • A Big Four member firm refunded part of an A$440,000 Australian government report with AI errors, and was later barred over another.41,42 Have a named reviewer read every AI-drafted deliverable before it goes out.1
  • Few firms write it down: only 21% have an AI policy or strategy, and 18% track AI returns (vendor surveys).12,13 Write a one-page policy, train staff, and record two measures for a month before any pilot starts.1
  • Billing for time AI saved may breach Circular 230 section 10.27, though an IRS advisory council asked OPR to revise that guidance.1,26 Move AI-heavy work to fixed or value fees, as 69% of surveyed accountants expect.11

Questions CPA firm leaders ask about AI

Will AI replace CPAs?

No sign of it yet: the Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 5% job growth for accountants and auditors from 2025 to 2035.20 About 115,300 openings are expected each year, and a 2026 field study found AI moved time from data entry to client work.20,10 The largest firms we checked still post mostly tax and audit roles.54

Can I put client tax data into ChatGPT?

Not into a consumer tool: the IRS says to use only secure, enterprise-approved AI, and Section 7216 penalties apply to unauthorized disclosure.1,21 The AICPA code also requires a vendor confidentiality contract or the client's specific consent.5

Do I need taxpayer consent before return data goes to an AI vendor?

Not settled: the IRS has not said whether a general AI vendor is an auxiliary service provider that needs no consent.23,26 An IRS advisory council asked for guidance on Section 7216 in November 2026.27,28 Check with your own tax counsel before return data reaches the vendor.

Do I have to tell clients I use AI?

Yes, usually, if the tool receives confidential client information: the AICPA code says to inform clients first, preferably in writing.5 Pure software hosting is exempt and whether AI counts is unsettled, so the AICPA-sponsored insurer suggests disclosing anyway.5,8

Which AI tools do accounting firms actually use?

Mostly AI tax research, document scan-and-fill, audit extraction tools and AI built into practice management software.11,9,12 The tools table above lists prices read on 26 Nov 2026.

Can we bill clients for the time AI saved?

No, not as if staff did the work by hand: OPR says billing for time not spent may violate Circular 230 section 10.27.1 Most surveyed accountants expect value-based, hybrid or fixed fees instead (vendor survey).11

Should a CPA firm buy AI or build its own?

Buy first: the AI inside your tax, audit and practice software covers most work, and you build only where your own process is the edge.46,43

Buy: Buy for general ledger, practice management and tax prep, and switch on the AI already inside those tools.46,47 Most enterprises now buy: 76% of AI use cases in 2025, up from 53%.43 Bought tools reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds in an MIT study.44,45

Build: Build only a thin layer that reflects how your firm works, such as your review checklist or research memo template.47 CPA.com warns that builds bring shadow IT, cross-client data leakage and key-person risk.47

When should a CPA firm pick something else?

Pick something else when your software already has the feature, the step never changes, or the volume is too low to repay the review.19,53

  • Your tax or audit software already has it: turn on the AI in SurePrep, Caseware or your practice management tool first.19,53
  • The step never changes: use rules-based automation in Karbon or TaxDome, or a data tool such as Alteryx or Power BI.50,51 Power BI appeared in 62 of the large-firm posts we counted.54
  • Seasonal volume is the problem: outsourcing can fit, but offshore work needs Section 7216 consent and an AICPA client notice.23,24,5
  • The task takes an hour a month: skip AI for now, because policy, training and review would cost more than it saves.1,12
