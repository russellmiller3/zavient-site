AI for private equity: what are PE-backed companies using it for?

Bottom line
  • Most portfolio companies are early, PE leaders say: about a third use AI daily, and many are still piloting.1,2

What is AI in PE-backed companies, and what does it change?

AI in a PE-backed company is software that drafts, sorts, predicts or answers inside daily work, from calls to the close.11,12 It drives the earnings growth sponsors now need from operations, not higher sale multiples.10 Clean data and an owner tied to the value creation plan make or break it.6,4

Where does your team stay in charge?

People keep the decisions: sales leaders approve prices, service managers own dispatch, the controller signs the close, and the COO decides what goes live.8,13,5

  • Sales leaders approve price changes and AI-drafted offers before a customer sees them. Three in four asset management and PE leaders require human validation of AI agent output, and 51% aren't yet comfortable with autonomous agents.8,9
  • Your service manager owns the dispatch board: at IFS, dispatch agents propose schedule changes and a person reviews them before schedules update.13 Granite Comfort's after-hours calls run with humans in the loop, per its vendor.14
  • Your controller signs the close and the board pack, even when AI drafts them. Operating partners mostly want supervised autonomy (41%) or autonomy only on low-stakes tasks (38%).5
  • Your COO owns the list of AI uses and decides what goes live. The free AI Risk Management Framework from NIST, the federal standards body, gives the steps: govern, map, measure, manage.16
  • HR keeps every hiring decision. From 1 Jan 2027, California gives people access to and an opt-out from automated tools in significant decisions, and Colorado's new law starts.17,18

Where does AI pay first in a PE-backed company?

AI pays first in revenue work: pricing, dormant-account follow-up and missed-call booking, then procurement and the finance close.1,3,14

  1. Pricing, quoting and margin

    Pricing and margin optimization is a high AI revenue priority for 47% of the PE leaders FTI surveyed.1 Among 400 US PE fund managers and operating partners BDO surveyed, pricing gave the strongest result for 15%, and 16% expect it to next year.21

    Payoff to measure: Price realization and gross margin per order, plus quote turnaround time.

  2. Dormant accounts and sales follow-up

    At Apollo's Univar Solutions, a chemical distributor, an AI sales agent re-engaged dormant accounts with 30% engagement in a pilot.3 At Blackstone's Renaissance Learning, a lead model doubled average order value in a pilot.19

    Payoff to measure: Accounts reactivated, and pipeline won from the dormant list.

  3. Missed calls and booking, for service businesses

    Per its vendor, AI books over half of bookable calls at Granite Comfort, a heating and cooling platform, and its pilot brand grew revenue 20%.14,15 Customer support is among the most common portfolio company uses in L.E.K.'s 2026 survey.11

    Payoff to measure: Answered-call rate, booked jobs and after-hours revenue.

  4. Procurement and spend

    Procurement spend and technology costs are the top AI cost priorities, each rated high by 48% of PE leaders.1 Apollo found price gaps on the same items across companies, and one company cut procurement cost over 65% with contract AI.22,3

    Payoff to measure: Spend under contract, duplicate vendors and savings at renewal.

  5. Month-end close, financial planning and the board pack

    Among PE firm and portfolio company executives, financial planning and analysis (FP&A) is the most common AI use, named by 55%.12 Of operating partners, 83% report at least one AI agent live or in pilot in portfolio finance work, led by loan covenant checks at 58%.5

    Payoff to measure: Days to close, hours per board pack, and variances flagged before the board meets.

  6. Answers for field technicians

    At Apollo's Barnes Group, an industrial firm, AI search of product specs gave technicians fast answers and a 5x return in year one.3 IFS, an EQT-backed software firm, sells dispatch agents that schedule 300,000+ technicians, with a person reviewing replans.13

    Payoff to measure: Time to answer a technician's question, and first-time fix rate.

  7. Add-on acquisitions: diligence and integration

    Of UK private equity and venture capital firms surveyed, 92% use AI in due diligence.23 FTI-Andersch found 27% use it for financial due diligence and 13% for sale preparation.24

    Payoff to measure: Weeks from closing an add-on to one set of books, customers and price lists.

  8. Value creation plan tracking

    Bain advises starting from the value creation plan and aiming AI at three to five big uses.4 Only 38% of operating partners measure AI's revenue or margin impact.5

    Payoff to measure: Share of AI uses with a measured revenue or cost number.

Where does AI go wrong in PE-backed companies?

AI goes wrong when nobody measures it, pilots never reach the profit and loss, data is dirty, or sale materials overstate it.5,48,6,34

  • No measured number, so no proof: only 38% of operating partners measure revenue or margin impact.5 Set one revenue or cost number per use before the pilot starts.
  • Pilots that never reach the profit and loss (P&L): MIT researchers found 95% of generative AI pilots delivered little or no measurable P&L impact.48 Tie each pilot to the value creation plan, as Bain advises, then scale it or stop it.4
  • Dirty data stalls AI: mid-market firms name data quality as the top barrier, and asset management and PE leaders name data readiness.6,7 Clean customer, item and price data before buying any AI tool.
  • Scaling without a playbook: 41% of operating partners say their firm does it, and 63% of portfolio companies have informal guidance only.5 Write a one-page AI policy and a list of uses, each with an owner.16
  • AI washing in the exit story: the SEC and FTC have acted on false AI claims.34,37 Claim only what you measured, and keep the evidence for buyer diligence.38
  • Staff left behind: 85% of mid-market leaders say executives are keener on AI than their staff.49 Train the people who use it, and frame it as help with their work.

Questions operators ask about AI in PE-backed companies

Do private equity firms hire consultants for AI?

Yes: half of UK private capital firms surveyed bring outside AI specialists into their portfolio companies.23 In RSM's survey of middle market executives, 52% say their firm uses outside consultants or advisers now and 37% plan to within a year.49

How is AI being used in private equity?

UK PE and VC firms use AI mostly on deal work: 92% in due diligence, 84% in sourcing and 78% in monitoring and investor reporting.23 Among PE firm and portfolio company executives, FP&A is the most common AI use, at 55%.12 Portfolio companies also aim AI at pricing, sales and customer support.1,11

Is AI actually paying off in portfolio companies?

Only partly so far: 19% of GPs see positive net returns now, and 40% expect them within a year.26 Bain and StepStone found 39% of GPs expect no material financial impact in 2026.25

Where should a portfolio company start with AI?

Start from the value creation plan and pick three to five high-value uses, revenue as well as cost.4,1 Give each one owner and one number to move before the pilot starts.5

Does the SEC regulate AI in private equity?

Not with an AI-specific rule: the SEC withdrew its predictive data analytics proposal in June 2025.33 It still examines AI use, and has settled false AI claims with advisers.39,34

How much are PE firms spending on AI in portfolio companies?

More each year: 80% of GPs say portfolio AI spending rose last year, and 39% raised it by 25% to 49%.26 Among mid-market firms, 84% expect AI spending to rise.6

Should a PE-backed company buy AI or build it?

Buy for standard jobs such as call answering, payables and contract review.47 Build only where your own data is the edge, such as pricing.4

Buy: Buy for standard work: 76% of enterprise AI uses are now bought, up from 53%.47 Granite Comfort bought a call-booking product, and Apollo shares vendors across its companies.14,3 MIT researchers found partnered or bought tools reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds.50

Build: Build, or configure on top of what you own, where your data is the edge: pricing from order history, dormant-account lists, product spec libraries.3 Bain says the wins come from a few uses tied to the value creation plan, with data readiness checked first.4

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your sponsor has a central AI team, the job is one standard workflow, you need a part-time leader, or data is the blocker.20,47,6

  • Your sponsor runs a central AI team, as Blackstone, Hg and Apollo do: use it first.20,28,3
  • One standard workflow, such as payables or call answering: buy the feature in your enterprise resource planning (ERP) system or a point tool.47
  • You need ongoing AI leadership, not a project: consider a fractional chief AI officer. Only 9% of sponsors have an AI center of excellence, per operating partners surveyed.5
  • Data is the blocker: clean master data in your ERP or business intelligence tool before any AI.6
