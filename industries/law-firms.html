What is AI for law firms, and what does it change?

AI for law firms is software that reads, searches and drafts: discovery review, summaries, research and first drafts.3 What changes is where lawyer hours go: RAND found computer-assisted review could cut attorney review hours by about three-quarters.5 A lawyer still checks each citation and signs, as ABA Opinion 512 expects.10,17

Where does your team stay in charge?

Lawyers keep the judgment and the signature: the signing lawyer checks every citation and owns the filing, and managing partners set the AI policy.19,20,10 The e-discovery lead samples AI document review, and the risk partner vets vendor terms before client data goes in.6,23,10

  • The signing lawyer checks every citation and owns the filing. In a 2025 Wyoming case against Walmart, two lawyers who only signed a brief with fake cases were fined $1,000 each.19,20
  • Managing partners set the AI policy and training, as ABA Opinion 512 and California's 2026 guidance expect.10,21 A federal court fined a supervising lawyer $1,001 in 2026 for not supervising a junior colleague's AI brief.22
  • The litigation support or e-discovery lead checks AI document review by sampling. Courts expect a review process with quality-control testing.6,23
  • The risk partner or general counsel reads vendor terms before client data goes in. The client gives informed consent before self-learning tools see their data, and boilerplate doesn't count.10,12

Where does AI pay first in a law firm?

AI pays first in document review for discovery, which was about 73% of e-discovery production cost, and courts have accepted it since 2012.5,6 Next come summaries of depositions and records, first-pass research with a cite-check, and drafting.3,18

  1. Document review in discovery

    Review was about 73% of e-discovery production cost in RAND's study of 57 large cases.5 Courts have accepted technology-assisted review (TAR) since 2012, if you check its quality by sampling.6,24

    Payoff to measure: Reviewer hours per 1,000 documents, and the share of relevant documents found in a checked sample.

  2. Summaries of depositions, productions and medical records

    Among lawyers who use generative AI, 73% use it to summarise documents.3 Vendors claim big savings, such as EvenUp's 9+ hours per case; those are their own figures.25,26

    Payoff to measure: Paralegal and associate hours per summary, and days to turnaround.

  3. First-pass legal research and memos

    Research is the top legal AI use, at 80% of users.3 It pays only with a cite-check: paid legal research tools still invented answers 17% to 33% of the time.18

    Payoff to measure: Hours to a first usable memo, and citation errors caught per memo.

  4. Drafting letters and contracts

    Among legal AI users, 55% draft correspondence with it and 49% draft contracts.3 A lawyer edits and sends every draft.10

    Payoff to measure: Partner edit time per draft.

  5. Time capture and billing prep

    The average lawyer captured about 3 billable hours in an 8-hour day in 2025.27 Clio says firms on its AI tools log 6.5% more billable hours; that is the vendor's own data.28

    Payoff to measure: Billable hours captured per lawyer per day, and days from work done to invoice.

  6. Intake and client updates

    Of firms using AI, 65% say it helped them answer clients faster, per Clio's survey.29 Florida's ethics opinion says an intake chatbot must disclose it is AI, not a lawyer.30

    Payoff to measure: Hours to answer a new inquiry, and the share of inquiries that become clients.

Which ethics opinions and court rules apply?

ABA Formal Opinion 512 sets the baseline duties and Rule 11 makes every signer check the law. California, New York, Florida, Texas and D.C. add their own.10,20,11,49,30,51,52 Some judges also require you to disclose AI use, so check each court's rules before you file.53

Where does AI go wrong for law firms?

AI goes wrong for law firms through fake citations in filings, client data in consumer accounts, unchecked co-counsel, and billing hours AI saved.7,12,9,50 Courts have flagged AI-invented citations in hundreds of decisions involving lawyers.7

  • Fake citations in filings: courts have flagged AI-invented citations in hundreds of decisions involving lawyers.7 Open every cited case in a primary source before you file.20
  • Big firms aren't immune: an Alabama federal court disqualified three Butler Snow lawyers in 2025.8,54 It released the firm, which had acted reasonably to prevent misuse, so write your policy down.8
  • Co-counsel you didn't ask: in a 2025 case against State Farm, two firms paid $31,100 after one never asked whether co-counsel used AI.9 Ask co-counsel and staff, and put it in work-sharing terms.9,22
  • Client data in a consumer account: a New York federal court held a client's consumer AI chats were not privileged in 2026.12,55 Use a business tier with written no-training and retention terms.41,14
  • Billing the hours AI saved: bill actual time, including review, but not time learning the tool.46,50,56 A quarter of firms already report fewer billable hours on some tasks, so consider flat fees there.57
  • No baseline, so no proof: only 18% of professionals say their organization measures AI's return; record hours per task before a pilot.3,4

What do lawyers ask about AI?

Lawyers ask about client data, telling clients, billing, accuracy, cost and jobs. The short answers: use business tiers only, disclose when ABA 512 calls for it, bill actual time, and check every citation.10,46,18

Can our lawyers paste client documents into ChatGPT?

Not into a consumer ChatGPT account; use a business or enterprise tier whose terms bar training and restrict access.10,11,12 Get informed consent before self-learning tools see client data.10

Do we have to tell clients we use AI?

Not always: ABA 512 says tell clients when they ask, the engagement requires it, consent is needed, or AI affects fees or significant decisions.10 California courts will require disclosure on filings from 1 Jan 2027.11

Can we still bill hourly for work AI sped up?

Yes, but bill the time actually spent, including review, not the time AI saved.46,50 Don't bill time spent learning the tool.46

Is legal-specific AI more accurate than ChatGPT?

Not on accuracy: legal tools and ChatGPT both scored near 80% in Vals' 2025 benchmark, though legal tools cited better sources.42,43 Paid legal tools still invented answers 17% to 33% of the time in Stanford's study.18

How much does legal AI cost?

Legal AI costs $20 to $25 a seat a month for general tools, and about $100 to $499 for legal tools that publish a price.14,31,16,15 Many legal tools quote only.35,36 For 50 lawyers billed yearly, that is $12,000 a year on Claude or ChatGPT, or $78,000 on MyCase Advanced.14,31,16

Will AI replace lawyers?

No: from 2027, California bars delegating the practice of law to AI, and a lawyer must sign every filing.11,20

Should your firm buy legal AI or build it?

Buy for standard work like case law research and e-discovery, and build only a light layer on your own precedents, playbooks and clause bank.36,24,42 Bought tools succeeded about twice as often as internal builds in MIT's study.59,60

Buy: Buy when the work is standard or the data isn't yours: case law research needs licensed databases, and e-discovery platforms have court acceptance.36,24 Most enterprise AI is now bought: 76% of use cases in 2025.58 Bought tools succeeded about twice as often as internal builds in MIT's study.59,60

Build: Build a light layer when your edge is your own precedents, playbooks and clause bank. General models matched legal tools on research accuracy in Vals' benchmark.42 California's access rule favours setups where the firm controls who sees client data.11,14

When should you pick something else?

Pick keyword search for small discovery sets, the AI already in your Westlaw, Lexis, Clio or MyCase plan, or a specialist like EvenUp for injury demand letters.6,38,36,16,25

  • Small discovery sets: use keyword search and manual review, or a pay-per-gigabyte platform such as Logikcull. Courts framed AI review for large data volumes.6,38
  • You need citable authority: use the AI inside your existing Westlaw or Lexis subscription before a general chatbot. Legal tools scored higher on authoritative sources.42,36
  • You already run Clio or MyCase: switch on the AI in your current plan first. MyCase Pro includes writing and document assistants.33,16
  • Personal injury demand letters at volume: a specialist such as EvenUp or Supio may beat a build. Their outcome numbers are their own claims.25,26
