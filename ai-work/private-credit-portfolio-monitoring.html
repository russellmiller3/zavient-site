What is covenant monitoring, and what does AI change?

Covenant monitoring checks each borrower's quarterly numbers against its loan terms; AI reads the package, spreads the figures, recomputes covenants and flags drift28,33. The aim is to spot trouble early29,34. What makes or breaks it: an analyst checks every flagged number, and nobody takes the borrower's own math on trust29,36.

Who on your credit team keeps each decision?

People keep every decision: analysts check flagged numbers, the head of portfolio management moves the watchlist, Required Lenders approve waivers, and the board or valuation designee signs each mark29,33,20,11.

  • Your people keep every decision: analysts own the numbers, portfolio heads the watchlist, and the board or valuation designee each fair value mark29,33,11.
  • Credit analyst: checks every number the AI flags and reconciles its covenant math with the borrower's certificate29,36. Add-backs, costs the borrower adds back to EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization), stay the analyst's call32.
  • Head of portfolio management: decides watchlist moves and what to raise with the borrower33. Waivers and amendments need written consent from the Required Lenders, the lender majority the credit agreement names20.
  • Board or valuation designee: owns each fair value mark, and must test and oversee the methods behind it11,12. AI drafts the inputs and a change memo; it does not set the mark13.
  • Operations analyst: confirms each package arrived complete and on time28. A late or resubmitted certificate is a signal in itself31.

What do your analysts do once AI takes the first pass?

Analysts check the AI's flags, watch more loans more often, and spend the freed time on early-warning work and on borrowers whose numbers drift50,38,33.

  • Analysts watch more loans, more often. One vendor reports monitoring time per loan fell from nine hours to one, and analysts covered 12 loans each instead of 4 (vendor claim)50.
  • Analysts spend the time on early-warning work. It matters more now: covenant-lite loans, with no quarterly tests, rose from 4% to 21% of deals, 2023 to 202538,30.
  • Portfolio heads edit a memo draft instead of writing from a blank page, and spend the time with borrowers whose numbers drift33,34.
  • Among US firms where AI changed tasks, 66% use it only to help staff; 2% saw AI-related employment fall37.

Which way to build AI covenant monitoring fits your fund?

A small team fits intake and spreading, a mid-size team adds a covenant recompute, and a larger book adds early-warning flags and a memo draft28,20,38.

1. Small team: package intake and spreading with a review queue

Funds with a few analysts who re-key borrower PDFs into Excel each quarter28. Builds 2 and 3 run on its spreads, so most funds start here.

  1. Operations analystSet one folder and naming rule for every borrower package
  2. Builder, in-house or hiredConnect a document extraction service to that folder
  3. AnalystMap each borrower's lines to the fund's chart of accounts, once
  4. AnalystRun the AI fill; each cell gets a confidence score
  5. AnalystReview every low-confidence or changed cell
  6. Head of portfolio managementSign off the quarter's spreads

Cost: Extraction lists at $10 per 1,000 pages on Azure, or $0.015 a page for Amazon Textract tables (read 26 Nov 2026)43,44. Sixty borrowers, four quarters, 80 pages each: 19,200 pages, about $192 a year at Azure's rate.

2. Mid-size team: covenant recompute and headroom tracker

Funds whose agreements carry their own definitions, add-back caps and cure limits20,28. The borrower's certificate shows its math, not yours29.

  1. Credit analyst or counselEncode each agreement's definitions, add-back caps and cure limits
  2. AnalystRun the recompute on each quarter's spread
  3. AnalystReconcile every gap against the borrower's certificate
  4. AnalystLog each equity cure used against the agreement's limit
  5. Head of portfolio managementTrack headroom, the room left before each covenant breaks, and decide next steps
  6. Required LendersApprove any waiver or amendment in writing

Cost: Model fees are small: Claude Sonnet 5.5 lists $2 per million tokens (word pieces) read and $10 written (read 26 Nov 2026)45. Encoding each agreement's definitions is the real cost, in analyst and counsel time.

3. Larger book: early-warning flags and a memo draft

Funds with many names, where covenant-lite deals leave fewer tripwires38. Early signals matter most when there is no covenant to trip.

  1. Head of portfolio managementPick the signals, such as PIK toggles, maturity extensions, amendments, late delivery, revenue drop and liquidity
  2. Builder, in-house or hiredScore each name on those signals every quarter
  3. AnalystEdit the AI's one-page memo draft for each flagged name
  4. Head of portfolio managementDecide watchlist moves
  5. Valuation designeeUse the memo as an input, never as the mark

Cost: The same small extraction and model fees as builds 1 and 243,45. No monitoring platform we checked publishes a price, so a bought version means a quote46,47.

Which SEC rules shape AI portfolio monitoring?

Three SEC items shape it: Rule 2a-5 on fair value, the staff's September 2026 statement on private-asset marks, and the fiscal 2027 exam priorities on private credit valuation11,13,15.

How do you test AI covenant monitoring on last quarter's packages?

Run the tool on last quarter's packages for 10 borrowers, score every line against your existing spreads, recompute the covenants, and list every flag raised and issue missed36,29.

  1. Run the tool on last quarter's packages for 10 borrowers in one fund, including two messy scans, and score it against your own spreads. Pilot on one fund or strategy, not the whole book.
  2. Record today's numbers first: analyst hours per borrower, days from package to finished spread, and errors found later.
  3. Use the spreads already on file, or a fresh hand spread, as the answer key.
  4. Run the extraction and score every line item exact, close or wrong, with its confidence score. Older models failed most questions on public filings, so score every line, not a sample36.
  5. Recompute the covenants from the agreement's definitions. Compare with both the hand spread and the borrower's certificate29.
  6. List every flag the tool raised. Then list every known issue it missed, such as a late package, a PIK toggle or an add-back over its cap.
  7. Check where borrower data is stored and whether it trains any model. Data security was lenders' most-raised question in one vendor's 90 conversations52.
  8. Price the lift in dollars: analyst hours saved each quarter times their loaded cost. Add the value of each issue caught a quarter earlier.
  9. Run the next quarter beside today's process, then decide go or no-go with the head of portfolio management.

Where does AI covenant monitoring go wrong?

Monitoring goes wrong when extraction errors flow into covenant tests, the borrower's own math is trusted, inputs go stale, data is messy, or fraud hides behind clean numbers51,29,17,41,18.

  • It goes wrong on extraction errors, trusting the borrower's math, stale inputs, shifting default definitions, messy data and fraud no spreadsheet shows51,29,17. Each fix is below.
  • Extraction errors flow straight into covenant tests51,36. Review every low-confidence cell before the recompute runs.
  • The certificate is the borrower's own math29. Recompute from the agreement's definitions and log every gap.
  • Stale model inputs led to an SEC case: Fifth Street's marks used August 2013 projections, and it paid a $1.65M penalty17. Refresh inputs every quarter and record the date.
  • Measures of default for the same market run from about 1% to over 10%23,27. Pick your definition, write it down and keep it.
  • Messy data sinks AI projects: Gartner expects 60% of AI projects without AI-ready data to be abandoned through 202641. In a vendor-sponsored survey, only 8% of managers rated their data maturity high35.
  • Fraud is not a spreadsheet problem: the SEC alleges Tricolor pledged the same collateral twice18,19. Pair AI flags with lien searches and field checks.

Questions funds ask about AI covenant monitoring

What is covenant monitoring?

Checking each borrower's reported numbers against its loan covenants every quarter28. Maintenance covenants, such as debt-to-EBITDA and interest coverage, are tested through a compliance certificate28. It shows the borrower's own calculation, not the lender's29.

Does AI covenant monitoring take the analyst out of the loop?

No: PwC finds assistive uses such as extraction and covenant analysis are the mature ones, and people stay in credit decisions33. Even a vendor selling it answers no to the same question53.

Why not just put the PDFs into ChatGPT?

Because general models make number errors: in FinanceBench, GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval failed or refused 81% of questions on public filings36. That was 2023, but the lesson holds: check every number against the source. Lenders asked this 20 times in one vendor's 90 conversations52.

Is our borrower data safe with AI?

Only with the right setup: check where data is stored, who can see it and whether it trains a model. Data security was the top question in one vendor's talks with 90 lenders52. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework gives a checklist to put to any vendor42.

Can AI write the credit memo?

Yes, as a draft from the spread and the flags; the analyst edits and signs it33.

What does portfolio monitoring software cost?

Expect a yearly quote, often six figures: buyers report a median Allvue contract of $201,144 a year, from $77,562 to $939,02454. None of the platforms we checked publish a price, and that figure covers all Allvue contracts, not monitoring alone46,47,48. The AI underneath is cheap: document extraction lists at $10 per 1,000 pages (read 26 Nov 2026)43.

Should you buy a monitoring platform or build your own layer?

Buy: Most funds should buy a platform and build a thin layer on top; buy outright when you have no build team and a typical process. Bought AI tools succeed about 67% of the time; internal builds about a third as often40. Menlo finds 76% of enterprise AI use cases are bought39.

Build: Build the thin layer no platform knows: each agreement's definitions, your flag rules and your memo template. Extraction and model fees are small43,45. The risk is messy data: Gartner expects 60% of AI projects without AI-ready data to be abandoned41.

When should a fund pick something else?

Buy a platform such as Allvue or iLEVEL when you want one system for the whole book, or use a service such as 73 Strings for extraction46,47,48.

  • When you want one system for the whole credit book: look at Allvue, which says six of the top ten private debt managers use it46. It publishes no price.
  • Portfolio data already sits with S&P: iLEVEL says 700+ managers use it, with AI-assisted data intake47. It publishes no price.
  • Extraction and monitoring as a service: 73 Strings claims 99% accuracy with confidence scores and human review48. Ask how it measured that, and on whose files.
  • Asset-based finance or a multi-strategy shop: a third-party comparison lists Chronograph, Cardo AI and Arcesium49. We have not checked their own pages.
