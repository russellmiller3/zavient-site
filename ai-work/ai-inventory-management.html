What is AI inventory management, and what does it change?

AI inventory management resets each item's safety stock and reorder point from real demand and supplier lead times25,13. It also flags slow stock and suggests branch transfers, while buyers approve every order21,8. Clean stock records and honest lead times make or break it17,15.

Who on your team keeps the order decision?

  • The buyer keeps it: they approve every suggested purchase order and transfer before it goes out11,8. They log a reason for each override, since overrides of one parts retailer's stocking tool cut profit 5.77%8,9.
  • Planner or inventory analyst: sets the service level (the share of orders filled from stock) for each segment, and owns lead-time and demand inputs25,23. Adjusting the tool's inputs, not its outputs, raised profit 4.92% in a later field test10.
  • Branch or warehouse manager: accepts or rejects each transfer suggestion, and recounts the items the system flags21,17.
  • Controller: decides markdown, return or scrap for flagged dead stock before year end35,36. A flag alone creates no tax write-down; the goods must be scrapped or offered at a lower price35,37.

What do buyers and planners do once AI takes the first pass?

  • Buyers work a ranked list of exceptions (stockout risk, excess, late orders) instead of scanning every item, and planners tune the inputs11,10. In one store-chain study, ordering time fell 36.5% while service rose 2.9%11.
  • Buyers spend more time chasing suppliers: at one machinery maker, about 14% of orders arrived late14. Lead-time models flag those orders early, but only roughly15.
  • Planners review the override log each month against results, and adjust inputs where a supplier or item has changed10,8.
  • Among US firms where AI changed tasks, 66% use it only to help staff; 2% report AI-related falls in employment12.

Which way to start fits your firm?

1. Start here: lead-time and dead-stock checks on your own history

Firms with an analyst who can work an ERP export. Buyers' reported lead times for production materials rose six days in a year, so old settings may run short3.

  1. AnalystExport purchase-order lines with order date, receipt date and supplier
  2. AnalystWork out each supplier's average lead time and spread, and compare with the ERP field
  3. BuyerReview the outliers and update the ERP
  4. AnalystList items with no sale in 180 or 365 days and over 12 months of cover
  5. BuyerChoose return, transfer, markdown or scrap for each
  6. ControllerSign off any write-down before year end

Cost: Mostly analyst time, with no new licence. Keep it narrow: internal builds reached deployment about half as often as outside partnerships, as reported in a copy of MIT NANDA's 2025 report40.

2. One warehouse on NetSuite or Business Central: tune what you own

Single-site firms whose ERP already holds clean sales and order history. NetSuite 2026.2 sets safety stock by segment; Business Central needs it typed in25,27.

  1. PlannerExport 24 months of sales and purchase-order dates
  2. PlannerSort items into ABC (value) and XYZ (demand swings) classes
  3. Ops headSet a target service level for each class
  4. PlannerSwitch on Inventory Optimization, or set reorder rules by class in Business Central
  5. BuyerApprove suggested orders each week and log each override with a reason
  6. Ops headAfter 12 weeks, compare stock and fill rate with the same weeks last year

Cost: Mostly your planner's time. The features sit inside the ERP you already pay for25,28.

3. Several branches and thousands of parts: a planning add-on

Distributors with branches, lumpy spare parts or many suppliers. Transfers between branches pay most where moving stock is cheap next to holding it20,22.

  1. Ops headShortlist two add-ons that connect to your ERP
  2. PlannerGive each vendor the same 24 months of history
  3. VendorReplay last year under its suggested settings
  4. PlannerCheck the replay against what really happened
  5. Branch managerApprove or reject suggested transfers before any new order
  6. BuyerApprove orders; planner reviews overrides monthly

Cost: Smart IP&O lists from $695 a month on Capterra; EazyStock quotes only33,34. Vendors claim 6-12 weeks to go live: Netstock 6-10, StockIQ about 1241,42.

Which tax and accounting rules touch dead stock?

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
Treasury Regulation 1.471-2(c), subnormal goodsIn forceDamaged, shop-worn or obsolete goods can be valued below cost for tax when actually offered for sale at the lower price35. The offer must come within 30 days after the inventory date35. Time dead-stock flags so you can act before year end.
IRS practice unit, lower of cost or marketIRS examiner guidanceExcess or overstock is not subnormal unless it is scrapped or actually offered at reduced prices36. A tool's dead-stock list is a prompt to act, not a write-down36.
Thor Power Tool Co. v. Commissioner, 439 U.S. 522Supreme Court ruling, 1979The court disallowed a tax write-down of excess spare parts still held for sale at full price37. Keeping the old price while writing the stock down does not work for tax.
FASB ASU 2015-11, Inventory (ASC 330)In forceFor FIFO or average-cost inventory, your books carry it at the lower of cost and net realizable value38. Your accountant sets any reserve; this page is not tax or accounting advice.

How do you test AI inventory management before you trust it?

  1. Check your counts, replay last year on the tool, then pilot one branch: start by cycle-counting a random sample of about 200 item-locations17. If many records are wrong, fix counting before you test any tool.
  2. Pull 24 months of demand by item and location, plus each purchase order's order, promised and receipt dates. Add today's ERP safety stock, reorder point and lead time.
  3. Measure each supplier's real lead time against the ERP field, and count late order lines14,24.
  4. Sort items into ABC by value and XYZ by demand swings: X under 50% variation, Y 50-100%, Z over 100%23.
  5. Replay the last 12 months twice on the same demand: once with today's settings, once with the tool's. Compare average stock value, fill rate (share of order lines shipped complete) and number of orders13.
  6. Then run it live on one branch or product family for 8-12 weeks, with buyers approving, beside a matched branch. Log every override with a reason code8,10.
  7. Price the result in cash and service: stock-to-sales ratio, fill rate, dead-stock value and buyer hours. Compare your ratio with your sector in our table above1.
  8. Ask each vendor to run the replay on your data before you sign anything.

Where does AI inventory management go wrong?

  • It goes wrong on bad stock records, lumpy parts, rough lead-time guesses, costly overrides and branch averages that hide problems17,26. Each fix is below.
  • Wrong stock records feed wrong orders: one study found 65% of 370,000 store records inaccurate17. Undetected losses can freeze automatic reordering, so recount flagged items first18,19.
  • Lumpy parts break the maths. NetSuite warns its results are approximate for intermittent, promotional or seasonal demand, so review those items by hand26.
  • Lead-time predictions are useful, not precise: one model explained only 30-55% of the variation15. Keep a buyer's judgment on top and refresh lead times often15,3.
  • Overrides can cost money: on mature and declining items they hurt profit in a field test8. Log every override and review the log against results9.
  • Much of the research behind these tools ignores lead time: most papers in a 2025 review assumed it was zero16. Ask each vendor how its model treats lead time.
  • Averages hide branches: Business Central's AI forecast adds up all locations and variants29. Check each branch's numbers before you trust a transfer or order.

Questions buyers ask about AI inventory management

Is AI inventory management just reorder points with a new name?

Sometimes. Ask whether safety stock recalculates per item from demand and lead-time swings, and how often25,24. A fixed days-of-cover default is not that24.

Do we need new AI inventory management software, or does our ERP do this?

Check the ERP first. NetSuite 2026.2 sets safety stock by segment, Business Central takes it typed in, and Acumatica points to partner add-ons25,27,30.

How much cash could a 10% stock cut free?

A tenth of your stock value: about $221k per $10M of sales for a typical machinery distributor1. Carrying savings then add 10-30% of that each year, depending on your rate5,4.

What carrying cost rate should we use?

Work out your own, item by item if you can6. APQC's median across 6,468 organizations is 10%, while textbooks say 20-50%5,4. One case firm used 19% but measured 37.8%7.

Can we write off the dead stock the AI flags?

Only once you act on it. For tax, goods must be scrapped or actually offered at a reduced price35,36. Ask your accountant well before year end.

Will buyers still approve the orders?

Yes, and they should. In field tests at a parts retailer, adjusting the tool's inputs added profit, while overriding its outputs cut it10,8.

Should you buy AI inventory management software or build it?

Buy: Mostly buy, starting with what your ERP already has, then a planning add-on for several branches, lumpy parts or many suppliers25,33. Most firms now buy: 76% of enterprise AI use cases were bought in 2025, up from 53%39.

Build: Build only the narrow pieces your own data does best, such as late-order prediction from your purchase history14,13. As reported in a copy of MIT NANDA's 2025 report, internal builds reached deployment about 33% of the time, against 67% for partnerships40.

When should you pick something else?

  • When you have a few hundred steady items in one warehouse: ERP reorder rules by ABC class, reviewed each quarter, may be enough28.
  • New items, promotions or strong seasons: fix the demand forecast first; see our AI demand forecasting guide26.
  • Counts you can't trust: fix cycle counting before you buy any planning tool17,18.
