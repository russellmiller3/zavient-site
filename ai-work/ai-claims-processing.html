What is insurance claims automation, and what does it change?

Insurance claims automation, or AI claims processing, gives software the first pass on a claim: reading the report, documents and photos, then flagging it.11,12 Adjusters still decide coverage, reserves and payment.1 It works only if the file shows who decided what, inside each state's deadlines.4,13

Who on your claims team keeps the decision?

  • Adjuster: decides coverage, sets the reserve, signs any denial and talks to the customer.1,3 Texas expects a person to review and agree with any consequential AI decision before action.4
  • Claims supervisor or examiner: approves payments within their authority and spot-checks AI-filled files.1 The insurer answers for every AI-supported decision, vendor tools included.11,14
  • Special investigations unit (SIU) investigator: works each fraud flag as a lead to investigate, not a finding.3 A homeowners' lawsuit alleges an insurer's claim-screening algorithms scrutinised Black policyholders more; that claim survived dismissal.15,16
  • Head of claims with the compliance lead: own the written AI program and the list of AI tools in use.11,14 Both report to senior management, which answers to the board.11

What does your claims team do once AI takes the first pass?

  • Adjusters spend freed time on investigation, coverage analysis and customers, a TPA's own survey reports.17 Analysts see the next value in the roughly 15% of claims that are complex.12
  • Intake reps confirm AI-filled fields instead of keying them. One TPA says intake fell from 10 days to 36 hours (vendor claim).18
  • Customers still get a person on hard claims: 77% prefer a human for complex or emotional claims (vendor survey).19
  • Among US firms using AI, 66% use it only to help existing staff, and 2% report AI-related job cuts.20 In one labor study, 49% of carriers expect to add staff.21

Which way to build AI claims processing fits your firm?

1. Small claims team (MGA or small carrier): AI at intake and documents

Teams on an existing claims system who want fewer re-keyed fields and on-time acknowledgments. A review site lists Snapsheet, Hi Marley and Ushur as fits under 5,000 claims a month.8

  1. Intake repConfirm the fields AI filled from the first report
  2. Desk adjusterCheck the AI coverage summary against the policy, then set the reserve
  3. Desk adjusterSend the acknowledgment letter AI drafted, inside the state deadline
  4. Claims supervisorSpot-check 10% of AI-filled files each week
  5. Compliance leadAdd the tool to the AI inventory the bulletin asks for

Cost: Not published; vendors quote by claim volume or users, so ask for a price on your volume.24,8 Size it against your adjusting cost, about 9 cents per premium dollar across US P&C.6,30

2. Multi-client TPA or mid-size carrier: triage plus deadline tracking

TPAs handling claims for several carriers or self-insured clients across states, each with its own service levels. Carriers will ask about your AI, since the bulletin makes them answer for outside tools.11

  1. Claims ops leadHave the tool score each new claim for complexity and litigation risk
  2. Claims ops leadHave the tool open a state-deadline task on every new claim
  3. Claims supervisorAssign claims by score
  4. AdjusterDecide coverage and set the reserve
  5. SIU investigatorWork the ranked fraud referrals and record which proved out
  6. Client service leadReport deadline compliance per client each month

Cost: Not published; triage tools are licensed by claim volume or line.8 Late-reported claims carry about ten times the litigation risk (TPA survey), which is where triage pays.17

3. Document-heavy lines (workers' comp, casualty, bodily injury): summaries with citations

Teams buried in medical records, legal demands and invoices. In a survey of 263 insurance executives, claim summaries saved 35-45% of time on complex claims.34

  1. Claims ops leadRoute medical records, demands and invoices to the reading tool
  2. Adjuster or nurse case managerCheck each fact against the page the summary cites
  3. Claims supervisorApprove any reserve change
  4. AdjusterSend the checked summary to defense counsel

Cost: Not published; document tools price by volume and lines.8 Budget review time too: a Nordic insurer's tool read 70% of documents correctly.32

How do you test AI claims processing before you trust it?

  1. Pick one line or office and pull 200 to 500 of its closed claims from the last 12 months, split by state and outcome. Examiners sample closed files the same way; California's 2026 exam sampled 220.54
  2. Record today's numbers on those files: days to acknowledge, decide and pay, the share inside each state's deadline, reopens and complaints.23,37
  3. Run the tool in shadow mode on the same files. It fills fields, scores triage and fraud and suggests reserves, but changes nothing.
  4. Score document reading field by field against what the adjuster recorded, counting wrong fields as well as missing ones. Published rates run from 70% read correctly to 87% handled with no manual touch.32,28
  5. Compare flags with what happened: how many fraud flags SIU would have opened and proved, and how many simple files went to litigation. Late reporting carries about ten times the litigation risk (TPA survey).17
  6. Compare reserve suggestions with the adjuster's reserve and with what the claim finally cost, at 30 and 90 days.
  7. Check for unfair discrimination: compare flag rates and days to payment across ZIP codes and other proxies for protected groups.11,60
  8. Price the lift in dollars: hours saved times loaded adjuster cost, plus late-payment interest avoided, such as Michigan's 12%.47,1 Set it against your adjusting cost baseline.6
  9. Write the decision rule before go-live: which outputs a person confirms, who signs off and what the file shows. Texas expects that sign-off, and examiners will ask for the record.4,55

Where does AI claims processing go wrong?

  • Biased fraud and routing scores. A homeowners' lawsuit alleges an insurer's tools gave Black policyholders' claims more scrutiny; test flag rates by area before go-live and each quarter.15,60
  • One bad rule, repeated on every claim, can become a general business practice under unfair-claims law.3,52 Have compliance review each automated rule before it runs.
  • Missed state deadlines. California's 2026 wildfire-claims exam cited missed 15-, 40- and 30-day clocks; have the tool open a deadline task on day one.54,37
  • Adjusters fixing the AI instead of being helped by it: one adjuster describes 15 to 20 minutes correcting AI mistakes.21,19 Show the source page for every fact the tool pulls, and track rework time.
  • Vendor black boxes: you answer for vendor AI, and in a health case a court ordered an insurer to show how its tool worked.11,61 Put audit and testing rights in the contract.
  • A thinner talent pipeline: junior adjuster postings are down about half since early 2024, and 25% of adjusters may retire by 2027 (TPA survey).21,17 Keep juniors on AI-checked files so they still learn.

Questions buyers ask about AI claims processing

Can AI do insurance claims?

Yes, for the first pass: intake, document reading, triage, fraud flags and reserve suggestions.11,12 In the regulators' 2022 survey, 96 of 193 auto insurers used AI to inform adjusters and none used it to deny claims.2

Is AI taking over claims adjuster jobs?

Not on the evidence: BLS expects about 21,600 openings a year to 2035, even as total jobs dip 6%.1 In one labor study, 49% of carriers expect to add staff and 11% to cut.21 Among US firms using AI, 2% report AI-related job cuts.20

How do you automate claims processing?

Start with first-report intake and document reading on one line of business, test on closed files, then add triage.12,11 Keep coverage and payment decisions with adjusters.4

Can an insurer deny a P&C claim using AI alone?

No P&C state law bans it outright yet; Florida's attempt died in March 2026.39,59 Denials still need a reasonable investigation and a stated reason, and Texas expects a person to agree first.3,4

Does the AI bulletin reach a TPA or MGA handling claims for a carrier?

Indirectly. It is addressed to insurers, but they stay responsible for AI from outside firms, so expect questions in your contracts and audits.11,62

Do the same rules apply to health insurance claims?

No. Health claims run on medical-necessity rules, and California bars AI as the sole basis for those denials.58,9 Medicare Advantage decisions must rest on each patient's circumstances.10

Should you buy AI claims processing or build it?

Buy: Buy when you have no data team, run a common core claims system and the job is standard: first reports, photos, documents.63 Purchased AI tools succeed about 67% of the time; internal builds about a third as often.64,65

Build: Build, or configure heavily, when your own claims history is the edge or you must show a regulator every rule.11 Either way, 45% of insurers name data quality as a barrier, so clean the claim data first.34 Only 24% of insurance leaders are very confident they'd pass an AI governance review in 90 days.66

When should a claims team pick something else?

  • Mostly simple auto physical damage: a photo-estimating network such as CCC, Tractable or Snapsheet already does this job.7,26
  • The problem is talking to claimants, not reading files: a texting platform such as Hi Marley may fix more.24,7
  • Your core claims system is the bottleneck: upgrade or replace it first, such as Guidewire, Duck Creek or Five Sigma.7 AI on a system nobody trusts adds rework.
  • Low volume across varied lines: rules in your current system plus a weekly report on state deadlines may cost less than AI.13,23
