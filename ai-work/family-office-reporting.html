What does AI change in family office reporting?

AI for family office reporting pulls custodian, bank and fund data into one view across the family's entities, then matches it to the books.11,3 It also drafts the quarterly commentary, which people still check.12,7 Private-fund data makes or breaks it: values arrive months late and are often keyed by hand.5,13

Who in the office signs off before the family sees it?

The controller clears every break and the COO signs off the report, because AI drafts can state every number right and still add causes nobody supplied.7,3

  • Controller: clears each break the platform or AI flags.3,14 Black Diamond says 99.5% of accounts complete automatically, yet its brief says exceptions are still researched and adjusted by hand.3
  • Investment operations analyst: checks each private-fund value against the general partner (GP) statement, with the document attached.14,27
  • COO: sets the AI policy, the approved tools and who sees which data.9 Two-factor sign-in among offices fell from 92% to 83%, so check it before any tool reads custodian data.1
  • Controller and COO: read every AI-written sentence before the principal sees it.7,8 BNY finds judgment and oversight in family offices remain largely human-led.12

What does your team do once AI takes the first pass?

Your team moves from keying and matching to clearing breaks, checking private-fund values and building the views the family asks for.3,9

  • Controllers move from keying statements to clearing the breaks the match leaves.3,13
  • Analysts check the private-fund line: the statement date, the roll-forward for cash flows and the label.5,27
  • Some offices rely on one or two platform power users; they build the views the family asks for.9 Meeting family members' needs is the top challenge for 54% of offices.18
  • Where AI changed tasks, 66% of US firms use it only to help staff, and AI-related employment fell at only 2% of AI-using firms.28

Which reporting setup fits your office?

Small offices should run reporting on one platform, and offices with many private funds should add an AI check layer that reads statements and sorts breaks.18,9

1. Small office: one platform, people check

Offices with six people or fewer, which are 63% of offices in Citi's survey.18 Lean on the platform's feeds, since offices average fewer than one IT professional.31

  1. COOPick one platform and map every entity and ownership percentage into it
  2. ControllerConnect custodian and bank feeds
  3. AnalystEnter each GP statement with the document attached
  4. Platform AIMatch positions and draft the commentary
  5. ControllerClear each break and strike any cause the inputs don't support
  6. COOLabel each private value with its statement date, then sign off

Cost: Published prices run from $250 a year for Kubera Essentials to $35,899 a year for FundCount's single family office plan, before fees.15,16

2. Office with many private funds: platform plus an AI check layer

Offices with several custodians and dozens of private funds, where statements are keyed by hand.13 The platform does the plumbing; you build the checks and the view.9

  1. ControllerKeep the platform as the book of record
  2. AIRead each GP statement into fields, linked to its page
  3. AnalystApprove each extracted value and roll it forward for cash flows
  4. AISort breaks by cause, such as timing, data mismatch or a changed security code
  5. ControllerClear the breaks and sign the reconciliation
  6. AIDraft commentary from approved numbers only
  7. COORead every sentence, strike unsupported causes and release the report

Cost: Asora quotes $15,600 a year at one net worth tier, plus $3,900 onboarding and $3,600 a year per 20 alternative holdings.29 Median IT spend among offices is U.S. $100,000.1

Which rules apply to a family office report?

Few rules govern a single family office's own report: an office serving only family is not an investment adviser, though Form 13F filing can still apply.21,22

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
Family office rule, 17 CFR 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1In forceAn office is not an investment adviser under the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) rule if it serves only family and is family-owned.21 It must also not hold itself out to the public.21 Its internal report is then not an adviser's client communication; a registered multi-family office's report is.
SEC Form 13FIn forceA manager with investment discretion over $100 million or more in 13(f) securities files Form 13F each quarter.22 The filing is due within 45 days after quarter end, even for an office exempt from adviser rules.22

How do you test AI on your own quarter before you trust it?

Run AI beside today's process for two quarter ends on one custodian and five private funds, then count breaks caught, causes invented and days saved.23,7

  1. Record today's numbers for one quarter: days from quarter end to final report, reconciliation hours and open breaks. In a FundCount-sponsored survey of 27 family office professionals, time to produce final reports was the most-used KPI (key performance indicator).23
  2. Pick one custodian and five private funds as the pilot; keep everything else on today's process.
  3. Run the AI match beside today's reconciliation for two quarter ends, and count breaks caught, missed and raised in error.
  4. Sort each break by cause: timing, data mismatch, or a changed security code.4,14
  5. Test the retrieval, not just the model: one benchmark's accuracy fell to 0.2% when the model lacked the right pages.25
  6. Check the words as well as the numbers: mark every cause or judgment the draft adds that no input supports.7
  7. Price the lift: hours saved times loaded cost, plus days cut from quarter end to the report the family reads.

Where does AI family office reporting go wrong?

AI family office reporting goes wrong when stale fund values go unlabelled, drafts invent causes, retrieval misses the source page, or ownership is mapped wrong.24,7,25,11

  • Stale private values: private equity values typically arrive 45 to 75 days after quarter end.24 Print each fund line's statement date and roll-forward, as pension plans do.19,5
  • Right numbers, wrong story: AI drafts add causes no one supplied, and AI readers took an invented cause as the reason in 99% of texts.7 Have the controller strike every unsupported cause.7
  • Retrieval misses: GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval answered wrong or refused on 81% of FinanceBench questions.41 In another benchmark, retrieval caused 66.5% of failures.25
  • Feeds are not the whole picture: about 40% of family offices still worry about spreadsheets and manual aggregation.30 In some offices, private-markets data sits outside the platform.9
  • Double counting across entities: map each entity's ownership share and multiply down the chain.11 A FundCount-sponsored survey of 44 family offices ranked missing look-through reporting among its main system weaknesses.42

Questions buyers ask about AI for family office reporting

Why does our quarterly report take weeks?

Private-fund statements arrive late by design: ILPA guidance gives direct funds 60 days after quarter end, and funds of funds 120.6 Keying them by hand adds the rest.13

Can AI reconcile custodian feeds against our ledger and flag breaks?

Yes, but platforms already match most accounts, so AI pays on sorting the breaks.3 Black Diamond says 99.5% of accounts complete automatically, yet its brief says exceptions are still researched and adjusted by hand.3

Can we say on the report that private values are stale?

Yes, and large investors already do: New York's pension fund says private equity is reported on a three-month lag, adjusted by cash flows.19

Who signs off before the family sees the report?

The COO signs off after the controller has read every AI-written sentence, because drafts can state every number right and still add causes.7,9

What does the family want to see first?

The principal wants net worth, liquidity and major changes in one view, says Masttro, a reporting vendor.43 Meeting family members’ needs is the top challenge for 54% of offices.18

Should you buy a reporting platform or build your own?

Buy the aggregation platform, because custodian feeds number in the hundreds, and build only the checks and views your family needs.17,9

Buy: Buy the aggregation and accounting platform: Simple’s 2026 report sums the pattern up as buy the plumbing, build the view.9 Vendors typically quote 2 to 4 months to implement.9 Across US enterprise AI decision-makers, 76% of AI use cases are now bought rather than built.44

Build: Build the check layer and the views on top, because your entity map, labels and review rules are your own.9,11 For organizations, partnered builds reached deployment about 67% of the time, against about 33% for internal builds.45

When should an office pick something else?

Pick something else when the books are simple, keying fund statements is the bottleneck, security comes first, or the ledger is not reconciled yet.15,26

  • Small office with simple books: a household tool such as Kubera Essentials, at $250 a year for 2 portfolios and 2 users, may be enough.15
  • Keying fund statements is the bottleneck: start with document capture, as in our AI document processing guide.46,29
  • Security comes first: cybersecurity is now the top near-term concern for 59% of offices, up from 16%.26 Fix sign-in and payment controls before adding tools.1
  • The ledger isn't reconciled yet: fix the close first, with our multi-entity close and reconciliation guides.47,48
