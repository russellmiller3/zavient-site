What is AI cash forecasting, and what does it change?

Cash forecasting estimates your future cash position, usually week by week for the next 13 weeks.2,8 AI changes the hardest part: predicting when each customer will actually pay, from their payment history.6,7 The forecast is only as good as the receivables, payables and bank data it reads.2,10

Which cash calls stay with your controller and COO?

Your controller owns the weekly cash number and each override, and the COO or owner decides on draws and signs what goes to the bank.11,6

  • Controller: owns the weekly forecast and signs the number. NetSuite's own documentation calls its predicted pay dates estimates, not guarantees, to weigh with business judgment.6
  • Accounts receivable (AR) lead: flags disputed invoices and promised payments the model can't see. In Accenture's rollout, users changed the key things the AI model couldn't.4
  • Controller: writes down every manual override and its reason. Forecasting texts say to adjust sparingly and justify each change, and NIST asks for documented human oversight.18,13
  • COO or owner-CEO: decides on draws, payment timing and spend holds, and signs what goes to the bank. Management owns the going-concern judgment, whether the firm can meet the next year's obligations.11

What does your finance team do once AI drafts the forecast?

After AI drafts the forecast, the controller explains variances, AR staff chase the customers likely to pay late, and sales and finance work on payment terms.4,6,10

  • AI automates the routine data work, and the controller interprets the forecast and explains variances to the COO.9 In Accenture's case, forecasts came out within minutes of loading the data.4
  • AR staff call the customers whose invoices the model expects to pay late, before the due date passes.6,20
  • The controller works with sales on payment terms, because easy terms win deals but delay the cash.10
  • Most firms keep their people. Of firms using AI, 66% use it only to help staff, and AI-related employment fell at 2% of firms.12

Which way to build a cash forecast fits your firm?

Firm size decides it: small single-entity firms buy a planning tool, mid-size ERP firms test the built-in prediction first, and multi-entity firms build an explainable model.22,6,11

1. Small team on QuickBooks or Xero (about 40-80 staff, one entity)

One entity, standard payment terms, and a controller who builds the forecast in a spreadsheet today. Float connects to QuickBooks and Xero.22

  1. ControllerConnect a planning tool such as Float, and set payroll, rent and debt calendars22
  2. AR leadLog disputed invoices and customer-promised pay dates every week6
  3. ControllerLog forecast against actual each Monday, using WAPE (weighted absolute percentage error) and bias19,17
  4. Owner-CEOReview the 13-week low case before any big commitment8

Cost: Float Growth is $215 a month billed annually, or $265 monthly (read 28 Nov 2026).22

2. Mid-size firm on an ERP like NetSuite or Dynamics 365 (about 80-250 staff)

Hundreds of customers, a year or more of paid invoices, and an ERP. NetSuite's pay-date prediction needs 12 weeks of history and 50+ paid invoices.6

  1. ControllerSwitch on the ERP's pay-date prediction or cash forecast where it exists6,7
  2. Data lead or outside builderPull paid invoices and bank lines into one table, and fit a per-customer pay-timing model20
  3. ControllerKeep payroll, rent and supplier payment runs on their calendars2
  4. ControllerBacktest against the due-date rule from past Mondays before trusting it16,15
  5. COOUse the weekly number for credit-line draws and spend decisions8

Cost: Neither NetSuite's nor Microsoft's documentation states a price for these features.6,7 Trovata's base package is $24k a year, and a data scientist's median pay was $120,230 in May 2025.23,29

3. Multi-entity or lender-reporting firm (about 150-500 staff)

Several entities, an asset-based loan or covenants, and an auditor or lender who reads the forecast. Auditors test a significant forecast's data and assumptions.11

  1. ControllerRun one forecast per entity and consolidate it weekly8
  2. BuilderFit an explainable model per cash line, showing the reasons behind each invoice's predicted date11,13
  3. ControllerKeep a forecast-against-actual log the auditor and lender can read11,7
  4. Owner-CEO or COOSign what goes to the bank8

Cost: Float's Scale plan is $315 a month billed annually, plus $63 a month per added entity.22 Enterprise treasury tools quote each deal, and AFP describes one in-house build still about 70% complete after five years.24,26,5

Does learning each customer's pay habit beat the due-date rule?

Yes, by our backtest: predicting each customer's pay date cut weekly receipts error from 32.3% to 21.2%, against assuming everyone pays on the due date.1 On 28 Nov 2026 we ran it on IBM's simulated sample of 2,466 invoices from, by our count, 100 customers, all on 30-day terms.1 From each of 40 Mondays in 2013, we forecast which week each open invoice would be paid, using only earlier payments.16

Which rules apply to a cash forecast?

Going-concern accounting rules apply: management judges whether cash covers the next year's obligations, and auditors test a significant forecast's data and assumptions.11

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
ASC 205-40, Going concern (management)In forceManagement must judge whether conditions raise substantial doubt about meeting obligations within one year after the statements are issued.11 Expected cash flows feed that judgment, so your forecast's assumptions must hold up.
AU-C 570, the auditor's going-concern workIn forceWhere a cash flow forecast is significant, the auditor checks the reliability of its data and the support for its assumptions.11 Keep inputs, assumptions and overrides written down.

How do you test a cash forecast before you trust it?

Backtest it from past Mondays on your own invoices, score weekly error and bias against the due-date rule, and make every vendor run the same test.16,15,19

  1. Record your before numbers: last quarter's weekly forecast against actual cash, and how often you drew on the credit line unexpectedly.
  2. Pull 12-24 months of paid invoices with paid dates, plus today's open invoices, for one entity or customer group first. NetSuite's own floor is 12 weeks and 50 paid invoices.6
  3. Set baselines: the due-date rule and your current spreadsheet. A new method must beat simple benchmarks to earn its place.15
  4. Backtest, meaning replay the past: forecast from each past Monday using only data before it. Never score a model on payments it has already seen.16
  5. Score weekly totals with WAPE, not MAPE (mean absolute percentage error), which breaks when a week's actual is zero.17,19
  6. Score bias, whether you run high or low, separately, because a forecast that runs high leaves you short. In our backtest the due-date rule ran 9.8% high on near-term receipts.1
  7. Score weeks 1-4 apart from weeks 5-13, because short-term forecasts are the most accurate.2
  8. Ask each vendor for the same numbers on your data: metric, horizon and baseline. A bare claim of up to 95% accuracy gives none of them.26
  9. Price the lift in dollars: smaller error means a smaller cash buffer and fewer surprise draws. Multiply the buffer you free by your credit line's interest rate.
  10. Run the model beside your current sheet on live Mondays before switching, and keep logging forecast against actual after go-live. Dynamics 365 builds this comparison in.7

Where does AI cash forecasting go wrong?

Cash forecasts go wrong on bad input data, errors that cancel in the total, shortcuts like adding average days late, unseen shocks and unverifiable vendor claims.2,10,1,26

  • Bad inputs make a bad forecast: missing paid dates or a messy customer list mislead any model.2,4 Clean the customer master and paid-date fields first.
  • Errors can cancel out in the total, so big over- and underestimates net to a good-looking number.10 Score by cash line and by week, not just the total.
  • Adding each customer's average days late to the due date was worse than the plain due-date rule in our backtest, 43.7% against 32.3% error.1 The shortcut ignores customers who pay early, so model days to pay from the invoice date.
  • Some drivers sit outside the model: in Accenture's rollout, users changed the key things the AI model couldn't.4 Adjust only for real extra information, like an acquisition, with a written reason for each.18
  • Accuracy claims without a method can't be checked: one vendor advertises up to 95% accuracy with no metric, horizon or baseline.26 Ask for a rolling backtest on your own data.16
  • Long builds stall: AFP describes one in-house treasury system about 70% complete after five years.5 Start with receipts timing only, on bank connections you buy.5

Questions buyers ask about AI cash flow forecasting

Can AI forecast cash flow?

Yes, mainly the timing of customer payments, while predictable lines like payroll stay on their calendars.6,2 In our backtest, predicting pay dates cut weekly receipts error by about a third.1

Can I do AI cash flow forecasting in Excel?

Only in part: Excel can hold the 13-week sheet, but learning each customer's pay habit needs months of invoice history and a model.8,6 In our backtest, each customer's average days to pay, which a spreadsheet can work out, got most of the gain.1

How much payment history do you need?

NetSuite's built-in prediction needs at least 12 weeks of history and 50 paid invoices.6 Your test window should be at least as long as the 13 weeks you forecast.17

How accurate should a cash forecast be?

Accurate enough to beat your due-date baseline on weekly error and bias, since no universal target exists.15 EY found only 28% of large companies' cash forecasts landed within 10% of annual free cash flow targets.10

Should we buy cash forecasting software or build our own?

Buy if you run one entity on standard terms with no data person, and build when customer pay habits or lender reporting are your edge.22,28

What is a 13-week cash flow forecast?

A 13-week cash flow forecast is a weekly view of cash in and out over the next 90 days, rolled forward each week.8,9

Should you buy cash forecasting software or build your own?

Buy: Buy when you run one entity on standard terms and have no data person. Buying is the norm: 76% of enterprise AI solutions are bought rather than built.27 Float publishes $215 a month billed annually, and a bought platform can include maintenance and upgrades.22,5

Build: Build, or build with a partner, when customer pay habits or lender reporting are your edge and you must explain each number to an auditor.11 Partnered custom builds reached deployment about twice as often as internal ones.28 AFP notes builds are practical again with AI, but control brings ongoing responsibility.5

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you have under 50 paid invoices, run one simple entity, already own an ERP forecast, or need treasury across many banks.6,22,23

  • Under 50 paid invoices or 12 weeks of history: use a plain 13-week spreadsheet. NetSuite won't predict below that floor.6,8
  • One entity on QuickBooks or Xero: try Float before any custom build.22
  • Already on NetSuite or Dynamics 365 Finance: switch on the built-in pay-date prediction or cash flow forecast and test it first.6,7
  • Many banks, entities and currencies with a treasury team: buy a treasury system such as Trovata or Agicap.23,24 For 12-month plans, use planning software such as Cube.25,9
