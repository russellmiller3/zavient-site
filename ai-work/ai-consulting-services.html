What should an AI consultant actually deliver?

AI consulting should take the busywork of one costly finance job off your team's plate, such as invoice exceptions, which hit 18.4% of invoices34. Most generative AI efforts never show up in the P&L1,16; the winners measure AI's impact and redesign the work around it15. They benchmark on their own files5,23 and plan for life after launch, where most of the cost sits3,12.

How should a good AI consulting engagement run?

1. Benchmark first

Before anyone builds, to settle which approach wins on your files and what it costs; public leaderboards are only a first filter5. Pick a high-volume, repetitive job with a known cost of errors, and price it today: an invoice costs $9.84 on average to process34.

  1. Process leadMeasure today's time and error rate per file
  2. Process leadPick 50 real files
  3. Your teamAgree the right answers, then lock them
  4. ConsultantRun the leading options
  5. ConsultantReport accuracy and cost per file

Cost: Quoted per job, with a fixed scope. Make sure you keep the files, the answer key and the code, so you can re-run it.

2. Build the winner into the work

Once the benchmark shows a clear winner that pays back.

  1. ConsultantSet up the winning model
  2. ITConnect it to your systems and workflow
  3. Named reviewerClear the queue of unsure cases
  4. ITLog every decision
  5. Process leadGo live at one site or team first
  6. Process leadMove to full volume

Cost: Depends on the systems it touches. Gartner warns that a production system can cost orders of magnitude more than the pilot3,12, so scope it from the benchmark, not the demo.

3. Keep it right

Always, because models, prices and your documents change. The price of a given level of AI performance has fallen 9 to 900 times a year22.

  1. ConsultantFlag a new model or price change
  2. ConsultantRe-run the benchmark
  3. Process leadCheck if it's cheaper or better
  4. YouDecide to switch, or keep

Cost: Small, because the benchmark already exists. Running the model is at least 70% of its lifetime cost3,12, so a cheaper model that passes the benchmark saves on every file.

How do you benchmark whether a consultant can deliver?

  1. Measure today first, so the after has a before.
  2. Ask for a benchmark on 20 to 50 of your own files before you sign for a full build.
  3. Agree the right answers with your own team first, and lock them, so the result is graded against your standard.
  4. Ask for cost per correct answer, not price per token. In our benchmark the cheapest tool made 16 wrong answers; the two best made none7.
  5. Ask why they picked their model. In our benchmark, two tools found nearly the same facts at prices 48 times apart7; Claude's own list prices span about 40 times11.
  6. Ask what happens when a better model ships next month. If the answer is a new project, the system will go stale.
  7. Ask for a client where the system has run in production for at least six months.
  8. Ask where your files are stored, who can see them, and whether anyone trains AI models on them.
  9. If you lend or insure, ask what model documentation and validation you get for your examiner, and who keeps it current.
  10. Ask who clears unsure cases, how fast, and who covers when they're out.

Why do most AI projects never pay off?

  • Firms that resell one software vendor's product and call it a recommendation. 37% of enterprise CIOs surveyed by a16z run five or more models, picked by use case5.
  • Demos on clean sample data that never meet your real files. Gartner lists poor data quality among the main reasons generative AI projects are dropped after the proof of concept13.
  • No answer key: without agreed right answers, nobody can show the system works. Anthropic's own guide says to set measurable success criteria and benchmark on cases that mirror the real task23.
  • Budgets that stop at the pilot. Gartner expects at least half of generative AI projects to overrun through 2028, and running the model is most of the lifetime cost3,12.

Should you hire a consultant, buy software or build in-house?

Buy: Hire a consultant when you need one system working this quarter, have no AI engineers, and want someone accountable for the result. Pay for a fixed scope with a benchmark you can check. Bought or partnered projects succeed about 3 times as often as internal builds1.

Build: Hire in-house when AI is core to what you sell and you will run many systems. Expect about two and a half months to fill each technical role20 and the salaries above8.

When is an AI consultant the wrong call?

  • AI is your product and you will run many systems: hire in-house. Technical roles take a median 75 days to fill20, so budget for that and the salaries in the table below8.
  • A standard job that software already does, such as accounts payable: buy the product and benchmark it on your own files. 76% of enterprise AI use cases are now bought2, and bought projects succeed about 3 times as often as internal builds1.
