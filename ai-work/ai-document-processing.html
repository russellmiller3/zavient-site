How does AI document processing work, and how is it different from OCR?

Document AI reads a PDF or scan and returns the fields your systems use, like totals and dates3,4. OCR returns raw text only; document AI knows which number is which2,3. Start with the document your team re-keys most: reading costs cents, and staff time is the real cost7,1.

FinExtractBench is our own benchmark of 30 public private-fund reports, 781 pages in all7. We wrote every right answer by hand and locked it before any tool ran7. Each tool read each raw PDF once; a mistake is a wrong value or a value the report never states.

Cheap and fast: one model, quotes checked by code

Internal use, modest volume, a person already reviews the output.

  1. PDF
  2. Small model reads it
  3. Each fact + page + quote
  4. Code checks the quote is on that page
  5. Your reviewer fixes failures and logs each fix

Cost: Under 2 cents per 57-page report with OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna, Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 or DeepSeek V4.1 Flash in our hard round7. Add review time.

Fewest wrong numbers: two models, a person on disagreements

The numbers feed money decisions, reports to investors or a regulator.

  1. PDF
  2. Model A and Model B read it
  3. Code compares fact by fact
  4. AgreeAccept
  5. DisagreeYour finance or ops checker decides (maker-checker: a second person approves)
  6. System logs each decision and who made it
  7. Approved facts load to your accounting or investor-reporting system

Cost: About $0.27 per report for two top models in our locked-answer round7. The two models disagreed on 66 of 270 facts across all 30 reports, about 1 in 4, and a person settled each7.

High volume, standard forms: cloud parser first, model for the hard cases

Many invoices, receipts or IDs in layouts the clouds already know.

  1. Scan or PDF
  2. Cloud prebuilt parser
  3. Low confidence?
  4. Model re-reads it
  5. Person on what is still unsure

Cost: About $10 per 1,000 pages for prebuilt parsers at AWS and Azure2,4, plus model cost on the share that fails.

How do you benchmark AI document tools on your own files?

  1. Record today's baseline first: staff minutes per file, and errors that reach the systems and reports downstream.
  2. Pick 50 real files, including your worst scans and longest reports, plus facts some files never state, to catch invented answers. Even vendors advise benchmarking on your own documents20.
  3. Write the right answer for every fact before any AI reads the files, and lock it, so the AI's output cannot sway it.
  4. Require each answer to name its page and quote the exact words, and have code check that the quote is there.
  5. Count wrong answers apart from blanks. A blank goes to a person; a wrong number can slip through.
  6. Report minutes per file, wrong fields reaching downstream systems and cost per file, before and after. Run the winner again on new files before you go live.

Can you trust a vendor's accuracy numbers?

  • Vendor accuracy numbers mostly come from the vendor's own benchmark runs20, and public answer keys have errors too18. Even on our locked-answer set, when the AI and our key differed, the AI was right in 22 of 57 cases, so check your key7.
  • A model that writes a plausible wrong number is worse than one that leaves the field blank17. Score them separately.
  • Two tables on one page with similar labels fooled 7 of 10 tools in our hard round7. Check the facts that sit near look-alikes.
  • Prices and model versions change: Mistral lists OCR 3 at $2 and OCR 4.1 at $4 per 1,000 pages8. Re-run your benchmark when either changes, and keep the files and answers so the re-run is cheap.
  • Read the vendor's data terms. Datalab's rate card shows prices with a 25% discount that assumes you let it train on your data12.
  • Plan reviewer time: in our benchmark, about 1 fact in 4 (66 of 270) went to a person7. Multiply by the facts you pull each month to size the workload.

Should you buy a document parser or build your own?

Buy: Buy a prebuilt parser or an accounts payable tool when your files are standard invoices, receipts or IDs. The big clouds' prebuilt models already cover those2,4. Buying fits best when you mainly need volume and an audit trail.

Build: Build on frontier models (the newest, most capable ones) when your files are unique to your business and a wrong number is expensive. The build is a prompt, a fact list, a checker and a review queue where your team settles about 2 facts per report7. If files must stay in-house, run open-source Docling6 or a local model; in our benchmark, local models made more wrong values7.

When should you pick something else?

  • Thousands of standard invoices a day? A prebuilt cloud parser, a ready-made reader for standard forms, costs about $10 per 1,000 pages2,4.
  • Your files cannot leave your network: run open-source Docling (free for business use)6 or a local model. Expect more wrong values: in our first round the two local models made 8 each, where Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 made none7.
  • You only need searchable text, not fields: basic OCR is about $1.50 per 1,000 pages at all three big clouds2,3,4.
