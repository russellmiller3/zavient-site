Can AI forecast demand better than last year plus growth?

Bottom line
  • AI cut error about 40% versus last year plus growth in our test. Simple statistics got most of that.1,2
  • AI helps most on fast, erratic items and least on slow, lumpy ones. Sort items by demand pattern first.3,4
  • Planner overrides help only when large and backed by news. Small or upward tweaks usually make forecasts worse.5,6
  • Test any model on your own history before you buy. Vendor accuracy claims use their own baselines.7,8
  • Price the gain in safety stock, your buffer against forecast error. Less error frees cash at the same service level.9,10

What does AI demand forecasting do, and what does it change?

AI demand forecasting predicts sales by item and week from your history and drivers like price and season.2,11 What changes: planners review exceptions instead of building numbers, and error fell about 40% against last year plus growth in our test.7,1 Clean history and news-backed overrides make or break it.14,6

Which forecast calls stay with your planner?

Your demand planner owns the final number and every override, with a written reason for each, and the supply planner picks the service level.15,7,13

  • Demand planner: owns the final number and every override, with a written reason for each. Forecasting texts say to adjust sparingly and document why.15,7
  • Sales and marketing: flag promotions, new accounts and lost customers. In one study, the best setup had people flag events and the model size them.16
  • Supply or inventory planner: picks the service level, meaning how often you accept running out. The model only supplies the error spread that sizes safety stock, your buffer against error.13,9
  • COO or owner: approves the monthly plan at the executive S&OP (sales and operations planning) meeting.12,13 ASCM's 2026 AI report advises keeping complex planning under human oversight.17

What do planners do once AI drafts the forecast?

Planners stop building numbers and start reviewing exceptions, spending their time on the few big calls like a promotion, a new customer or a lost account.7,16

  • Planners stop building numbers and start reviewing exceptions.7 Forecast value added (FVA) scores each step against a naive forecast, such as same week last year, and keeps only steps that help.18,19
  • Planner time goes to the few big calls: a promotion, a new customer, a lost account.16 At one European retailer, planner changes to 30 million AI forecasts added no accuracy on average.20
  • S&OP prep shifts to scenarios and supply trade-offs, the steps that come after the forecast.12 ASCM advises keeping those complex planning calls with people.17
  • Most firms keep their people. Where AI changed tasks, 66% of firms only used it to help staff, and 2% reported employment falling.21,22

Which way to start fits your firm?

Size decides it: Excel teams trial a low-cost tool, mid-size ERP firms switch on a forecasting module, and firms with an analyst and customer data build their own.37,29,39

1. Small team on Excel (under about 2,000 items, one buyer)

One buyer or planner, forecasts in a spreadsheet, and no planning tool yet. Excel is still the most used forecasting tool.37

  1. BuyerExport 2-3 years of weekly sales by item, with stockout weeks marked23,14
  2. BuyerBuild an Excel FORECAST.ETS baseline next to last year plus growth26
  3. Ops leadScore both on the latest 13 weeks you held back, using the test below38
  4. BuyerTrial one low-cost tool on the same held-back weeks24,27
  5. OwnerApprove only if the safety stock dollars freed beat the fee13

Cost: Excel's baseline is included.26 Forecast Pro Professional is $199 a month per user, billed annually, and StockTrim is $298 a month at $3-5M revenue (prices read 26 Nov 2026).27,24

2. Mid-size firm on an ERP (about 2,000-20,000 items, a demand planner)

A distributor or manufacturer on Dynamics 365, Business Central, NetSuite or Cin7, with a planner who owns the number.25,30

  1. Demand plannerSort items by pattern: smooth (steady), erratic (sells often, swings a lot), intermittent (often zero) and lumpy (often zero, then big)3
  2. IT or ERP adminSwitch on the forecasting module or add-on in a test company29,30
  3. Demand plannerTest it on 13-26 held-back weeks from several start dates38
  4. Supply plannerRecompute safety stock from the new error spread9,10
  5. S&OP process ownerRun two monthly cycles beside the old forecast12
  6. COO or ownerDecide at the executive S&OP meeting12,13

Cost: Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Premium is $300 per user a month.25 Rand Group's Business Central app is $3,500 a year plus $2,400 for AI forecasting; NetSuite and Cin7 don't publish forecasting prices.30,31,28

3. Data-led build (customer sell-through or EDI data the vendors don't see)

You have an analyst and data a vendor won't use, like customer point-of-sale or EDI (electronic order) feeds. In one 2026 study, retailer data cut error 44.7% against shipments alone.39

  1. AnalystBuild the baselines: same week last year, exponential smoothing and Croston2,3
  2. AnalystAdd one gradient-boosted model across all items, with recent sales, calendar and price2
  3. Demand plannerReview forecast value added every month7
  4. Head of supply chainKeep the model only for demand patterns where it beats the baselines4

Cost: The libraries are free, and our 2,524-item test ran in about 7 minutes on a 4-core cloud machine.1 The real cost is staff time, and internal builds succeed about half as often as bought tools.40

How much better than last year plus growth is an AI forecast?

In our test, an AI forecast cut error from 43.1% to 26.0% against last year plus growth, but simple smoothing got most of that gain at 27.3%.1 AI won by the widest margin on erratic sellers, 39.4% against 79.0%.

On 26 Nov 2026 we backtested six forecasting methods on public Walmart sales data from the M5 competition.1,2 We summed 2,524 items across 10 stores, like one warehouse, and forecast May 2015 to May 2016 from four start dates.38 Error is WAPE (weighted absolute percentage error): units missed divided by units sold, so lower is better.41

How do you test demand forecasting software on your own history?

Hold back your latest 13 to 26 weeks of sales, have each tool forecast them, and score it against last year plus growth using WAPE and bias.38,43

  1. Record your before numbers: today's forecast error, bias, fill rate and safety stock dollars. Score your current method on the same weeks you test the tools on.
  2. Pull 2-3 years of weekly demand by item; two years is the minimum to learn seasons.23,42 Use orders, not shipments, where you can, and mark stockout weeks.14
  3. Pick one warehouse or product family, and hold back its latest 13-26 weeks to forecast from several start dates. Never score a model on weeks it trained on.38
  4. Set three baselines: same week last year, last year plus growth, and Excel's FORECAST.ETS.19,26 A tool must beat these to earn its fee.7
  5. Score with WAPE and bias (whether you run high or low), not MAPE (mean absolute percentage error), which breaks on zero-sales weeks.43,41 Weight by dollars if a few items matter most.
  6. Split results by demand pattern: smooth, erratic, intermittent and lumpy.3 Expect the biggest gains on steady and erratic sellers.2,4
  7. Run forecast value added on your overrides: naive, then system forecast, then the planner's final number.7,18 Stop the kinds of override that cost accuracy.6
  8. Price the lift in dollars: safety stock scales with error spread, so by our math 10% less spread frees about 10% of safety stock.9,10 Multiply by unit cost and your yearly carrying-cost rate.
  9. Make each vendor run the same test on your data and hold-out before you sign. Published accuracy, like one vendor's 32% average error cut, uses that vendor's own baseline.8,7

Where does AI demand forecasting go wrong?

AI demand forecasting goes wrong when stockouts hide real demand, small upward overrides add error, or a forecast that runs low hides behind a good error score.14,6,1

  • Stockouts hide real demand, so the forecast drifts down and the next order shrinks.14,44 Record stockout days; recovering lost demand cut WAPE from 31.75% to 29.02% in one study.45
  • Small and upward overrides add optimism and error.5,6 Require a reason code, track FVA by planner, and allow only large, news-backed changes.15,7
  • Growth rates on single items amplify noise. In our test, an item-level growth rate (last 13 weeks against a year earlier) raised error from 37.0% to 43.1%.1,19
  • A good error score can hide a forecast that runs low: our AI model was 6% under, against 1.9% for smoothing.1 Track bias next to error, because low forecasts mean stockouts.13
  • The model trusts the past when the world shifts: Zillow shut its home-buying arm after a $304.4M write-down.46,47 Watch error weekly, and let planners pause the model after a shock.
  • Services can close: AWS shut Amazon Forecast to new customers on 29 Jul 2024.48,49 Keep your data, baselines and test set in-house so you can switch.

Questions buyers ask about AI demand forecasting

Is AI actually useful for demand forecasting, or mostly hype?

Useful, mostly on steady and erratic fast sellers. In our test AI cut error about 40% against last year plus growth, but plain smoothing got most of that.1 In the M5 competition, gains at the item level averaged about 3%.2

Do we need to hire a data scientist?

Not to buy, because tools like StockTrim and ERP add-ons ship with built-in models.24,30 You need one to build your own, and internal builds succeed less often.40

How much sales history do we need?

You need at least 2 years of weekly sales to learn seasons, and 3 is better.23,42 New items need forecasts borrowed from similar items, or judgment.23

Can we run S&OP in Excel?

Yes, because S&OP is a monthly meeting process, not a tool.12,13 Excel is the most used forecasting tool in supply chains.37

What does demand forecasting software cost?

From free in Excel, to $199 per user a month for Forecast Pro Professional, to $300 per user a month for Dynamics 365 Premium.26,27,25 We read these on vendor pages on 26 Nov 2026. Cin7's add-on, NetSuite and Flieber publish no price.28,31,33

How do planners override the forecast without making it worse?

Rarely, with a written reason, and measure each override's value added.15,7 Large, downward, news-backed changes tend to help; small upward ones hurt.5,6

Should you buy demand forecasting software or build your own?

Buy: Buy when you have no data person and your items sell in common patterns. Most firms now buy: 76% of enterprise AI uses are bought, and bought tools succeed about twice as often.50,40 Start with your ERP's module or add-on, tested against Excel's free baseline.30,26

Build: Build when your data is the edge, such as customer sell-through or EDI orders a vendor won't use. Retailer data cut error 44.7% in one 2026 study.39 Keep baselines and data pipelines in-house, because managed services can close.48

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when items are mostly new or have under two years of history, demand is slow and lumpy, or orders come from contracts.23,4,39

  • Mostly new items, or under two years of history: forecast from similar items and judgment, not machine learning.23,42
  • Slow, lumpy spare parts: use exponential smoothing, Croston's method or a reorder point.4,3 In our backtest, smoothing beat AI on lumpy items.1
  • Make-to-order or contract demand: plan from customer orders and agreed schedules, or ask customers for their own forecast.39
  • Few items with steady demand: Excel's FORECAST.ETS may be enough.26 Check it with step 3 of the test above.
