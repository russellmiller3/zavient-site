Private equity value creation: where does AI pay in the 100-day plan?

Bottom line
  • Returns now hinge on operating profit (EBITDA) growth, not cheap debt or rising multiples.1,2
  • Lead with revenue: investors favour growth, and margin added almost nothing in the median buyout.5,4
  • Price is the strongest single lever, yet only 14% of real plans change pricing.9,3
  • AI drafts, ranks and flags; named people own targets, prices and credit calls.8,17
  • Finish and measure: plans that complete more actions return more, so count dollars, not hours.3,7

What does AI change in a private equity value creation plan?

AI speeds the analysis behind each move in a value creation plan, from pricing to collections.8,15 The plan lists the moves that grow operating profit during a private equity hold, each with an owner.23,3 Finishing makes or breaks it: plans that complete more of their actions earn higher returns.3

Who owns each lever when AI is in the plan?

The owner-CEO owns the targets, and a named manager owns each lever: commercial lead for price, sales leader for coverage, controller for cash.6,8

  • Owner-CEO: owns the plan's targets and reports them to the board.6 One operating partner told EY the company itself must own and execute the value creation plan.6
  • Commercial lead: sets price guardrails and approves quotes outside them.8 In a field study, AI price advice worked best when people priced the complex or unusual quotes.8
  • Sales leader: owns territories and account calls, while AI ranks accounts and drafts call plans.12 AI plus a human coach beat either one alone.12
  • Controller: owns credit holds, disputes and every number in the board pack, and tracks AI risks as NIST's framework asks.22 AI gets worse on tasks outside its strengths, so a named person checks it.17

What does your team do once AI takes the first pass?

Your team moves from building numbers to acting on them: checking exceptions, coaching reps and explaining results.7,13

  • Finance staff spend less time drafting: in one experiment, writing tasks took 37% less time.14 In one sponsored survey, 38% of operating partners said their firms put AI savings into strategic finance roles.7
  • Newer staff close the gap fastest: support agents with AI solved 15% more issues an hour, and the least experienced gained most.13 In sales coaching, middle-ranked reps gained most.12
  • Jobs change shape more than size: of US firms using AI, 66% use it only to help staff.18 A national study in Denmark ruled out earnings or hours effects above 2%.19

What does a 100-day AI plan look like in practice?

A 100-day AI plan spends 30 days on a baseline and data, 30 on two tested pilots, and the last 40 scaling what worked.23,3

Which 100-day build fits your company?

Pick by your biggest gap: revenue first if prices vary, cash and cost if cash is tight, back office if the close drags.9,7

1. Revenue first: price guardrails and sales coverage

Most companies that sell on quotes. A point of price is worth more than a point off any cost,9 yet only 14% of plans change pricing.3

  1. ControllerExport 24 months of quotes and invoices by customer and product
  2. Analyst with AIFind the price spread for like customers buying like products
  3. AIDraft guardrail price bands for each customer group
  4. Commercial leadApprove the bands and the override rules
  5. Sales repsPrice inside the bands, and give a reason code for each override
  6. Sales leaderAssign accounts from an AI ranking of fit and unsold products, with drafted call plans
  7. Operating partnerCompare margin each month against reps working without the tool

Cost: Mostly your team's time, since the data already sits in your quote and invoice systems. Field studies saw about 11% more profit from price advice.8

2. Cash and cost: collections and supplier spend

Companies with tight cash or large supplier spend. The evidence here is thin: one case study and one simulation.15,16

  1. AIRank open invoices by the risk of paying late
  2. AIDraft reminder emails for the collector to edit and send
  3. ControllerApprove credit holds and settle disputes
  4. AISort 12 months of supplier invoice lines into spend categories
  5. BuyerPick the top three categories to re-bid, and run the talks
  6. ControllerTrack days sales outstanding (DSO) each week against the baseline

Cost: Mostly team time plus any tool you buy. Each day of DSO is worth revenue divided by 365 in one-off cash (table below).

3. Back office: month-end close and board pack

Finance teams whose close takes weeks. In one sponsored survey, 53% of operating partners report AI agents that detect and escalate budget variances.7

  1. ControllerList the close steps and who signs each one
  2. AIDraft variance commentary and the first draft of the board pack
  3. Finance leadCheck every number and sign off
  4. ControllerLog close days and hours before and after

Cost: Mostly setup and review time. In a writing experiment, AI cut task time 37%.14

Which rules apply to AI in the plan?

No law governs the plan itself, but AI that talks to customers stays under consumer-protection law, and NIST offers a voluntary framework for tracking AI risk.27,22

RuleStatusWhat it means for you
FTC Act, as applied in Operation AI ComplyIn force; sweep announced 25 Sep 2024The US Federal Trade Commission says there is no AI exemption from the laws on the books.27 What your AI tells customers is held to the same truth-in-advertising rules as your staff.
Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149Decided 14 Feb 2024 (British Columbia tribunal)An airline was held liable for its chatbot's wrong answer to a customer.21 The tribunal rejected the idea that the bot was a separate entity.21 Any customer-facing AI in the plan needs a named owner.
NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkVoluntary guidanceIt asks that AI performance be measured against set criteria, that the system's limits be documented and that named personnel track its risks in use.22 Use it as the template for the owner map in your plan.

How do you prove an AI lever worked?

Set a do-nothing baseline, run each pilot beside a holdout group, and count the gap in dollars, not hours.23,7

  1. Record a do-nothing baseline before day 1: where margin, DSO and spend are heading with no new action.23
  2. Split the value bridge, which shows how much of the target comes from price, volume, cost and cash.23
  3. Pick a holdout group: one site, product line, region or set of reps keeps working without the tool.
  4. Write down the limits the AI must stay within, such as the lowest price it may suggest.22
  5. Compare pilot and holdout each month in dollars: margin per order, DSO days, spend per unit.
  6. Count hours saved too, but never alone. Only 38% of operating partners surveyed say their firms measure revenue or margin impact.7
  7. Track the share of plan actions finished, since that share predicts returns.3
  8. Report monthly to the operating partner and quarterly to the board. Scale only what beat its holdout.

Where does AI in a value creation plan go wrong?

AI in the plan goes wrong when teams start with the tool, skip the data work, or trust output no one checks.20,7

  • Starting with the tool, not the problem: RAND lists the wrong problem and technology-first starts among the root causes of AI project failure.20 Pick the lever from the value bridge (the target split by price, volume, cost and cash) first.
  • Bad data at close: operating partners in one sponsored survey rated only 6% of companies data-ready at close.7 Spend the first 30 days on data.
  • No playbook: 41% of operating partners in the same survey say their firms scale AI without one.7 Write the owner map before the first pilot.
  • Trusting AI outside its strengths: consultants using AI on a task beyond its reach were 19 points less likely to be right.17 A named person checks every number that reaches a customer or the board.
  • Hiding a sales bot: telling buyers about a bot cut purchases,28 yet you are liable for what it says.21 Disclose it honestly and give it an owner.
  • Rosy price maths: the sizer below assumes no lost volume, which flatters price moves.9 Test price bands on a holdout before you book the gain.

Questions CEOs and operating partners ask

What is a value creation plan in private equity?

A value creation plan is the list of moves meant to grow a company's value during the hold, each with an owner.23,3 In 1,136 real plans, 84% included operational improvement and 74% top-line growth.3

What is a 100-day plan in private equity?

A 100-day plan is the first stretch of the value creation plan, where the baseline is set and the first moves start.23 Plans keep growing after it: 55% add operational items after year one.3

What does an operating partner do?

An operating partner helps portfolio companies deliver the value creation plan, driving growth and margin work and spotting risks that can derail a deal.6

Where does AI fit in a value creation plan?

AI fits inside each lever rather than as a lever of its own, speeding pricing, selling, collections and reporting.8,11 Operating partners rate AI 3.8 out of 5 for priority but 3.1 for delivery.6

Will AI shrink the team at a portfolio company?

No, on current evidence: 66% of US firms using AI use it only to help staff.18 Only 2% saw AI-related job losses,18 and a Danish national study ruled out earnings or hours effects above 2%.19

How do you measure AI impact in a portfolio company?

Measure it in dollars against a do-nothing baseline and a holdout group, not in hours saved.23,7 Only 38% of operating partners surveyed say their firms measure revenue or margin impact, while 67% count time saved.7

Should you buy AI for the plan or build it?

Buy for standard work like collections and spend sorting.25 Build only where your own data, such as quote history, is the edge.8

Buy: Buy for standard work such as collections, spend sorting or close tooling: 76% of enterprise AI uses are now bought.25 Most companies lack a build team; operating partners in one survey say only 9% of sponsors run an AI center of excellence.7

Build: Build where your own data is the edge, such as quote history for price bands.8 Keep it small: one model on your own quotes, owned by your commercial lead and tested on a holdout.8,22

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you need one lever, an add-on integration or a faster close, not a whole plan.

  • One lever only, such as supplier spend: start with the AI procurement guide or a spend analysis tool, not a full plan. Expect modest gains there.16
  • Just closed an add-on: start with the AI post-merger integration guide. Missing or messy data is a top reason AI projects fail.20
  • Month-end across several entities is the bottleneck: start with the multi-entity close guide. Weak data foundations sink AI projects before any lever pays.20
  • Sales coverage is the only gap: most enterprise AI is now bought, so lead scoring in your customer relationship management (CRM) system may be enough.25 See the sales analytics guide.
