What is loan review, and what does AI change in it?

Loan review is the independent check that loans are graded right and problems surface early.1,2 Vendors report AI that reads every commercial file between reviews, drafts the reviewer's write-up with page citations and flags grade gaps.17,46,48 Clean core data and reviewers independent of lending make or break it.2,4

Where does your loan review team stay in charge?

Your reviewers stay in charge: AI drafts and flags, reviewers validate each line, the chief credit officer settles grade disputes and the board approves policy.1,2,16

  • Loan reviewer: checks every AI flag and draft against the cited pages before it enters the official file.17 At Old National, AI output is not examiner-visible, and reviewers validate it (Abrigo case, vendor-reported, 2026).16
  • Loan review stays independent of lending: reviewers don't originate the loans and aren't paid on the ratings.1 Keep the AI's prompts, thresholds and flag list with loan review, not lenders, to protect that independence.1
  • Chief credit officer: settles lender-versus-reviewer grade disputes through an appeal process, which the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) expects.2
  • Board: approves the loan review policy each year and receives the findings, typically every quarter.1,2

What do reviewers do once AI takes the first pass?

Reviewers move from reading every page to checking flagged and larger credits, visiting watch-list borrowers and reporting trends to the board.18,21,1

  • Reviewers move from reading every page to checking flagged and larger credits. Teams on automated software report 4.6 files per reviewer a week, against 3.6 manual (Abrigo survey, vendor).18
  • Watch-list loans get borrower calls and site visits, since soft information is where AI helps least.21,28
  • Reviewers spend more time on trends for the board and the allowance for credit losses (ACL).1 Seacoast aims to automate every nonjudgmental part of credit risk review by 2028 (Abrigo case, vendor-reported).17
  • Headcount isn't the goal: of firms using AI, 66% only use it to help staff, and only 2% of firms report AI-related job losses.20

Core data and loan files go in, and a ranked flag list with page citations comes out for a reviewer to accept or reject.17,1

Which way to add AI to loan review fits your bank?

Start with an add-on if you run loan review software, platform monitoring if it holds your covenants, or your own rules if you have core data and an analyst.15,47,49

1. Small team already on loan review software

One to three reviewers, often at a $250M to $1B bank, already on a loan review platform with an AI module. Abrigo reports Old National and Seacoast use one with human validation (vendor-reported, 2026).15,17

  1. Loan review officerSwitch on the AI module and choose what it drafts
  2. Credit adminLoad the trial balance, statements and covenant certificates
  3. AIDraft each line sheet, the reviewer's write-up of one loan, with page citations
  4. ReviewerCheck each line against the cited pages, then accept or reject
  5. Chief credit officerSign grade changes after the lender appeal step
  6. Loan review officerReport findings to the board each quarter

Cost: Most AI modules carry no published price, so expect a vendor quote.19,46 The lowest published loan review software price is LenderAnalyzer's, from $99 a month, read 26 Nov 2026.45

2. Mid-size bank with a covenant-heavy commercial book

A $1B to $5B bank, heavy in commercial real estate (CRE), whose loan platform already tracks covenants and statement dates. Interagency guidance lets key risk indicators set how often and how deeply you review.1

  1. Credit adminMap covenants and statement due dates in the platform
  2. AITest covenants and flag deteriorating credits every month
  3. Loan reviewReview flagged loans first, then the usual scope list
  4. LenderAnswer each flag in writing
  5. Chief credit officerApprove or reject grade changes
  6. Loan review officerLog every rating change, a risk indicator OCC examiners track

Cost: Monitoring modules from nCino and Moody's publish no price, so cost comes from a quote.46,47 As a floor, published loan review software starts from $99 a month, read 26 Nov 2026.45

3. Your own rules on core data, with an analyst

A bank or credit union with a data analyst and core extracts, often next to an outsourced annual review. Fixed rules fall outside SR 26-2's definition of a model.49

  1. Data analystPull payments, extensions, overdrafts, maturity changes and statement dates from the core
  2. RulesFlag stale financials, repeated extensions, overdrafts and maturity changes with no renewal memo
  3. ReviewerTriage the list and pull the files
  4. Internal auditReconcile a sample of core fields to loan documents
  5. Risk managementDocument the rules and the test results

Cost: We found no public cost figure for an in-house build, so price it as analyst time on tools you own. For scale, published loan review software starts from $99 a month, read 26 Nov 2026.45

How do you test AI loan review before you trust it?

Run the AI blind on last year's closed reviews, count missed downgrades, check its numbers against source pages, then run one cycle in parallel.18,37,16

  1. Record today's numbers: files per reviewer a week, commercial dollars covered, grade changes per review and exam findings. Surveyed teams average 3.6 files a week manual, 4.6 automated (vendor survey).18
  2. Build a test set from last year's closed reviews, with every downgrade and every loan examiners graded lower. Examiner-versus-management gaps were the warning sign at Metropolitan Capital.37
  3. Run the AI blind on that set and compare its flags with the final grades. Count missed downgrades first, then false flags.
  4. Check every number it quotes against the source page. The best model scored 82.4% on financial spreadsheets, and complex synthesis averaged 0.31.24,25
  5. Check the grades it suggests against how those loans actually performed, as the OCC expects of any rating system.13
  6. Run one review cycle in parallel: reviewers do their normal sample while AI reads every loan. Keep AI output out of the official file until a reviewer validates it.16,17
  7. Pilot one segment first, such as commercial real estate, and widen to the whole book only after a clean parallel cycle.
  8. Test the data feed, not just the model: reconcile maturity dates and past-due status to loan documents. The OCC found Lindsay's CEO altered exactly those core fields.34
  9. Price the lift: reviewer hours freed times loaded cost, plus commercial dollars newly reviewed. Old National's 15-40% gain by review type is a vendor-reported reference only.15
  10. Document it for examiners: list the tool with your lending models and third parties, plus the validation results.2

Where does AI loan review go wrong?

AI loan review goes wrong when optimistic grades go unchallenged, extensions hide trouble, core records are altered, lenders tune the tool, or numbers come out confidently wrong.37,38,34

  • Optimistic grades go unchallenged: Metropolitan Capital's management routinely applied unreasonable risk scores.37 Compare each grade to its numbers and send gaps to loan review, not the lender.
  • Extensions and deferrals hide trouble: examiners cited liberal deferrals and extensions, and weak loan review, at Community Bank & Trust.38 Flag repeated extensions as a trigger.
  • The records themselves can be wrong: the OCC found Lindsay's CEO changed maturity dates and past-due status in the core.34,33 Reconcile core fields to signed loan documents.
  • Independence leaks if lenders tune the tool.1,4 Loan review should own the prompts, thresholds and flag list.
  • Confident wrong numbers: accuracy falls 18.60% on multi-period work.26 Require page citations and recompute ratios with fixed formulas.
  • Capacity mistaken for quality: more files per reviewer is not a better catch rate, as Abrigo's own survey warns.18 Measure the catch rate on downgrades, and treat vendor speed claims as claims.18

Questions credit officers ask about AI loan review

How much of the loan portfolio should loan review cover?

No regulator sets a fixed minimum share for your own review, though Fed examiners sample at least 10% at $10B to $100B state member banks.1,14 The FDIC tells its examiners the right loans in the sample matter more than the percentage.4 The Fed expects internal review to cover substantially more than the examiners' sample.14

How often should loans be reviewed?

Significant loans are typically reviewed once a year, at renewal, or more often when risk rises.1,5 Key risk indicators can raise or lower that frequency.1

Can a small bank or credit union do loan review in-house?

Yes, if reviewers are independent of origination and not paid on the ratings, or you use a qualified third party.1 Credit unions generally use third-party reviewers.42

Will examiners accept AI-assisted loan review?

Yes, if reviewers stay in charge: the OCC's November 2026 framework sets no new expectations, and the NCUA treats AI like any other technology.8,9 Keep reviewer sign-off and validation records.16

Is AI loan review a model under model risk management?

Only partly, because SR 26-2, which replaced SR 11-7, leaves generative AI out of scope and excludes fixed rules.7,49 The OCC still expects lending models under model risk management.2

What's the difference between loan review and internal audit?

Loan review grades credit risk, while internal audit tests whether controls work, loan review included.1 The guidance keeps the two functions separate.1

Should you buy AI loan review or build it?

Buy the file reading and line-sheet drafting, and build the core-data flags that loan review owns.43,49

Buy: Buy the file reading and line-sheet drafting: 76% of enterprise AI solutions were bought in 2025, up from 53%.43 Bought or partnered pilots reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds.44 Loan review platforms already ship document AI.17,19

Build: Build the flags that run on your own core data, where the rules are yours and easy to explain.49 Fixed rules sit outside SR 26-2's model definition, and 84% of small banks have never posted an AI job, so keep the build simple.49,21

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when your book is mostly consumer loans, you have one reviewer, your core data is unreliable, or you need an independent annual opinion.41,45,34

  • Mostly consumer or small, similar loans: track delinquency by loan type and by the year loans were made, in your core or allowance software. One Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFI) Fund memo suggests 10-15% sampling for similar products.41
  • A few hundred commercial loans and one reviewer: a covenant reminder report and a stale-financials report may be enough. Low-cost loan review software starts from $99 a month (vendor price, read 26 Nov 2026).45
  • Core data you can't trust: fix the data first, with a reconciliation project that internal audit tests. Altered core fields hid Lindsay's bad loans.34
  • You need an independent annual opinion for the board or examiners: keep an independent reviewer, in-house or outsourced, and let AI support them.1
