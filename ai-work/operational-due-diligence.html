What is operational due diligence on a fund manager, and what does AI change?

Operational due diligence (ODD) checks whether a fund manager's business, not its strategy, can be trusted.15,6 AI pulls each manager's due diligence questionnaire (DDQ) answers into one grid and flags what is missing.18,19 Judgment still decides, because a DDQ is the manager's own account.7,14

Who at the office makes the call on a manager?

People keep every call on the manager: the committee votes, and the chief operating officer (COO) or chief investment officer (CIO) owns the ODD view.1,16 Counsel reviews the limited partnership agreement and side letters and flags off-market terms.16,23

  • Investment committee: votes on the commitment and its size. In the Standards Board for Alternative Investments (SBAI) survey, the committee is the main reader of ODD findings.1
  • COO or CIO: owns the ODD view where there is no dedicated team. In SBAI's survey, 54% of such investors put ODD under the CIO and 23% under the COO.1
  • Operations lead: calls the administrator and auditor directly instead of trusting the questionnaire.14,15 In Deutsche Bank's 2014 survey of hedge fund investors, 92% named the fund administrator the most important outside provider to contact.14
  • Counsel: reviews the limited partnership agreement (LPA) and side letters, and flags terms that stray from market standard.16,23

What does your team do once AI takes the first pass?

Your team moves from copying answers into spreadsheets to chasing gaps, confirming facts with outside parties and writing the judgment.19,14

  • Analysts stop retyping answers and start chasing the gaps the grid shows.19,18
  • The operations lead spends the saved time on confirmations, visits and background checks.14,15
  • The memo writer keeps the judgment: CENTRL's case study says the client kept analyst time for judgment-heavy work.19
  • Where AI changed tasks, 66% of US firms use it only to help staff, and AI-related job losses hit only 2% of AI-using firms.26

Which diligence setup fits your office?

Offices that vet a handful of managers a year should build a grid on the ILPA template, and busier offices should buy a diligence platform.6,1

1. A few managers a year: a grid on the ILPA template

Offices with no dedicated ODD team, like 30% of the investors in SBAI's survey.1 In Campden Wealth and RBC's 2026 survey of 155 offices across the Americas, 35% would like AI help choosing managers.5

  1. COOSend every manager the ILPA DDQ 2.0 and its document list
  2. AIPull each manager's answers into one grid, each cell linked to its page
  3. AnalystCheck every blank and every 'not found' by hand
  4. Operations leadConfirm the administrator, auditor and cash controls directly
  5. COOWrite the approve, condition or veto view for the investment committee

Cost: The ILPA DDQ 2.0 is free, so the main cost is staff time on gaps and confirmations.6,14

2. Many managers a year: a diligence platform

Offices with a steady flow of private equity, credit and hedge fund reviews, where reading long document packs is the bottleneck.19,3

  1. COOLoad the office's questionnaire and red-flag rules into the platform
  2. Platform AIFill answers from each manager's existing DDQ and send back only the gaps
  3. AnalystApprove each extracted answer against its source page
  4. AICheck DDQ claims against the data room and filings, and list mismatches
  5. Operations leadConfirm with the administrator and auditor, and run background checks
  6. Investment committeeDecide, with the ODD view in the memo

Cost: DiligenceVault, CENTRL and Ping publish no price on the pages we read.18,19,20

Which rules shape due diligence on fund managers?

No rule tells a single family office how to vet managers, and the rule that would have forced standard quarterly fund statements was vacated in 2024.25,11

How do you test AI on a past manager review before you trust it?

Rerun one finished manager review with AI, then count the answers it lined up right, the gaps it caught and the gaps it missed.19,24

  1. Pick one manager review you finished last year, with the DDQ, the documents and your final memo.
  2. Record today's numbers: days from DDQ received to decision, and hours spent building the comparison. In SBAI's survey of 44 institutional investors, 41% don't always have enough time for a review.1
  3. Have AI build the grid from the raw DDQ and documents, without your memo.
  4. Score every cell against your own grid: right, wrong or missed.
  5. Check every 'not found' by hand. In the Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset (CUAD) benchmark, 70 to 80% of clause checks have no matching clause, so absence is the usual answer.24
  6. Test questions that span documents on their own: in the Vals legal test, tools struggled most when answers sat across several documents.22
  7. Price the lift: hours saved times loaded cost, plus weeks cut from the cycle. CENTRL's client reports DDQ cycles cut from 10 weeks to 6, by the vendor's account.19

Where does AI-assisted manager diligence go wrong?

AI-assisted diligence goes wrong when a tidy grid is taken as proof, because the grid only repeats what the manager wrote.7,13

  • Taking the grid as proof: ILPA says yes/no answers give only a top-level view, not a basis for investing.7 Confirm key facts with the administrator and auditor directly.14
  • Missed clauses: in contract benchmarks most clause checks find no clause, so a 'not found' may really sit in an appendix or side letter.24 Have a person check every gap.22
  • Mixed bases: returns with and without subscription lines, or gross and net, lined up in one column.9 Label the basis in every cell, as ILPA's Performance Template does.10
  • Missing the costly risk: Capco put half of over 100 hedge fund failures down to operational risk.13 Castle Hall counted $80 billion of investor losses across 327 operational failures.13
  • Pressure to approve: just under a quarter of SBAI respondents have felt pressed to approve despite known risks.1 Write the veto rule down before the review starts.15

Questions buyers ask about operational due diligence

What is operational due diligence in private equity?

Operational due diligence checks a private equity manager's business: its controls, administrator, valuation, compliance and people.15,6 Investment diligence, which judges the strategy and track record, runs beside it.15,16

Is fund operational due diligence the same as M&A due diligence?

No, because fund ODD vets a manager you hand money to, while mergers and acquisitions (M&A) diligence vets a company you buy.15 For acquisitions, read our AI due diligence guide.29

What goes on an operational due diligence checklist?

An ODD checklist covers ownership, people, compliance, valuation, cash controls, service providers and legal terms.15 ILPA's DDQ 2.0 spreads these over 20 sections and asks for 80 distinct documents, each if applicable.6

How long does operational due diligence take?

For family offices, operational and legal due diligence takes 4 to 12 weeks, says Private Markets Group.17 The whole manager decision runs from two weeks to twelve months.16,17 An older benchmark: in Deutsche Bank's 2014 survey of hedge fund investors, most said an initial ODD review takes two to eight weeks.14

Can AI fill in or compare a DDQ for us?

Yes, AI can pull each manager's answers into one grid and flag blanks, but a person must check every gap.19,7 Vendor tools cite the source page for each answer, so a person can check it against the page.18,20

Should we use the ILPA DDQ or our own questionnaire?

Start from the ILPA DDQ so answers line up across managers, then add your own follow-up questions.6,7 In SBAI's survey, 59% of investors use their own questionnaire and 14% use industry templates.1

Should you buy a diligence platform or build your own grid?

Buy a platform when reviews pile up past what one person can read, and build a light grid when you vet only a few managers a year.1,18

Buy: Buy a diligence platform when reviews pile up: SBAI found investors under $5bn use no third-party tools, while larger ones sometimes do.1 Vendors such as DiligenceVault claim review time falls by over 70 percent.18 Across US enterprise AI decision-makers, 76% of AI use cases are now bought rather than built.27

Build: Build a light grid when you vet a handful of managers a year.6 The ILPA DDQ is free, and about 39% of it lines up without judgment, by our count.6 For organizations MIT NANDA studied, partnered builds reached deployment about 67% of the time, against about 33% for internal ones.28

When should an office pick something else?

Pick something else when you are buying a company rather than a fund stake, or when an outside adviser already runs manager diligence for you.29,2

  • Buying a company, not a fund stake: that is M&A diligence, covered in our AI due diligence guide.29
  • An adviser already runs manager selection: 41% of offices using outside investment advisers rely on them for due diligence and manager access.2 J.P. Morgan finds 80% of 333 single family offices outsource some of their portfolio, for example to outsourced CIOs or for manager selection.4
  • Your pain starts after you commit: fund statements and quarterly reports belong in our family office reporting guide.30
  • You vet only one or two managers a year: a shared spreadsheet on the ILPA template, filled by hand, may be enough.6
