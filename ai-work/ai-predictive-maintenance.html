What does predictive maintenance software do, and what does AI change?

Predictive maintenance software reads machine data, like vibration and temperature, plus work-order history, and flags machines starting to fail.3,11 What AI changes: it spots fault trends early, so planners book repairs before breakdowns; DOE puts predictive at 8-12% cheaper than scheduled maintenance.11,3 False alarms decide whether it pays.4

Who confirms the alert and makes the call?

A technician confirms each alert on the machine, the planner decides when the repair happens, and the maintenance manager signs off run-or-stop calls.9,10,13

  • Technician: confirms each alert on the machine, with a handheld reading or a look and listen, before parts are ordered. Inspection is one of the calls ISO 17359 lists after an alert.9,10
  • Maintenance planner: decides when the repair happens, around production. The standard's options run from keep monitoring to bring maintenance forward or stop the machine.10
  • Maintenance manager: picks which machines are watched, sets alarm levels and signs off run-or-stop calls. Time to failure still needs expert judgment, per NIST's summary of ISO 13381-1.13
  • Electrical gear stays with qualified people: NFPA 70B wants corrective action decided and recorded by them. OSHA lockout rules still cover every repair an alert triggers.14,15

What does your team do after?

Your team moves from firefighting to planned work: alerts give lead time, so the planner books repairs before breakdowns, and technicians write fuller work orders.16,17

  • From firefighting to planned work: an alert gives lead time, so the planner books the job before the breakdown does. In MaintainX's vendor survey of maintenance leaders, half of teams spend under 40% of their time on planned work.16
  • From tribal knowledge to written work orders: 74% of maintenance professionals in Limble's vendor survey say one person alone can repair a critical asset. Better notes feed the model and keep that know-how.17,18
  • Technicians make the model better. In McKinsey's offshore case, maintenance teams and data scientists removed false alarms failure by failure.19,20
  • Jobs grow: BLS projects maintenance mechanic jobs up 14% to 2035. Among AI-using firms, 66% only use it to help staff; 2% saw AI-related employment fall.21,22

What does an AI maintenance alert look like?

An AI maintenance alert names the machine and the fault trend, matches it to past work orders, and suggests a next step a technician must confirm first.10 Each alert also carries a confidence level.4

Predictive or preventive maintenance: which fits each machine?

Use calendar PM for parts that wear out with use, condition checks for parts that fail at random with warning, and run-to-failure for cheap, redundant parts.23,3 Trial AI prediction on critical machines with known failure modes.12

In airline and navy studies, most failures were random, so calendar work alone can't prevent them.1,23 Those figures come from aircraft and ships, not plant averages, so treat them as a guide.24

Which way to start fits your maintenance team?

Start from work orders if PM is shaky. Add open sensors to 5-10 critical machines if someone likes data, or use a managed service if nobody reads vibration data.29,12

1. No sensors yet: start from your work orders

PM completion is shaky, the CMMS (maintenance software) is half-used, or there's no hardware budget. Deloitte says get the basics in place first; in Limble's vendor survey of maintenance professionals, 39% report consistent CMMS use across the team.29,17

  1. Maintenance plannerExport 2-3 years of work orders to a spreadsheet
  2. Reliability leadTag them with NIST's free Nestor tool, or an AI chatbot checked by hand, to find repeat failures by asset31,18
  3. Maintenance managerRank assets by criticality and repeat failures, and pick the top 10 within a month
  4. PlannerReview each PM task on those assets; keep it if the wear is age-related, else switch it to an inspection1,23
  5. TechnicianAdd a "how was this found" field to every new work order6

Cost: Staff time and the CMMS you already pay for. Nestor is free.31

2. Five to ten critical machines: open sensors you run

You have a technician or engineer who likes data, and you want to own the data. Simple thresholds often do the job before any model.12

  1. Maintenance managerChoose up to 10 critical rotating machines from recipe 1
  2. TechnicianMount sensors and check the mounts monthly
  3. Reliability leadSet simple alarm thresholds first, such as a fixed vibration level that triggers an alert12
  4. TechnicianFor 90 days, confirm every alert by hand and log it as caught, false or unclear
  5. Maintenance managerReview the alert log monthly and adjust thresholds

Cost: About $4,200 to $5,550 in list-price hardware for 10-12 points, before installation, per our 26 Nov 2026 check.7,8 Software runs from $70 a year (Monnit Premiere) to bring your own (NCD).32,33

3. No one reads vibration data: a managed service

No one on staff can read vibration data. On MaintainX already, its Asset Intelligence add-on brings machine data into the CMMS, price on request.34

  1. Maintenance managerShortlist 2 services and ask for a trial on your own machines; KCF offers a $199, 30-day trial of 48 sensors35
  2. Vendor analystSends a diagnosis with each alert
  3. TechnicianConfirms on the machine
  4. PlannerBooks the repair
  5. Maintenance managerTracks confirmed catches and false alarms against the contract

Cost: Mostly quote-only: Tractian, Augury, Waites and Siemens Senseye publish no price.36,30,37,38 Petasense's Starter plan lists at $999 a month, billed yearly, as read on 26 Nov 2026.39

NFPA 70B covers electrical maintenance where adopted, OSHA lockout covers every repair, ISO 17359 guides the monitoring program, and CISA and NIST cover plant-network security.14,15,9,48,49

How do you run a fair 90-day pilot?

Pick 5-10 critical machines, record a 12-month baseline, fix the alarm rule before day one, log every alert as caught or false, then decide per machine at day 90.17,9,4,12

  1. Pick 5-10 critical machines at one site whose failures are random and costly, using the table above.1,29
  2. Record 12 months of baseline for those machines: unplanned downtime hours, emergency work orders and actual PM completion. Maintenance leaders in Limble's vendor survey estimate their teams complete 54% of scheduled PM.17
  3. Add a "how was this found" field to every work order: inspection, alert, operator or breakdown. One study found it missing on 60-98% of work orders at four companies.6
  4. Write the alarm rule down before day one, so nobody moves the goalposts.9
  5. Log every alert as caught (confirmed by a technician), false or unclear, with the lead time before failure and the technician hours it took.4,50
  6. Count avoided downtime only on confirmed catches, at your own cost per downtime hour. Skip survey averages: Siemens' figures come from the world's 500 largest firms.51
  7. Net value is avoided downtime, minus false-alarm visits and needless stops, minus a year of fees. McKinsey saw a 10% false-alarm rate add 1,000 cases a year and erase the savings.4
  8. At day 90, decide per machine: keep it, switch to a simple threshold, or go back to scheduled checks.12

Where does predictive maintenance go wrong?

Predictive maintenance goes wrong through false alarms, dirty work orders, alerts nobody acts on, sensors without context, vendor exits and over-maintaining.4,5,40,1

  • False alarms: in a McKinsey example, a model catching a quarter of breakdowns still lost money when 10% of its alerts were false.4,50 Log every alert, and tune or drop noisy machines.
  • Dirty work orders: NIST found entry errors are non-random and skew maintenance KPIs (key performance indicators), even with large samples.5,52 Clean the history and add a how-found field before you train anything.
  • No one acts on the alert. DOE warns the savings are "not readily seen by management", so report confirmed catches monthly.3
  • Sensors bought before a plan. Start from asset criticality: ISO 17359 builds monitoring on a cost-benefit and criticality review, and simple thresholds often do the job.9,12
  • Vendor exits: Amazon closed Monitron to new customers in Oct 2024 and shut Lookout for Equipment on 7 Oct 2026.40,53 Ask who owns your data and how you export it.
  • Over-maintaining: NASA notes scheduled overhauls can raise failure rates by adding early-life failures after the work.1 Drop PM tasks that don't match how the part fails.

Questions buyers ask about predictive maintenance

Predictive vs preventive maintenance: which should come first?

Preventive for parts that wear with use; condition checks for parts that fail at random, which is most of them in reliability-centred maintenance studies.1,23 DOE puts predictive savings at 8-12% over preventive.3

Is predictive maintenance worth it, or mostly a sales pitch?

On a few critical machines with costly random failures, often yes; across the whole plant, often no.12 A 10% false-positive rate erased the savings in one McKinsey example.4

Do we need sensors, or can we start with the data we have?

Start with your work orders: find repeat failures and fix the PM.18 NIST's free Nestor tool tags free-text work orders.31 Then add sensors to the few machines that justify them.12

How accurate is our CMMS data, really?

Often less than reports suggest: NIST found entry errors hide inside KPIs.5 One technician wrote, "Management thinks we're 90% compliant. We're maybe 40%." Clean it before you train any model.52

Is a sensor subscription worth it if it saves one or two breakdowns a year?

Only if one avoided breakdown costs more than a year of fees plus the false-alarm visits.4 Test it on your own downtime cost, not survey averages from much larger firms.51

What does a 5-10 machine pilot cost?

Our 26 Nov 2026 check found self-run hardware for 10-12 points at about $4,200 to $5,550 list.7,8 KCF offers a $199, 30-day trial, and most managed services publish no price.35,36

Should you buy predictive maintenance software or build it?

Buy: Buy when you have no data team and your machines are standard motors, pumps, fans and gearboxes. Most firms buy: 76% of enterprise AI use cases are bought, per Menlo Ventures.54 Internal builds succeed about a third as often as bought or partnered tools, in MIT research on AI pilots.55

Build: Build only for fleets of identical machines or a process no vendor knows. McKinsey's offshore case took 10-15 data scientists two years and 500+ models.19 The middle path: buy open hardware such as NCD or Monnit, keep the data, and start with thresholds.33,12

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when parts wear on a known life, one failure mode needs only a threshold alarm, your CMMS is barely used, or the gear is electrical distribution.1,12,17,14

  • Parts that wear with use and have a known life: keep time-based PM.1,23
  • One narrow, well-known failure mode: a simple threshold alarm in the sensor's own software.12,32
  • Low PM completion or a barely used CMMS: fix that first with planned routes in the CMMS you have. Deloitte says get the basics in place before predictive.29,17
  • Electrical distribution gear: a scheduled NFPA 70B infrared program before any AI.14,25
