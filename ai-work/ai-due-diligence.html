What is AI due diligence for acquisitions, and what does it change?

AI due diligence uses AI to read a target's data room first, pulling contract terms, numbers and gaps with page cites.15,16 It changes speed and coverage, so your team checks more and asks the seller better questions.2,30 Human checking of every flag makes or breaks it.6

Where do your deal team and counsel stay in charge?

Your team keeps every judgment: corporate development owns the deal call, the controller checks the numbers, and counsel signs legal findings.2,5

  • Head of corporate development: owns the scope, the request list and the go/no-go call. Surveyed acquirers rank doing more diligence in the time available as AI's top goal.2
  • Controller: ties every number AI pulls to the target's trial balance and working-capital schedule. Post-closing price adjustments are nearly universal in private-target deals, and working capital is the most common measure.17,39
  • Outside counsel: checks AI output for accuracy, then signs legal findings and the disclosure schedule. Under American Bar Association (ABA) Opinion 512, lawyers using AI keep their duties of competence and confidentiality.5
  • Compliance lead: decides whether to disclose misconduct found at the target. The Department of Justice (DOJ) safe harbor needs disclosure within six months of closing.9,10

What does your deal team do once AI takes the first pass?

Your deal team moves from first reads to seller questions, full contract review and earlier integration planning.2,4

  • Deal leads move from first reads to follow-up questions for the seller and management interviews. Surveyed acquirers rank doing more diligence in the time first, and headcount reduction last.2
  • Counsel can cover more contracts, because AI fills the contract grid first and links each answer to its source.23,30
  • Corporate development starts the integration plan earlier: acquirers now use AI to size a target's cost base from public sources before bidding.4
  • Headcount isn't the goal: only 2% of AI-using firms report AI-related employment decreases, and about 96% see no change in employment.12,13

The seller's data room goes in, and a cited contract grid, a working-capital check and a gap list come out for your team to check.15,16

Which setup fits how many add-ons you buy?

Start with AI seats and the seller's room AI if you buy a few add-ons a year, a shared playbook for several, or a contract-review tool for hundreds of contracts.26,20,23

1. A few add-ons a year, a small deal team

A few deals a year, a deal team of a few people, and sellers whose data rooms already have AI built in.20,21

  1. CounselClear the non-disclosure agreement (NDA) and the AI tool's retention terms before any file goes in
  2. Corporate development leadLoad the request list and the data-room index
  3. AICompare the room with the request list and draft a gap list with page cites
  4. ControllerTie every number AI pulls to the target's trial balance
  5. CounselRead every change-of-control and assignment hit
  6. Corporate development leadTurn gaps into seller questions and the go/no-go memo

Cost: Claude Team lists at $20 to $25 per member a month, read 26 Nov 2026.26 By our math, five seats cost $1,200 to $1,500 a year, plus the seller's room AI.20

2. Several add-ons a year on one playbook

Several deals a year run on the same checklist, where your past issue lists are the edge.20

  1. Corporate development leadWrite the standard request list, prompts and issue-list format once
  2. CounselApprove which AI assistant may connect to data rooms
  3. AIWork inside each room through the vendor's connector, under the room's permissions
  4. ControllerRun the same working-capital check on every deal
  5. CounselSign legal findings and the disclosure-schedule questions
  6. COOCarry flagged issues into the integration plan

Cost: Data rooms price per deal or by data volume and users.20,21 Claude Team seats list at $20 a member a month billed yearly, read 26 Nov 2026.26

3. Hundreds of similar contracts in one room

Customer, supplier or lease contracts number in the hundreds, and change-of-control consents decide the deal.23

  1. CounselPick the clause fields to extract
  2. AIFill a contract grid with each answer linked to its source page
  3. CounselCheck every hit and a sample of blanks
  4. ControllerTie contract values to revenue by customer
  5. Corporate development leadPrice the consents needed before closing

Cost: Kira and Hebbia publish no price, so expect a demo and a quote.23,24 The review usually runs through counsel's own bill.

How do you test AI on a closed deal before you trust it?

Rerun your last closed deal's data room through the tool, score every finding as found, missed or false, then price the time and issues it saved.7,6

  1. Record today's numbers on your last closed deal: deal-team hours, days to the issue list, and issues found after signing.
  2. Rerun that closed deal's data room through the tool, since you already know the answers.
  3. Score each finding as found, missed or false, with the page it cites. Vals scored tools against a lawyer baseline on the same tasks.7
  4. Count hallucinations, not just hits: the Stanford-led study scored answers correct, incomplete or hallucinated.6
  5. Check every hit against its cited page, as NIST's generative AI profile suggests for sources and citations.11
  6. Run a sample of the free CUAD and MAUD test sets for a public baseline.28,29
  7. Price the lift: hours saved, plus each issue found before signing, valued at the price cut or claim it would have cost. Nearly three in ten private deals see an indemnity claim, and median escrow is 10% of deal value.38,39
  8. Decide with counsel and the controller to keep, change or stop.

Where does AI-assisted due diligence go wrong?

AI-assisted diligence goes wrong through confident wrong answers, leaked seller files, overstated AI at the target, and diligence cut short because AI was fast.6,15,36,14

  • Confident wrong answers: commercial legal research tools hallucinated 17% to 33% of the time in a preregistered test.6 Require a page cite for every finding, and have a person check each one.11
  • Strong at answering questions, weak at markup: lawyers beat every tool at redlining, 79.7 to at most 65.0, in the Vals benchmark.7 Keep contract markup with counsel.
  • Leaking the seller's data: exporting data-room files to an outside AI tool creates uncontrolled copies (Datasite's view, vendor).15 Check the NDA and the tool's retention terms first.5
  • Buying overstated AI: Presto did not tell investors that its voice AI ran on a third party's technology.36 Ask the target for usage logs, vendor contracts and human-help rates.
  • Buying a model you must delete: FTC orders made Everalbum and Rite Aid delete algorithms.43,48 Ask how each model's training data was collected and what consent covers it.
  • Cutting diligence because AI is fast: acquirers that did less diligence saw weaker post-deal results and more goodwill write-downs.14 Spend the saved time on deeper checks.

Questions buyers ask about AI in M&A due diligence

Can AI do due diligence?

Only as a first pass, because AI reads and flags while people check and decide.30,5 Legal research AI tools hallucinated 17% to 33% of the time in a preregistered test.6

How is AI used in M&A due diligence?

Large acquirers use AI most often to review legal agreements, manage workstreams and review financial statements.2 In one 2026 survey of large serial acquirers, 79% used or piloted it on legal agreements.2

Will AI replace due diligence jobs?

No, the evidence points to help rather than job loss: only 2% of AI-using US firms report AI-related employment decreases.12 About 96% of AI-using firms report no change in employment.13 In M&A, surveyed acquirers rank headcount reduction last among AI goals.2

Is it safe to put data-room files into ChatGPT?

Only after counsel checks the NDA and the tool's retention terms, because lawyers must keep client information confidential.5 OpenAI says ChatGPT Business data is never used for training (vendor).25 Datasite warns that exporting files creates uncontrolled copies (vendor).15

How much does AI due diligence software cost?

General AI seats list at $20 to $25 per member a month for Claude Team and $21 for the Copilot add-on, read 26 Nov 2026.26,27 Data-room AI is quoted per deal, and contract-review tools such as Kira publish no price.20,21,23

What does due diligence on a target's own AI check?

Diligence on a target's AI checks how it uses AI, who owns AI-made work, and whether its AI policies hold up.19,18 Our count found 111 public merger-agreement documents mentioning "artificial intelligence" from 1 Jan to 25 Nov 2026.1

Should you buy diligence tools or build your own setup?

Buy the tools and build the playbook: your request list, prompts and issue-list format, run the same way on every deal.40,20

Buy: Buy the tools: 76% of enterprise AI use cases are now bought rather than built, up from 53% in 2024.40 For a few deals a year, the seller's room AI plus business AI seats is enough.20,26

Build: Build the playbook, not the software: your request list, prompts and issue-list format, run the same way every deal.20 Data rooms now let your approved assistant work inside the room (Datasite, vendor).15

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you need earnings checked, want to insure the risk, have one deal in a seller's room, or face hundreds of similar contracts.41,42

  • You need earnings checked, not files read: get a quality-of-earnings (QoE) report, which vets normalized earnings and working capital.41 AI can prepare the data for it.
  • To shift the risk: representations and warranties insurance (RWI) was referenced in 63% of private-target deals in the ABA's 2025 study, up from 55%.42
  • One deal in a seller's room: use the room's own AI, such as Datasite Blueflame or the Intralinks assistant, before building anything.16,21
  • Hundreds of similar contracts: a contract-review tool such as Litera Kira, with 1,400+ pre-built fields (vendor), run through counsel.23
