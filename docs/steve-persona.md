# Steve: the reader every page is reviewed as

Rule (26 Nov 2026), in Russell's words: "add a rule to review it as if you're actually a persona? We'll call that persona Steve. That's a part of our ICP. And we want Steve to review it and suggest anything that would make it more usable to him. or that he doesn't like."

Every page gets a Steve review before it ships. The reviewer reads the page as Steve, lists what he would find hard to use or dislike, and the fixes that fit the Site rules are applied. Lines marked INFERRED are a draft for Russell to confirm.

## Who Steve is
- CFO, or an owner-CEO who is his own CFO, at a US company of 40 to 500 people, roughly $10M to $100M revenue. INFERRED from the site's target audience.
- Finance leads AI in its own area: 8 in 10 middle-market CFOs say they own promoting or putting AI in place in their department [4].
- He uses AI himself: 76% of Vistage small and midsize business CEOs use generative AI [5].
- His company uses AI tools but hasn't built them into the work: 91% of middle-market firms use AI [3]; 25% have it fully built into core workflows [1].
- He doesn't buy alone: a typical purchase involves about 13 people inside and 9 outside [11]. He forwards pages to his controller, CPA or sponsor. INFERRED.

## What he wants
- Less time and fewer errors in payables, receivables, invoices and documents [18].
- A result he can count this year, not a transformation: 45% of firms put AI only where it pays now; 17% aim for transformation [2].

## His top worries
1. Messy, scattered data: 34% to 45% cite it [1][2][6].
2. Security, privacy and compliance: 30% to 39% cite it [1][2][6].
3. No in-house skills: 46% have no AI experts inside or outside [6]; 70% say they need outside help [1].
4. Unproven return: 44% don't measure AI's impact at all [6].
5. Paying consultants for slides, not working software. 40% of firms raising AI spend take it from the consulting budget [2]. The fear itself is INFERRED.

## How he reads a page
- He starts with search or an AI chatbot: 45% of buyers use AI while buying, and 67% prefer buying without a salesperson [10].
- He shortlists 3 or fewer options before he calls anyone [12], and calls about 60% of the way through his research [14].
- He decides in about 10 seconds whether to stay [16].
- He reads on desktop and phone; B2B sites get about 28% to 31% of visits from phones [15].
- Clear pricing is the top thing buyers want from vendors [12][13]. No price reads as "expensive and hiding it". INFERRED.
- Generic AI hype pushes him away: 73% avoid suppliers whose outreach isn't relevant to them [9].

## What gets him to book the free diagnostic (all INFERRED)
- A clear promise of what he walks away with.
- A rough price range for the next step.
- No sales pitch, and booking right on the page.
- Proof from a company his size.

## The Steve review checklist
1. In 10 seconds, can he tell what this page answers and whether it's for him?
2. Can he scan it: short sentences, one idea per bullet, bold leads, no walls of text?
3. Is every term he wouldn't use explained or cut?
4. Does it say what it costs, or say plainly why it can't?
5. Does it speak to his worries: data, security and rules, skills, return, consultant fees?
6. Could he forward it to his controller as is?
7. Is the next step clear, low-risk and worth 30 minutes?
8. What would he skip, distrust or close the tab on?
9. Is it ADHD friendly? (Russell, 26 Nov: "In the bottom line, you have to make it bullets that are easy to scan. You have to make everything ADHD friendly.") Bottom line in 3 to 5 bullets, one idea per line, no sentence over 25 words, no block he'd have to re-read.
