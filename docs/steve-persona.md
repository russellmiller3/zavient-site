# Who every page is reviewed as: the ICP (ideal customer profile)

Rule (26 Nov 2026), in Russell's words: "add a rule to review it as if you're actually a persona? We'll call that persona Steve. That's a part of our ICP. And we want Steve to review it and suggest anything that would make it more usable to him. or that he doesn't like."

Review order (Russell, 26 Nov 2026: "do the reviews wiht cicp that include coo head of ops and gm and ceo last since he is for smaller companies"):
1. Dana, the COO or head of operations.
2. The GM (general manager) who runs a business unit, site or region.
3. Steve, the owner-CEO, last, because he fits the smaller firms (under about 150 people).
There is no CFO reader (Russell, 26 Nov: "Nix cfo").

Every page gets one review per reader, in that order, before it ships. The reviewer reads the page as Steve, lists what he would find hard to use or dislike, and the fixes that fit the Site rules are applied. Lines marked INFERRED are a draft for Russell to confirm.

## Who Steve is
- Owner-CEO of a US company of 40 to 500 people, roughly $10M to $100M revenue. Russell's pick (26 Nov 2026): "Steve = owner-CEO". The revenue band is INFERRED.
- He owns AI himself. Mid-market firms where the CEO or C-suite owns AI report measurable return 62% of the time, against 18% with no single owner (UK survey of 755 leaders) [20].
- He researches with AI: 66% of business buyers who use AI at work use it to research vendors, and 92% say it shaped their shortlist [19]. Most are final decision-makers (54%) [19].
- He uses AI himself: 76% of Vistage small and midsize business CEOs use generative AI [5].
- His company uses AI tools but hasn't built them into the work: 91% of middle-market firms use AI [3]; 25% have it fully built into core workflows [1].
- He doesn't buy alone: a typical purchase involves about 13 people inside and 9 outside [11].

## Reader 1: Dana, the COO or head of operations
Russell (26 Nov 2026): "Add the COO persona". Dana usually finds Zavient in search and builds the shortlist; Steve signs.
- Most common at about 50 to 500 people. Below about 50, the CEO should own AI directly; above that, a business operator (COO, head of operations, GM) who holds budget and process authority should [21].
- Dana is measured on throughput and cost, and changes how teams work, not just which software they use [21]. Dana reports to the CEO in hours, cost, quality and risk, not model names [21].
- Dana feels the urgency more than the owner: 73% of functional leaders (operations managers, department heads) say their business must act on AI within 6 months; 70% call AI essential within 3 years, against 56% of owners [22].
- Not IT: at larger firms the CIO or CTO leads AI 42% of the time and the CEO 15% [23]. Most firms of 40 to 500 people have no full-time CIO. INFERRED.
- What Dana needs from a page: which process to fix first, a before-and-after measure, how long it takes, who on the team does what, and something to forward to Steve. INFERRED.

## Reader 2: the GM (general manager)
Russell (26 Nov 2026) added the GM to the ICP.
- Runs a business unit, product line, site or region, with full P&L (profit and loss) for it. Reports to the CEO or COO [25].
- Is who should carry out AI work: midmarket CEOs set the ambition, and business leaders and P&L owners execute [24].
- Midmarket firms already lean this way: 45% hold business leaders accountable for AI results, against 28% at large firms [24].
- A business operator such as a COO, head of operations or GM should own AI above about 50 people [21].
- What the GM needs from a page: what it does to the unit's margin, how much of the team's time it takes, and whether one site can pilot it first. INFERRED.

## Reader 3: Steve, the owner-CEO (last)
Steve reads last. He signs, and he fits the smaller firms best.

Steve's needs and worries:

## What he wants
- Less time and fewer errors in payables, receivables, invoices and documents [18].
- A result he can count this year, not a transformation: 45% of firms put AI only where it pays now; 17% aim for transformation [2].

## His top worries
1. Messy, scattered data: 34% to 45% cite it [1][2][6].
2. Security, privacy and compliance: 30% to 39% cite it [1][2][6].
3. No in-house skills: 46% have no AI experts inside or outside [6]; 70% say they need outside help [1].
4. Unproven return: 44% don't measure AI's impact at all [6].
5. Paying consultants for slides, not working software. 40% of firms raising AI spend take it from the consulting budget [2]. The fear itself is INFERRED.

## How he reads a page
- He starts with search or an AI chatbot: 45% of buyers use AI while buying, and 67% prefer buying without a salesperson [10].
- He shortlists 3 or fewer options before he calls anyone [12], and calls about 60% of the way through his research [14].
- He decides in about 10 seconds whether to stay [16].
- He reads on desktop and phone; B2B sites get about 28% to 31% of visits from phones [15].
- Clear pricing is the top thing buyers want from vendors [12][13]. No price reads as "expensive and hiding it". INFERRED.
- Generic AI hype pushes him away: 73% avoid suppliers whose outreach isn't relevant to them [9].

## What gets him to book the free diagnostic (all INFERRED)
- A clear promise of what he walks away with.
- A rough price range for the next step.
- No sales pitch, and booking right on the page.
- Proof from a company his size.

## The review checklist (every reader)
1. In 10 seconds, can he tell what this page answers and whether it's for him?
2. Can he scan it: short sentences, one idea per bullet, bold leads, no walls of text?
3. Is every term he wouldn't use explained or cut?
4. Does it say what it costs, or say plainly why it can't?
5. Does it speak to his worries: data, security and rules, skills, return, consultant fees?
6. Could Dana or the GM forward it to Steve as is? Does it name a first process to fix, a before-and-after measure, and whether one unit can pilot it?
7. Is the next step clear, low-risk and worth 30 minutes?
8. What would he skip, distrust or close the tab on?
9. Is it ADHD friendly? (Russell, 26 Nov: "In the bottom line, you have to make it bullets that are easy to scan. You have to make everything ADHD friendly.") Bottom line in 3 to 5 bullets, one idea per line, no sentence over 25 words, no block he'd have to re-read.
