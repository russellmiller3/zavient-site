## Provider orgs are administrative-cost businesses wearing a clinical costume

A physician group or RCM company makes money by collecting on care delivered. The leak is everywhere between the visit and the deposit. Initial claim denials hit **11.8% in 2024**, and hospitals lose an average of 4.8% of net revenue to denials ([HFMA/TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/feature/Breaking-down-claim-denial-rates-by-healthcare-payer)). A single manual prior authorization eats up to 24 minutes of staff time and costs roughly **$3.41 versus $0.05 fully electronic** — CAQH pegs industry-wide administrative waste at $20 billion ([CAQH 2024 Index](https://www.caqh.org/blog/new-caqh-index-reveals-20b-savings-opportunity-to-cut-waste-reduce-costs-and-improve-patient-access)).

The PE read is simple. You're not buying clinical edge — you're buying a labor-arbitrage and leakage-recovery business. AI's job is to shrink cost-to-collect and stop revenue from walking out the door.

## Revenue cycle is the clearest near-term play — the smart money already moved

This isn't a thesis you have to talk sponsors into; they've voted with checks. **New Mountain Capital** combined three portfolio companies into Smarter Technologies, an AI-RCM platform expected to clear $800M+ in annual revenue ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/new-mountain-capital-creates-ai-revenue-cycle-management-company-smarter-technologies/748748/)). **Blackstone** is acquiring RCM firm AGS Health from EQT for ~$1.1–1.3B — a company doing ~$60M EBITDA that EQT bought for $320M in 2019 ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/front-runner-to-acquire-rcm-co-ags-health-emerges-report/)). The value-creation math: buy at 4–7x EBITDA, bolt on AI to cut cost-to-collect, exit at 10–15x ([onhealthcare.tech](https://www.onhealthcare.tech/p/the-ai-driven-private-equity-revolution) — treat that arbitrage as the sell-side narrative it is).

## Autonomous coding is the one play with real, audited numbers

Most AI healthcare claims are mush. Medical coding is the exception — it's measurable, and the numbers are good. **Fathom** reports a deployment hitting 95.5% automation at 98.3% accuracy across all service lines, and an average 42.3% reduction in cost-to-code ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260319818217/en/Your-Health-Deploys-Fathom-Autonomous-Medical-Coding-to-Achieve-95.5-Automation-Rate-at-98.3-Accuracy-Rate-Across-All-Service-Lines)). Call these vendor-reported, but coding has a built-in honesty test: claims either get paid or denied, and auditors check the codes. If a portfolio company codes high-volume, repetitive encounters (radiology, path, EM), this is the first place to look.

## Prior auth: real provider ROI, but know which side of the table you're on

For a provider org, automating PA is a clean cost play. Cohere Health reports ~85% real-time PA determinations, and Geisinger saw a **63% reduction in PA denials** after deploying it ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/cohere-health-ai-prior-authorization)).

Here's the Zavient warning. The PA-AI story has two faces. **Payer-side denial AI is a regulatory minefield** — UnitedHealth's nH Predict faced allegations of a ~90% error rate, and a 2026 Stanford/Health Affairs study found denied patients win on appeal over 80% of the time but only 0.2% ever appeal ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00897)). CMS rules now require explicit reasoning for AI-assisted denials, and several states ban AI as the sole basis for denial. **If your portfolio company touches the payer side of PA, the AI is a liability, not an asset.** Provider-side automation is the safe, EBITDA-positive play.

## Ambient documentation: buy it for retention, not a fake productivity story

Ambient AI scribes (Abridge, Nuance DAX) are the most-hyped play and the one most likely to fool an operating partner. The evidence is mixed: a DAX study showed a 20.4% drop in note time, but a longitudinal **NEJM AI study of 112 clinicians found DAX did not make them more efficient as a group** ([NEJM AI](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/AIoa2400659)). The real money isn't time saved — it's revenue capture: Riverside Health saw an 11% rise in physician wRVUs and a 14% increase in documented HCC diagnoses ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12973079/)). Buy ambient for retention and accurate acuity capture, not "see more patients" — and watch the upcoding/audit risk.

## The unglamorous wins: scheduling, no-shows, and call deflection

The least sexy plays often have the cleanest ROI because the baseline is so bad. AI-driven reminders and no-show prediction cut missed appointments **20–38%**; El Rio Health, an Arizona FQHC, reported a 32% no-show reduction and ~$100K/month revenue increase ([Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/fqhc-slashed-its-patient-no-show-rate-ai-3-months)). For behavioral health and home health, every recovered slot is near-pure margin on an already-staffed clinician.

## What kills these initiatives

- **Buying ambient AI for a productivity story the evidence doesn't support.** NEJM-grade studies show no group efficiency gain.
- **Confusing payer-side and provider-side PA AI.** Provider-side = savings. Payer-side denial AI = lawsuits, CMS mandates, state bans.
- **No baseline metrics before deployment.** If you can't state denial rate, cost-to-collect, and no-show rate today, you can't prove the AI moved anything at exit.
- **EHR integration debt.** Tools that don't sit natively in the EHR die in the workflow.
- **Upcoding exposure.** Ambient + autonomous coding can inflate acuity — audit and clawback risk, not durable EBITDA.
- **HIPAA / data-governance hand-waving.** PHI flowing to a vendor model without a BAA is a deal-killer in diligence.
