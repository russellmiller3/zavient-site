What does AI change for a family office COO?

AI changes how fast a family office COO's team clears paperwork: statements, notices, deadlines and drafts get a first pass.1,3 The COO runs the office as an operating business: reporting, technology, compliance, vendors and controls.10 AI pays only with privacy controls and a named reviewer.1

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every payment, filing, investment call and family report, while AI drafts, extracts and flags work for them to check.1,8

  • COO: owns the list of approved AI tools, what family data may go in, and which outputs need a human check.1,9
  • Controller: reviews every AI-extracted capital call, K-1 figure or journal entry before it posts. Citi found careful offices treat AI output as drafts needing human checks.1
  • Payment approver: a named person approves every wire and bank-detail change, with a call-back. FinCEN warns criminals use deepfakes of executives to order transfers.8
  • Principal or chief investment officer: keeps every investment decision and signs off what the family sees. Citi found final decisions stay with experienced people.1

Where does AI pay first in a family office?

AI pays first in consolidated reporting and document intake, and 73% of family offices in the Americas call investment reporting too manual.5,1

  1. Consolidated reporting

    73% of family offices in the Americas say investment reporting is too manual, a top concern three years running.5 Citi found monthly adviser reports still merged into Excel by hand.1

    Payoff to measure: Days from month-end to the family's report, and breaks per cycle.

  2. Capital calls, K-1s and statements

    Citi found family office tax work highly manual and voluminous.1 In our model office, by our count, 15 trust and personal returns wait on 13 partnership K-1s.16,17

    Payoff to measure: Minutes per document, and fields the controller corrects.

  3. Bookkeeping, bill pay and entity accounting

    Bill pay is the task family offices most often keep fully in-house, at 69%, with accounting or tax at 51%.3

    Payoff to measure: Days to close each entity, and invoices handled per person.

  4. The filing and compliance calendar

    By our count, a model 30-filer office faces 159 federal, SEC and Delaware deadlines a year.16,24 Family office executives spend 19% of their time on administration and compliance.6

    Payoff to measure: Deadlines met without a scramble, and hours spent chasing documents.

  5. Email, meeting notes and memos

    Copilot users spent about 2 fewer hours a week on email in a field study of 7,137 workers.28 Writing tasks took 40% less time with ChatGPT in a controlled study.29

    Payoff to measure: Hours a week on email, notes and memos.

  6. Software, vendors and security

    Choosing new software can take 8 to 12 weeks and implementing it 3 to 6 months, per Deloitte.6 So a 90-day start runs on tools you already license.

    Payoff to measure: Vendor AI terms read, and features switched on with sign-off.

What will the principal or family ask?

The principal or family will ask about data safety, savings so far, fraud, the SEC, what peers do and whether to hire.1,3

Is the family's data safe in these tools?

Only on business plans with admin controls and a written rule on what data goes in.31,1 Citi calls privacy non-negotiable, and 28% of family offices cite cybersecurity or privacy as a barrier.1

What has AI saved other family offices so far?

Little measured cost so far: only 18% of single-family offices in the Americas report AI cut operating costs.4 Another 29% of those offices say it is too early, so set a baseline before day one.4

Are we exposed to AI-driven fraud?

Yes: 72% of family offices in a Bank of America survey say the office or a family member has faced fraud.3 FinCEN warns deepfakes of executives are used to order transfers.8 Keep a call-back rule on every payment request.

Does the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) examine our AI use?

No, not by default, because an office that meets the SEC's family office rule is not an investment adviser.15 A multi-family office or registered adviser arm is different.

Are other family offices using AI yet?

Yes: 42% of US family offices say employees use AI today, and 37% more are interested, in Bank of America's survey.3 Citi puts AI use for operations or investment analysis at 22% of family offices, up from 13%.1,2

Do we need to hire someone to run AI?

No, not to start, because live job postings write AI into the existing operations seat.11,12 A Chicago multi-family office COO role names AI initiatives, and a family enterprise operations director must measure time saved.11,12

What does a 90-day start look like?

A 90-day start is one job, four weeks of baseline hours, a six-week pilot on tools you already license, then a keep, change or stop call.6,1

1. Lean office, 10 staff or fewer: seat tools plus one job

No in-house IT, and Microsoft 365 already in place. By our math from Bank of America's survey, 42% of family offices have no more than 10 employees.3

  1. COOPick one job, such as the monthly family report or meeting notes, and name its owner (days 1 to 10)
  2. ControllerRecord four weeks of baseline hours, errors and turnaround (days 1 to 30)
  3. IT providerTurn on phishing-resistant multifactor sign-in for email and bank portals
  4. COOTurn on business-plan seats and post the rule on what family data may go in
  5. StaffRun the pilot for six weeks while a hold-out group keeps the old way (days 31 to 75)
  6. ControllerCheck every AI draft before it leaves the office
  7. COOKeep, change or stop at day 90

Cost: Copilot Business lists at $21 a user a month on an annual plan, and ChatGPT Business at $20 (read 28 Nov 2026).30,31 Ten users for three months at $21 is $630 in seats, by our math.

2. Document-heavy office: capital calls, K-1s and statements

Many entities and private funds, with notices arriving by email. Citi found family office tax work manual and voluminous.1

  1. ControllerCount documents, minutes each and errors for four weeks
  2. COOTest one extraction tool, or your platform's own feature, on your own documents
  3. Staff accountantReview every low-confidence field before it posts
  4. ControllerSign off the posting rules and the exception log
  5. COOCompare minutes per document with the baseline at day 90

Cost: We give no list price for extraction tools here; our family office accounting guide covers K-1 and capital call intake. Seats for staff who review drafts cost as in recipe 1.30,31

3. Office with a deadline crunch: the filing calendar

Many partnerships and trusts, and returns that wait on K-1s. By our count, a model 30-filer office faces 159 deadlines a year.16,24

  1. COOList every entity, filer and form, with the person who owns each
  2. ControllerLoad due dates from IRS Publication 509 into one shared calendar
  3. Outside tax accountantConfirm extension and estimate dates for each return
  4. Operations associateHave AI draft document requests to advisers, then edit and send them
  5. COOReview late and chased items each month

Cost: Seats cost as in recipe 1, and the calendar needs no new software.30,16

How do you show it worked?

Show it worked by timing two jobs for four weeks, piloting against a hold-out group, and pricing the hours saved in dollars.28,12

  1. Pick two jobs and one unit each, such as a capital call notice or a month-end report. Record four weeks of volume, minutes per unit and errors.
  2. Time the filing calendar too: deadlines due, met and chased. Our model office carries 159 a year, by our count.16
  3. Pilot with a few staff and keep similar work without AI as a hold-out group. The large email field study compared AI users with non-users.28
  4. Check quality as well as speed. Citi found careful offices fully review AI summaries before moving to spot-checks.1
  5. Measure time saved per job, as one family enterprise's operations director role requires.12
  6. Expect modest numbers: only 18% of single-family offices in the Americas report AI cut operating costs.4
  7. Use your own payroll for pay: we found no posted single-family-office COO range. A multi-family office COO posting shows $400,000+, and a wealth adviser's family office COO $190,000 to $250,000.11,13
  8. Price the lift: hours saved times pay plus benefits, minus seats. Example: by our math, 5% of a $150,000 salary is $7,500 a year.14
  9. Decide at day 90: keep, change or stop each pilot against its baseline. Offices that build their own tools wait for 80%+ efficiency gains.1

Where does AI in a family office go wrong?

AI in a family office goes wrong through spoofed payment requests, missing incident plans, stalled pilots, wrong facts sent to the family and single points of failure.8,7

  • Payments on a spoofed request: FinCEN warns deepfakes of executives are used to order transfers.8 Fix: keep AI out of payment approval, and call back on every bank-detail change.
  • No incident plan: 31% of family offices have no cyber incident response plan, in Deloitte's survey.7 Fix: write one, and turn on phishing-resistant multifactor sign-in first.32
  • Pilots that never ship: MIT NANDA found 95% of organizations getting zero return from generative AI.34 Fix: one job, one owner, a baseline and a day-90 decision.
  • Wrong facts sent to the family: a tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's advice.35 Fix: a person reads every AI draft before it reaches the family.
  • One person holds the workflow together: a recruiter's guide tells new COOs to find single points of failure in their first 90 days.10 Fix: write each AI workflow down and train a backup.
  • Falling behind: failure to upgrade technology tops family offices' long-term risk list, at 51%, in RBC and Campden Wealth's survey.5 Fix: start small now, on tools you already own.

Should a family office buy AI or build it?

Buy first, because most family offices prefer ready-made tools and 57% cite lack of internal expertise as the biggest barrier.1,2

Buy: Most family offices should buy first, because most prefer ready-made tools and 57% cite lack of internal expertise.1 Enterprise buyers now buy 76% of AI use cases, say decision-makers polled by Menlo Ventures, an AI investor.33

Build: Build only where privacy or customization demands it: some family offices run open-source models on site for security.1 Even then, those family offices build only for 80%+ efficiency gains.1

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when providers do most of the work, an operating business dominates, you are a registered adviser, or the pain is a multi-entity close.3,15

  • Providers do most of the work: start with their AI terms, not new tools. Bank of America found 24% of family offices fully outsource cybersecurity.3
  • The office runs beside an operating business: a holding company playbook may fit better. One new office's first hire splits time 50/50 with the family business.14
  • You are a registered adviser or multi-family office: SEC rules apply, so start from wealth management controls.15
  • The pain is closing many entities' books: a multi-entity close method beats a chat assistant. In our count, entity count drives the deadline calendar.16
