What is AI for compliance work, and what does it change?

AI for compliance teams means tools that read, sort and draft compliance paperwork while officers decide.1,4 It pays where examiners already test: rule changes, complaints and ads, each signed off by a person.2,12 Making your own AI obey AI laws is AI governance: see our governance consulting page under Related.

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your officers keep every filing, sign-off and escalation call, while AI reads, sorts and drafts for them to check.5,23

  • Chief compliance officer: owns the list of AI uses and the human check on each. Under the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) 2026 examiner framework, controls can rise with the consequences of an AI failure.5
  • Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) officer: makes every call to file a suspicious activity report (SAR), with AI only preparing the case. FinCEN's 2026 proposal keeps a US-based anti-money laundering (AML) officer and a written program approved by the board or senior management.23
  • Compliance analyst or legal reviewer: signs off every ad and disclosure that AI pre-checks. The OCC flags marketing that compliance, legal or audit staff have not reviewed for clarity and accuracy.7
  • Complaint lead: decides which complaints raise unfair, deceptive or abusive practice (UDAAP) risk, and routes each to an owner. FDIC examiners ask whether complaints are cross-referenced to the rest of the program.16,12

A chief compliance officer owns six AI calls: where to start, model validation, who checks output, what to record, the insurer AI program, and buy or build.5,10

Where does AI pay first in compliance?

AI pays first on regulatory change tracking, then complaint capture, then marketing pre-checks, with Bank Secrecy Act alert triage last.15,12

  1. Regulatory change tracking

    By our count, six federal financial regulators posted 554 Federal Register documents from 1 Jan to 27 Nov 2026.15 Only 152 were rules or proposed rules, so AI sorts out the notices and a person assigns each rule.15,2

    Payoff to measure: Days from publication to an assigned owner, and rules missed at exam.

  2. All-channel complaint capture and trending

    The FDIC expects complaints captured from phone, mail, email, branch, regulator, text and chat, then trended for UDAAP risk.12,16 Insurers must keep a complete complaint record under NAIC Model 880.17

    Payoff to measure: Share of complaints logged, days to close, and issues linked to a root cause.

  3. Marketing and disclosure pre-check

    Regulation Z lists trigger terms that require added disclosures in credit ads, a narrow and testable check.18 Credit unions follow the NCUA advertising rules, and the FDIC digital signage rule applies from 1 Apr 2027.19,20

    Payoff to measure: Hours per ad reviewed, and findings caught before launch.

  4. BSA alert triage, after a pilot elsewhere

    Industry false positives run around 90% to 95%, in one interview study.21 A survey of 15 big banks put 21.41 hours on each SAR, against FinCEN's 1.98-hour estimate.22

    Payoff to measure: Alerts worked per analyst hour, with the escalation rate held steady.

What will your CEO, board or examiner ask?

Your CEO, board or examiner will ask about examiner acceptance, BSA alerts, model validation, failed pilots, cost and what peers are doing.5,3

Will examiners accept AI-assisted compliance work?

Yes, if a person signs off and controls match the stakes: the OCC's 2026 examiner framework for AI sets no supervisory expectations for banks.5 Regulators oversee AI under existing laws, and most use AI output to inform decisions, not make them.6

Can a community bank use AI on BSA alerts?

Yes, but tune and test it first: the OCC found one bank's triage system auto-closed a very high share of alerts that needed escalation.31 FinCEN's 2026 proposal says its Director would consider whether a bank uses innovative tools such as AI.23

Do we need model validation for a chatbot or Copilot?

Not under the OCC's new model risk guidance: Bulletin 2026-13 puts generative and agentic AI out of its scope.11 SR 26-2, which replaced SR 11-7 in April 2026, is aimed mainly at banks over $30B.10 Still record each tool's use and who checks it.5

What happens if our AI pilot fails?

A failed pilot alone should not draw supervisory criticism, under a 2018 statement from FinCEN and the banking agencies.24 Pilots that expose gaps in a BSA program will not necessarily draw supervisory action either.24

What does a 90-day start look like?

A 90-day start runs a rule-change log in days 1 to 30, complaint capture in days 31 to 60 and a marketing pre-check in days 61 to 90.15,12

1. Three to five person team at a community bank or credit union

Microsoft 365 is in place and there is no data team. Most community banks surveyed are still piloting or testing AI.3

  1. Chief compliance officerName an owner per workflow and post the allowed-tools list (days 1 to 5)
  2. Compliance analystPull the six regulators' Federal Register feed, let AI sort rules from notices, and assign each rule (days 1 to 30)
  3. Branch and call-center leadsLog every complaint taken by phone, in person or by chat into one shared log (from day 31)
  4. Complaint leadLet AI tag product and issue, then check each tag and route UDAAP risks to an owner (days 31 to 60)
  5. Marketing reviewerAI flags Regulation Z trigger terms, and the reviewer signs off each ad (days 61 to 90)
  6. Chief compliance officerKeep, change or stop each workflow at day 90

Cost: Copilot Business lists at $21 a user a month, paid yearly, read 28 Nov 2026.34 The plan is yearly, so by our math five users cost $1,260 a year at list.

2. Insurer compliance team: complaint register and AI program

Your state is one of the 30 jurisdictions that adopted the NAIC AI bulletin, by our count.14

  1. Chief compliance officerWrite the AI program the NAIC bulletin expects and list each AI use
  2. Complaint leadAI drafts the register fields Model 880 requires (line, nature, disposition, time to process), and the lead checks each
  3. Market conduct leadTest register entries against NAIC exam standards for response times
  4. Advertising reviewerAI pre-checks each ad, and the reviewer signs it off
  5. Chief compliance officerReport results to the board at day 90

Cost: Seat prices as in recipe 1.34 Complaint and policy tools quote each deal, so ask for a written list price.36

3. Lender with a BSA team: alert triage pilot after day 90

Alert volume swamps the team. Industry false positives run around 90% to 95%.21

  1. BSA officerRecord four weeks of alerts, close rates and hours per case
  2. BSA analystRun the AI on last quarter's closed alerts as a test set before live use
  3. Model ownerTune and document thresholds, with no auto-close in the pilot
  4. BSA officerDecide every SAR, with AI drafting the narrative
  5. Internal auditTest a sample of AI-cleared alerts at day 90

Cost: ComplyAdvantage Starter lists from $99 a month, read 28 Nov 2026.35 Its claim that AI resolves up to 85% of routine alerts is the vendor's.35

How do you show it worked?

Show it worked by timing rules, complaints and ads for four weeks before and six weeks after, with a hold-out group, then pricing the hours.22,27

  1. Pick one unit: a rule, a complaint, an ad or an alert. Record four weeks of volume, hours per unit and findings.
  2. Compare real effort with official estimates. A survey of 15 big banks found 21.41 hours per SAR, against FinCEN's 1.98-hour estimate.22
  3. Use measures examiners already read: complaints cross-referenced to the program, and response times on the complaint register.16,25
  4. Run the AI on last quarter's closed items first, and compare its calls with your officers' calls before live use.
  5. Keep a hold-out: for six weeks, half the ads or complaints go through the old process.
  6. Track misses, not just speed. An untuned triage tool auto-closed alerts that should have been escalated.31
  7. Price the lift as hours saved times pay. By our math, 5% of a compliance officer's year at the BLS median of $80,730 is about $4,040.27
  8. Decide at day 90: keep, change or stop, and record why for the next exam.

Where does AI in compliance go wrong?

AI in compliance goes wrong when triage auto-closes alerts, monitoring goes untuned, AI citations go unchecked or staff stop sampling what AI clears.31,32

  • Auto-close without tuning: an OCC order found a bank's triage system auto-closed a very high percentage of alerts that needed escalation.31 Fix: no auto-close in a pilot, and tune thresholds on your own data.
  • Monitoring left untuned: not an AI case, but the OCC fined Bank of America $750 million in Oct 2026 for deficient BSA and AML compliance.32 Fix: review scenarios and coverage at least once a year.
  • Unchecked AI drafts: a federal appeals court imposed a $2,500 sanction for an AI-drafted brief nobody verified.33 Fix: a person checks every citation in an AI-drafted policy or memo.
  • Automation bias: in a vendor survey of 230 US banks, 34% of respondents named it a risk.9 Fix: sample what AI clears every week and track overrides.
  • No off switch: in the same vendor survey of 230 US banks, 34% of respondents said kill-switch protocols are not in place.9 Fix: write down who can turn each tool off, and how.

Should you buy compliance AI or build it?

Buy first for rule feeds, screening and seat tools, and build only the link to your own complaint, policy and exam data.28,6

Buy: Most compliance teams should buy first, since rule feeds, screening and seat tools are standard work.28,36 Across organizations interviewed by MIT NANDA, partnered tools reached deployment about 67% of the time, against about 33% for internal builds.28 Credit unions should plan more vendor diligence, because the NCUA cannot examine technology providers.6

Build: Build only the link to your own complaint log, policy library and exam findings, where your data is the edge. If a build becomes a model, SR 26-2 sets the model risk expectations.10 OCC examiners will also ask how much human oversight each use has.5

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when you mean AI governance, when fraud is the real problem, when volumes are tiny, or when a BSA rewrite awaits a final rule.3,29

  • You mean making your own AI obey AI laws: that is AI governance, covered on our AI governance consulting page. Insurers in 30 jurisdictions face the NAIC AI bulletin, by our count.14,37
  • Fraud losses or bank-wide AML alert volume is the real problem: see our community banks page. In the CSBS survey, 77% of AI-using banks use it for fraud detection and security.3
  • Complaints are a few a month and ads a handful a quarter: a checklist and a shared log beat a tool. By our count, the median company in the CFPB database got 5 complaints in 2025.29
  • Your BSA program redesign hangs on FinCEN's 2026 rule: wait, since it is still a proposal and the OCC plans an AI request for information.8,11
