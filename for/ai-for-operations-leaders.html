What is AI for operations, and what does it change?

AI for operations means tools that read, write, answer and sort the paperwork around your operation, while your people keep the calls.1,10 AI speeds up answering, drafting and checking, not who decides.3 Whether it pays depends on redesigning the workflow, not on buying seats.16,17

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people keep every call that directs, schedules or commits the firm. AI drafts, answers and flags work for them to check.10,29

  • COO: owns the risk call and the list of AI outputs that need a human check.29,30 High performers set rules for when people must validate AI output.15
  • Customer service lead: approves every answer pattern before AI uses it with customers, and owns the route to a person. A tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong advice.23
  • Planner or scheduler: keeps the schedule and work assignments, while AI drafts and flags. In our analysis, scheduling and assigning work show no measurable AI use.10,11
  • Controller: signs off any AI-matched or AI-coded number before it posts.30 On a task outside AI's range, AI users were 19 points less likely to be right.5

Where does AI pay first in operations?

AI pays first on customer and order questions, then order paperwork and status writing, where field studies show the biggest gains.3,4

  1. Customer and order questions

    Customer service reps rank 7th of 785 jobs for AI help in our analysis.10 Support agents with an AI assistant resolved 14% more issues an hour, and novices about 34% more.3

    Payoff to measure: Resolutions per hour, escalations and new-hire ramp time.

  2. Order entry and order-to-cash paperwork

    Median order-to-cash takes 38 days and costs $2.32 per $1,000 of revenue, per APQC, a benchmarking body.34,35 A vendor-sponsored 2017 survey of 167 supply chain and customer service representatives found automated order entry cut cycle time over 46%.36

    Payoff to measure: Order entry hours, error rate and days to invoice.

  3. Status updates, reports and email

    Professionals using ChatGPT finished writing tasks 40% faster at 18% higher quality.4 Copilot users spent about 2 fewer hours a week on email.6

    Payoff to measure: Hours a week on email and reports, and report turnaround.

  4. Purchase orders and supplier questions

    APQC's median cost to process a purchase order is $55, and issuing one takes 2.0 days.32,33 AI helps buyers gather product facts, but negotiating shows no AI use.10

    Payoff to measure: Cost per purchase order and days to issue one.

  5. Records, reconciliations and data checks

    Compiling records scores high for AI help, but reconciling financial data shows no use in our analysis.10 So AI prepares and flags, and people keep the matching rules and sign-off.30

    Payoff to measure: Exceptions per close and days to close.

  6. Planning and forecasting

    A 2026 operations survey finds planning and sourcing lead AI use.19 Chat tools show no use for analyzing data to improve operations, so this pays through a forecasting model.10

    Payoff to measure: Forecast error against last year's method.

What will your CEO or board ask?

Your CEO or board will ask about jobs, payback, ownership, tools already bought, peers and data safety.13,15

Will AI replace our operations managers?

No: the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects operations manager jobs to grow 5.0% from 2025 to 2035, above 3.5% for all jobs.13 BLS rates the role high for AI exposure, but says exposure does not imply job loss.14 In our analysis, 69% of the role's work shows no AI use.10

What's the return on AI, and when will we see it?

Expect returns per workflow first, not company-wide: only 39% of firms report any EBIT impact from AI.15 Users save about 5.4% of their hours, or 2.2% of all work hours.8,9 So set a baseline and judge each workflow at day 90.

Who owns AI here, the COO or IT?

The workflow owner owns the result, and IT owns access and security.30 NIST puts AI risk decisions with executive leadership.29 Senior leaders at AI high performers show ownership three times as often.15

Why did the AI tools we bought change nothing?

Seats without a redesigned workflow rarely move the numbers.16,17 Only 21% of firms using generative AI had redesigned any workflow, the step with the biggest earnings effect.16 In a Copilot field study, email time fell but the mix of tasks did not change.6

Are firms our size using AI yet?

Yes: 32% of US firms with 100 to 249 staff and 37% with 250 or more used AI, in Census data to 3 May 2026.2 The national rate was 19.8%, and use rose among firms with 20 or more staff.2,1

Is our data safe in these tools?

Only on business plans with admin controls, plus a written list of allowed tools.39,18 Google says Workspace data isn't used to train its models; that is the vendor's claim.39 A 2025 survey found 54% of workers would use unapproved tools anyway.18

The one-page brief to hand your CEO

  • Top workflow: customer and order questions. Support agents with AI resolved 14% more issues an hour.3,10
  • What it saves: writing and email time first, about 40% on writing tasks.4,6 Expect a few percent of total hours, not a step change.8,9
  • Risk and who checks: the firm owns what its AI tells customers.23 The ops lead approves answer patterns, and the controller signs off numbers.
  • First step: one workflow, four weeks of baseline, then a pilot against a hold-out group.3,6
  • Cost: AI seats list at $14 to $21 a user a month, so by our math 15 users for 90 days cost at most $945.39,37 Connecting AI to your order or ticket system costs extra.
  • How we'll know: resolutions per hour, order cycle time and cost per purchase order, against APQC medians of 38 days and $55.34,32

What does a 90-day start look like?

A 90-day start is one workflow, four weeks of baseline, six weeks of pilot against a hold-out group, then a keep, change or stop call.3,6

1. Small ops team, about 40 to 100 staff: seat tools plus one workflow

No IT team, and Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace already in place. Most firms start AI in three or fewer functions.1

  1. COOPick one workflow, such as order status emails or the weekly ops report, and name an owner (days 1 to 10)
  2. Ops leadRecord four weeks of baseline: volume, hours, error rate and turnaround
  3. Office manager or IT adminTurn on business-plan seats for 10 to 15 users and post the allowed-tools list
  4. Ops leadWrite answer templates and a check-before-send rule, then train users
  5. Ops leadRun six weeks with a hold-out group and compare
  6. COOKeep, change or stop at day 90

Cost: Copilot Business lists at $21 a user a month, annual; ChatGPT Business at $20; Google Business Standard at $14 (read 26 Nov 2026).37,38,39 Fifteen users for three months at $21 is $945 in seats.

2. Mid-size firm, 100 to 500 staff: service desk assist

A service or inside-sales team of 10 or more answering repeat questions. Novice agents gained most in the field study.3

  1. COOSponsor the pilot and set the measures: resolutions per hour and escalations
  2. Customer service leadBuild an approved answer library from past tickets
  3. ITConnect the AI assistant to the ticketing system and knowledge base
  4. AgentsAI suggests replies, and agents edit and send; no auto-send in the first 90 days
  5. Quality leadCheck 5% to 10% of AI-assisted replies each week for accuracy
  6. COOCompare gains for new and experienced agents at day 90

Cost: Seat prices as in recipe 1.37,38 We did not check helpdesk AI add-on prices, so ask your helpdesk vendor for its list price.

3. Document-heavy operation: orders or invoices arrive as PDFs

Orders or invoices arrive by email and get retyped into your ERP (enterprise resource planning system). APQC's median purchase order costs $55 to process.32

  1. COOPick the document flow with the highest volume
  2. Order desk or accounts payable leadCount documents, minutes each and error rate for four weeks
  3. IT or integratorSet up extraction into the ERP with a review queue
  4. Order desk leadApprove every low-confidence field and track the exception rate
  5. ControllerSign off the posting rules
  6. COOCompare cost per order with the baseline and APQC's median

Cost: We give no list price here; our document processing and invoice processing guides carry their own price checks. Measure against your baseline and APQC's medians.32,35

How do you show it worked?

Show it worked by comparing a pilot group with a hold-out group against four weeks of baseline, then pricing the gain in dollars.3,6

  1. Pick one workflow and one unit: a ticket, order, purchase order or report. Record four weeks of volume, minutes per unit, errors and cycle time.
  2. Compare your baseline with outside medians: APQC's 38-day order-to-cash, $55 per purchase order and 2.0 days to issue one.34,32,33
  3. Split users into a pilot group with AI and a hold-out group without, on similar work. Both big field studies measured AI users against non-users.3,6
  4. Measure quality as well as speed. Consultants using AI were faster and better inside AI's range, but 19 points less accurate outside it.5
  5. Check self-reported time savings against system data. In an early-2025 trial, developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster.26
  6. Count rework that lands on others. In a survey with BetterUp, a coaching vendor, 40% of desk workers had received AI work needing fixes in the past month.24,25
  7. Track where saved time goes. Danish workers moved 80% of saved time to other tasks.7
  8. Price the lift: share of a role's year saved times annual pay, plus errors avoided, minus seats and setup. Example: by our math, 5% of a customer service rep's year at the BLS annual mean wage of $46,590 is about $2,330.12
  9. Decide at day 90: keep, change or stop.

Where does AI in operations go wrong?

AI in operations goes wrong when pilots lack an owner, agents are oversold, customers get wrong answers or nobody checks the output.20,22

  • Pilots that never ship: an MIT study found 95% of firms saw no measurable profit impact from generative AI.20,21 Fix: one workflow, one owner, a baseline and a day-90 decision.
  • Agent hype: Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by end of 2027, and warns of agent washing.22 Fix: demand a demo on your own documents, and start with assist, not autonomy.
  • Wrong answers to customers: Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot, and Klarna said a cost focus cut quality.23,27,28 Fix: a person approves answer patterns, with an easy route to staff.
  • Using AI outside what it does well: on such a task, consultants with AI were 19 points less likely to be right.5 Fix: test your own hard cases before rollout.
  • Workslop: 40% of desk workers got AI work needing fixes last month, about 2 hours each, in a BetterUp vendor survey.24,25 Fix: the sender checks AI output before passing it on.
  • Shadow AI: 54% of workers would use AI tools even if not authorized.18 Fix: an allowed-tools list plus business plans with admin controls.39

Should you buy AI for operations or build it?

Buy: Most ops teams should buy first, for standard work such as email, meeting notes, helpdesk replies and document capture.31,20 Firms now buy 76% of AI use cases, say enterprise AI decision-makers polled by Menlo Ventures, an AI investor.31 Bought or partnered tools reached deployment about twice as often as internal builds.20

Build: Build when your own data or process is the edge, such as a pricing rule, a scheduling limit or a quoting method. Gartner warns many agent products are relabeled tools, so a build on a proven model can beat a weak product.22 Middle path: buy the seats, then build the connection to your systems.

When should you pick something else?

Pick something else when the process is broken, the rules are fixed, the problem is a forecast, or physical work dominates.16,10

  • The process is broken or undocumented: map and fix it first with standard process improvement, such as lean methods and written procedures. Redesign is what pays.16
  • The work is rules-based and stable: plain workflow automation or your ERP's built-in rules may be cheaper and more predictable.40
  • The problem is a forecast or schedule with years of history: a statistical forecasting or planning tool beats a chat assistant.19
  • Physical labor dominates: in our analysis, hand material movers rank 678th of 785, and 88% of their job has no AI use.10,11 Equipment, layout or a warehouse system matter more.
