Custom AI solutions: what should you walk away with?

Bottom line
  • Buy the AI model. Build only the part that holds your own data and workflow.4,3
  • Bought or partnered tools reached daily use about twice as often as internal builds in one MIT study.1,2
  • Custom usually means search over your own documents, not training a model. Search beats retraining for adding facts.5,24
  • Usage is cheap: $35 to $1,400 a month for 50,000 questions in our check. Upkeep is the hard part.17,30
  • Get it in writing: you own the prompts, test cases, search index, tuned models and code.13,36

What is Custom AI solutions, and what does it change?

Custom AI solutions connect a bought model to your own data, rules and software, so AI drafts, finds and extracts from your documents.5,6 Your staff check and decide.10 Testing on your own cases and planning upkeep make or break it.23,30

What should you walk away with?

Walk away with a build-or-buy call per use, a 3-year cost sheet, a test set of real cases, a contract saying you own the work, and a named owner.3,9,23,13,10

  • You should leave with a build-or-buy call, a 3-year cost sheet, a test set from your files, an ownership contract and a named owner.3,9
  • A written build, buy or leave-it call for each use, with the off-the-shelf option tested first. Most AI uses at large firms are now bought, not built.3,4
  • A 3-year cost sheet: build, AI usage at dated list prices, upkeep and model changes. Gartner warns AI cost estimates can be off by 500% to 1,000%.9,25
  • A test set of 20 to 50 real cases from your files, with today's results and a pass mark agreed before any demo.23,12
  • A contract that says you own the prompts, test cases, search index, any tuned model and the code, and can take them with you.13,36,37
  • A named owner who reruns the tests every month and whenever the vendor changes or retires a model.10,12

Where does your team stay in charge?

The COO or process owner makes the go or no-go call, a domain reviewer spot-checks output, and a named person approves customer-facing answers.10,12,14

  • Your people make the go or no-go call, set the right answers, settle ownership and approve customer-facing answers.10,14
  • The COO or process owner owns the result and makes the go or no-go call. NIST asks for checks by people who did not build the system.10
  • A domain reviewer, such as the controller or an ops lead, sets the right answers and spot-checks output. Model makers say human judgment should calibrate any automated scoring.12,23
  • Your legal or contracts lead settles who owns data, outputs and any model tuned on your data before signing.13,37 If the use is high-risk under EU rules, like hiring or credit, heavy changes can make your firm the legal provider.11
  • Your firm stays liable for what its AI tells customers, so a named person approves customer-facing answers at launch.14

What shapes can the work take?

Custom AI work takes three common shapes: answers from your own documents, pulling fields from documents into your system, or tuning a model for format or sorting.5,15,6

1. Answers from your own documents (the most common first build)

Pick this when staff hunt through manuals, contracts or specs for answers. Cloud providers advise starting with retrieval, which cites its sources and updates in minutes.5,6

  1. Ops leadPick one document set and 20 to 50 real questions with known answers
  2. DeveloperBuild retrieval (search that hands the AI the right passages) on a bought model
  3. IT leadLimit each person to the documents they can already open
  4. Domain reviewerGrade answers against the test set and sign off
  5. Ops leadGo live in one team first, then rerun the test set monthly

Cost: Our check: $35 to $1,400 a month in AI usage for 50,000 questions.15,17 A search index on Pinecone, a hosted search service, has a $50 monthly minimum on its Standard plan.21,22

2. Pulling fields from documents into your system (batch work)

Pick this for forms, orders or statements that arrive in volume and can wait a day. All four providers we checked halve the price for batch work, with results within a day.15,16

  1. AP (accounts payable) lead or clerkLabel 100 sample documents with the right fields
  2. DeveloperRun batch extraction and score it against the labels
  3. ReviewerCheck low-confidence fields before anything posts
  4. ControllerApprove go-live once accuracy meets the agreed mark

Cost: Our check: $3 (batch) to $240 a month in AI usage for 10,000 documents.15,17 Labelling, review time and the link into your system cost more than the tokens.30

3. Tuning a model for format or sorting (only with many labelled examples)

Pick this for a stable task, like sorting tickets or a fixed output format, with many labelled past examples. Do not tune it to learn facts; retrieval does that better.6,24

  1. Ops leadCollect labelled past examples and hold some back for testing
  2. DeveloperTune a small model and compare it with the untuned one
  3. ReviewerCheck results on the held-back cases
  4. Ops leadPlan a re-tune for when the base model retires

Cost: OpenAI lists $5 to $25 per million training tokens, but it has closed fine-tuning to new customers.16 The bigger costs are labelling data and re-tuning after model retirements.26,25

Why do most AI pilots stall?

Pilots stall when firms build what they could buy, start with messy documents, or stop testing as models change and quality slips.1,8,25,28

  • Most stall from building what could be bought, messy documents, model changes, unnoticed quality slips, weak contracts and cost surprises at volume.1,8 Each fix is below.
  • Building what you could buy. Internal builds reached daily use about half as often as partnered ones, so test the off-the-shelf option on the same cases first.1,4
  • Messy or scattered documents. Most firms (63%) lack or are unsure of AI-ready data practices, so test on 50 real records before you build.8,6
  • Model retirements and price changes break the build. Two Google prices we checked double, on 1 Dec 2026 and 1 Jan 2027; vendors retire models on 2 to 6 months' notice.17,25,26,27
  • Quality slips without anyone noticing. Quality decayed over time in 91% of 128 model and dataset pairs in one study, so rerun your test set monthly.28,29
  • A contract that leaves the build with the vendor. Government buying guides say settle ownership, data rights and exit terms before signing.13,36
  • Cost surprises at full volume. Gartner warns cost estimates can be off by 500% to 1,000%, so price the pilot at real volume, not demo volume.9

How do you judge any AI advisor?

Judge an advisor by whether they set a pass mark before any demo, test on 20 to 50 of your own cases, and compare against off-the-shelf tools.38,23,10

  1. Judge them by a test on your own cases: a pass mark set before the demo, run against an off-the-shelf tool and today's process.12,23
  2. Write the pass mark before any demo, such as the share of fields that must be right, and require every answer to cite its source.38,12
  3. Pull 20 to 50 real cases from your files, messy ones included, and keep them from the vendor until test day.23,12
  4. Have two staff mark the right answers on their own, and drop cases they disagree on.23
  5. Run the custom build, an off-the-shelf tool and today's process on the same cases.10,7
  6. Run each case more than once, since AI answers vary from run to run.23
  7. Ask the vendor in writing how they tested any accuracy claim, and on what data. The FTC has acted against AI accuracy claims made without solid evidence.39,40
  8. Measure cost per case at your real volume, not just accuracy.9
  9. Price the lift in dollars: minutes saved x cases a month x loaded cost per minute (pay plus benefits). Subtract AI usage and upkeep.
  10. Rerun the same set every month after go-live and whenever the model changes.12,10

Questions buyers ask about Custom AI solutions

How much does custom AI development cost?

AI projects reviewed on Clutch typically cost $10,000 to $49,999, and average about $120,600 over 10 months.41 Gartner puts an enterprise custom app at $750,000 to $1 million upfront; model usage ran just $35 to $1,400 a month in our check.42,15,17 Building, data preparation, testing and upkeep are the big costs, so ask for each priced separately.30,9

Can I create my own custom AI?

Yes, usually by connecting a bought model to your own documents, not by training one from scratch.5,6 Google's engineers advise launching without machine learning until you have the data.7

Should we fine-tune on our documents or use RAG?

Use RAG (retrieval-augmented generation: search that hands the AI the right passages). It beat fine-tuning (further training the model on your examples) for adding facts in a Microsoft study.24,5 RAG also cites its sources; tune only for format or sorting.5,6

Does the AI vendor train on our data, and who owns the output?

No, not on paid business plans: Anthropic and OpenAI don't train on your content, and their terms say you own the outputs.31,32,33 Google's paid Gemini tier isn't used for training, but its free tier may be, so keep confidential files off it.35,34

Should we build, buy or assemble AI solutions for our business?

Buy the model, and build only the layer that holds your data and workflow.4,1 In one investor's survey of US enterprise decision-makers, 76% of AI use cases were bought rather than built in 2025, up from 53%.3

When should you not hire help?

Don't hire help when a rule or no-code tool does the job, bought seats cover drafting and search, or your ERP or CRM already has AI built in.7,3,4

  • When the job follows clear rules: use a rule or a no-code tool such as Zapier or Power Automate. Google says a simple rule often gets you about half the way.7
  • Staff need drafting, summaries and search across email and files: buy seats of ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude for Work, Microsoft 365 Copilot or Gemini for Workspace.3,1
  • Your ERP (finance system) or CRM (customer database) already has AI built in: try it before building.4 Our AI integration guide shows what each system connects to.
