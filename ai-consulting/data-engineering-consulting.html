What is data engineering consulting, and what does it change?

Data engineering consulting builds the pipes, checks and data models that turn your system records into reliable data for reports and AI.1,2 One RAND interviewee put data work at 80 percent of AI.3 Fitting the data to one named AI use makes or breaks it.4,5

What should you walk away with?

Walk away with one AI use and its fields mapped, one subject area live, written data tests, a before-and-after quality score and a spend cap.5,6,8

  • One named AI use, with the fields it needs listed and each one's owner. Gartner's first step is to align data to the use case.5,13
  • One subject area live and used, such as orders or invoices, not a full platform plan. Kimball and Microsoft both say build in increments, most important first.6,7
  • Written data tests and freshness rules your team can run, such as no blank customer IDs and no duplicate invoices.8,9
  • A data-quality score before and after the work. Among middle-market executives whose AI pilots had moderate or limited success, 53% blamed data quality.10,12
  • A monthly spend cap in the warehouse, with alerts set before anyone runs a query.34,33

Which kind of help do you need?

Most firms need data cleanup first, then reports, AI connections to the ERP and CRM, and models, in that order, because data quality is the top AI blocker.11,4

Where does your team stay in charge?

Business owners sign off each data set's definitions, a data steward decides each fix the checks flag, and business users accept each release.12,13,14

  • The business owner of each data set signs off its definitions: the controller for finance data, the ops lead for orders. Gartner names lack of ownership as a common data quality challenge.12,13
  • A named data steward fixes what the automated checks flag. The checks do the first pass; the steward decides each fix.13,8
  • Business users accept each release before the old report is switched off. Kimball calls business acceptance of each deliverable the overarching goal.14
  • The COO picks the problem and stays with it. RAND tells leaders to commit to a problem for at least a year.3

What shapes can the work take?

Stay on the ERP when one system holds the data, add a small Fabric capacity in a Microsoft shop, or use a pay-per-use warehouse across mixed systems.15,17,23

1. Stay on the ERP and clean the data (about 40 to 100 staff, one main system)

Pick this when one ERP (enterprise resource planning system) holds most of the data and the AI use needs standard fields. Ready-made ERP reports avoid a pipeline build.15,16

  1. COOName the AI use and the report behind it
  2. ControllerList the critical fields and who owns each
  3. Data stewardRun tests for blanks and duplicates on those fields
  4. AnalystInstall the ERP's ready-made Power BI apps or analytics add-on
  5. Ops leadTry the AI use in one team, then retire the spreadsheet exports once the numbers match

Cost: Business Central's Power BI apps need a Power BI Pro licence per user, or a Power BI or Fabric Premium capacity.15 NetSuite publishes no price and quotes each deal; a small Fabric capacity, the nearest public figure, is about $263 a month by our math.16,17

2. Microsoft shop with a small Fabric capacity (about 100 to 300 staff)

Pick this when your data sits in Business Central, Dynamics 365 or other Microsoft tools. Fabric can keep a live copy of those tables with no ETL (extract, transform and load) code to write.20,21

  1. IT leadSet who can see which tables before anything is copied
  2. Data engineerMirror the ERP tables into Fabric
  3. Data engineerModel the most important subject area first
  4. ControllerReconcile totals to the books
  5. COOApprove the first AI use on the new store

Cost: Fabric bills $0.18 per capacity-unit (CU) hour, so the smallest F2 size runs $262.80 a month pay-as-you-go by our math.17 A one-year reservation ($938 per CU) cuts F2 to about $156 a month by our math; mirroring's copy compute is free.18,19

3. Several systems, not all Microsoft (about 150 to 500 staff)

Pick this when the ERP, CRM (customer relationship management) and other tools disagree and none of them is Microsoft's. Snowflake bills only while a warehouse runs, and BigQuery by the data each query scans.33,27

  1. COOPick the AI use and its two or three source systems
  2. IT leadSet who can see which data before anything is copied
  3. Data engineerConnect each source with a managed connector tool, such as Fivetran
  4. Data engineerShrink the warehouse size and set a spend cap
  5. Data stewardWrite tests for each critical field
  6. Division headAccept the first subject area

Cost: A Snowflake X-Small warehouse costs $2.00 an hour while it runs on Standard, and a Databricks 2X-Small serverless one $2.80, by our math.23,24,25 Fivetran's own Standard-plan example for a firm of 1-200 employees totals $511.15 a month.28

Why do most AI pilots stall?

AI pilots stall when the data doesn't fit the use, the build tries everything at once, compute runs uncapped, or nobody owns broken pipelines.3,6,33

  • The data doesn't fit the use. RAND names missing data as a root cause of AI failure, so map the use's fields before any build.3,4
  • Gartner predicts organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data. Test the data against the one use before money goes into models.5
  • A big-bang build that ships nothing for months. Kimball warns against it; ship one subject area, then the next.6,7
  • Runaway compute. A new Databricks SQL warehouse defaults to X-Large, and Snowflake warehouses without monitors risk runaway costs, so size down and cap spend on day one.26,33
  • Pipelines break and nobody owns the fix. In a Fivetran vendor survey of 500 leaders at firms over 5,000 staff, pipelines failed 4.7 times a month, each fix taking nearly 13 hours.29
  • Wrong numbers reach people unless tests catch them first. In a dbt Labs vendor survey of 363 data practitioners and leaders, 71% named that a top concern.32,8

How do you judge any AI advisor?

Judge an advisor by whether they start from one AI use, size the build per source system, score data quality first and run old and new reports side by side.4,35,12

  1. Ask them to start from one named AI use. Gartner says data readiness depends on the use, so an advisor who starts with all your data is guessing.4
  2. Have them size the build per source system. TDWI's ballpark scores each system 3, 9 or 15 worker-days, plus a margin for uncertainty.35
  3. Agree the two or three quality measures that matter for the use, and score them before work starts. Gartner advises picking two or three per use.12,36
  4. Ask for written tests on each critical field: not blank, unique, from an allowed list, and matching a real record elsewhere.8
  5. Check that each data feed arrived on time, at least twice as often as reports need it: every 12 hours for a daily report.9
  6. Test any AI output on a realistic sample of your own records, not a demo set.37
  7. Run the old report and the new one side by side, and switch only when the totals match. Databricks, a vendor, advises running benchmark queries alongside legacy systems before cutover.38
  8. Ask for a handover: written notes on each data set, who fixes a broken feed, and every account in your firm's name. RAND warns that without notes, losing a data engineer can leave no one knowing which data sets are reliable.3
  9. Price the result: hours a month spent fixing broken data feeds today, times what an hour of that person's time costs you.29

Questions buyers ask about data engineering consulting

Do we need a data warehouse before we start AI?

No, because readiness depends on the AI use, not on owning a warehouse.4 Business Central, for one, ships ready-made Power BI apps that report without a separate warehouse.15

Should a firm our size pick Fabric or Snowflake?

Pick Fabric if you run Microsoft systems, because it mirrors Business Central and Dataverse with no pipeline code.20,21 Fabric bills a fixed capacity from $0.18 per CU hour; Snowflake bills $2.00 per credit while a warehouse runs.17,23 By our count, Snowflake shows up in 16.1% of data job ads at firms your size and Fabric in 4.7%.31

What does the data software cost a month?

A small Fabric capacity runs about $263 a month by our math, pay-as-you-go.17 A Snowflake X-Small warehouse costs $2.00 an hour while it runs, and BigQuery's first 1 TiB (tebibyte) of queries a month is free.23,27

How long does a data engineering project take?

No neutral study gives a week count, so plan in increments, with the most important subject area first.7,6 Databricks, a vendor, says a proof of concept takes weeks and a production pilot several months.38

Can we report straight from the ERP?

Yes, for standard reports: Business Central ships Power BI apps for finance, sales and inventory.15 For more, mirror up to 1,000 Business Central tables into Fabric, a feature still in preview.20

Should we hire a data engineer instead?

Only when the work will fill a full-time role for years. For comparison, database architects earned a median $139,500 in May 2025.30 By our count, 41 data and analytics ads at 40-500 staff firms posted a median pay midpoint of $131,500; only 9 data-engineer ads posted pay.31

When should you not hire help?

Skip outside help when your ERP's own reports cover the questions, your Microsoft data can be mirrored, or volumes fit a free tier.15,19,27

  • Your reports come from one ERP and the questions are standard: install Business Central's ready-made Power BI apps instead.15
  • Your data already sits in Dataverse or Business Central: use Link to Fabric or mirroring, which need no pipeline build.21,19
  • Your data is small and queried now and then: BigQuery's first 1 TiB of queries a month is free.27 Fivetran's free plan moves up to 500,000 monthly active rows.28
  • No one can commit to one problem for a year: wait. RAND tells leaders to stay with a problem at least that long.3
