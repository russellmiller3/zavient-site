What is AI consulting for small businesses, and what does it change?

AI consulting for a small business is outside help that picks one or two jobs for AI and gets your staff using it.11,1 What changes: AI drafts and sorts the work, and your people check it and make the calls.12,2 Staff with AI help resolved 15% more issues per hour.6

What should you walk away with?

You should leave with a one-page AI use rule, one named owner with set hours, and one pilot with a before-and-after number.3,1,11

  • A one-page use rule: which tools, what data may go in, and who checks the output. Only 36% of small and mid-market owners have a written AI policy.3,13
  • One named owner on your staff, with hours set aside each week. NIST's manufacturing program puts it plainly: "It can't be this person's third job."1,2
  • One pilot with a before-and-after number, such as hours or errors per job. The SBA and New York's Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) center both advise starting small.11,14
  • A short list of what not to use AI for. On tasks outside AI's reach, consultants using it were 19 points less likely to be right.15
  • Trained staff, not just bought seats. At firms of 50+ staff, 43% of workers got no formal AI training, says security vendor Huntress.16

Where does your team stay in charge?

Your people check every AI draft and make the calls, with the owner-CEO setting the use rule and signing off anything customer-facing.12,2

  • Owner-CEO: sets the use rule and signs off any AI that talks to customers or touches hiring. NIST's AI framework puts AI risk decisions on executive leadership.2,17
  • Ops lead or GM (general manager): runs the pilot day to day, picks the job, tracks the number and can stop it. At a 200-person chemical maker, the process engineer led the AI test.1,18
  • Controller: checks any AI output that feeds a price, invoice or forecast. Only 25% of owners have steps to verify AI output before decisions.3,12
  • HR lead: owns any AI used in hiring, reviews or discipline. Colorado's law covers AI in job decisions from 1 Jan 2027, with notice and human review.19,20

What shapes can the work take?

The work scales with firm size: one writing job at about 40 staff, one handoff fix at about 75, and one department pilot at about 150.12,24,1

1. About 40 staff: a use rule, then one writing job

Pick this when the owner still makes most calls and staff use free AI tools. Writing tasks are where small firms use AI most (83% of users).12,21

  1. Owner-CEOWrite a one-page use rule: tools, what data goes in, who checks
  2. Ops leadPick one writing-heavy job, like quotes or customer replies, and time it for two weeks
  3. Ops leadBuy business seats for the pilot team only
  4. Person sending each draftCheck every AI draft before it goes out
  5. ControllerSpot-check any draft with a price or number in it
  6. Owner-CEOAfter six to eight weeks, keep it, change it or stop

Cost: Ten Standard seats on ChatGPT Business or Claude Team cost $2,400 a year at list price.22,23 The bigger cost is staff time for the baseline, checks and training.

2. About 75 staff: find the hidden AI use, then fix one handoff

Pick this when staff likely use personal AI accounts. In write-ups of Census data, about 18% of firms with 50 to 99 staff don't know if staff use AI.24,4

  1. Ops leadRun a short anonymous survey of who uses which AI tool, and for what
  2. Owner-CEOApprove one company tool and move people onto it
  3. Ops leadPick one slow handoff, like intake to scheduling or order to invoice
  4. ControllerSet the before-and-after number, such as days or errors per order
  5. Team leadsTrain their people on the approved tool and the use rule
  6. Owner-CEOAt eight weeks, keep it, change it or stop

Cost: One business plan for 75 people runs about $12,600 to $18,900 a year at list price.25,22,26 Linking two systems adds build work, so scope and price it separately.

3. About 150 staff: one department pilot, with a go-or-stop check before rollout

Pick this when one department head can own the work, with real time set aside. NIST's manufacturing program advises piloting one site or line before scaling.1,14

  1. Owner-CEOName one department head as owner, with hours set aside
  2. Department headPlan most effort on people and process, not the AI itself
  3. HR leadCheck whether anything touches hiring, reviews or discipline
  4. Department headRun the pilot in one team or site and measure it
  5. Owner-CEORoll out only with a measured gain, a written check step and trained users

Cost: Claude Team stops at 150 seats; Microsoft and Google business plans stop at 300 users.23,26,25 Price is driven by seats, staff time and any systems you connect.

Why do most AI pilots stall?

Most stalled AI pilots fail on people and process: a fuzzy problem, an owner with no time, or a build bought before the basics.8,1,30

  • Pilots stall when the problem is fuzzy; RAND found a misunderstood problem is the most common root cause.8 Name one job and one number first: 42% of firms dropped most AI projects in 2025.27,28
  • Staff paste company data into personal AI accounts; security vendor LayerX says 82% of pastes come from unmanaged accounts.29 Approve one business tool and write what data may go in.3
  • AI gets things wrong: accuracy is the top problem small-firm AI users report (46%).12 Your firm owns what its chatbot tells customers, so a person checks anything customer-facing.17
  • An owner with no time stalls the work; NIST's manufacturing program warns it "can't be this person's third job".1 Owner-CEO: block real hours on the ops lead's calendar before kickoff.
  • AI in hiring or reviews faces new state rules; Colorado's law covers job decisions from 1 Jan 2027.19 As of Nov 2026, a federal court order pauses enforcement, so have your HR lead check first.20
  • Buying a build before the basics wastes money. Gartner expects firms to drop 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data, so check your data on one job first.30

How do you judge any AI advisor?

Judge any AI advisor by whether they name one job, one number that proves it worked, and who on your staff owns it after they leave.14,1

  1. Record today's numbers for one job for two weeks: volume, hours, errors and turnaround.11,14
  2. Ask which one job they would start with, and what number proves it worked.14 Walk away from a plan with no named job.
  3. Ask what they would not use AI for here. A good advisor rules some jobs out.15
  4. Ask who on your staff owns it after they leave, and for how many hours a week.1,2
  5. Ask whether it can run on tools you already pay for, like Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace.26,25
  6. Ask to see the use rule they would write, and who signs it. NIST lists acceptable-use policies as a basic step for generative AI.13
  7. Hold back real past cases the advisor never sees, and test the output on them before you trust it.
  8. Price the lift: hours saved a month times loaded cost per hour (pay plus benefits). Subtract seat costs, upkeep and the hours your checkers spend.
  9. Ask whether any paid mapping work ends in a pilot.

Questions buyers ask about AI consulting for small businesses

How much does an AI consultant cost?

On the US government's published price list, AI roles have a median hourly ceiling of $183 to $194.35,36 Commercial fees aren't published, so get a fixed price in writing; most consultants price by project or by the hour.37 Business AI seats add $14 to $21 per person a month.25,26

What is the 10-20-70 rule for AI?

BCG's 10-20-70 rule of thumb says about 10% of AI effort goes to algorithms, 20% to technology and data, and 70% to people and process.7 RAND's interviews point the same way: the top failure is a misunderstood problem.8

What is the best AI program for a small business?

Usually the one built into what you already pay for. Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace both sell AI on business plans of up to 300 users.26,25 Pick on fit with your email and files, not on rankings.

When does a custom AI agent make sense, versus ChatGPT or our existing tools?

A custom agent makes sense when a job's steps can't be fixed in advance and simpler tools have already fallen short.38 That is rare in a first project at this size: most small-firm AI use is writing that business seats cover.12 Gartner says many jobs sold as agents don't need one.31

Should a consultant map our workflows first, or is that wasted money?

Map only the job you will pilot, and tie the mapping to that pilot. RAND found a misunderstood problem is the top reason AI projects fail.8

When should you not hire help?

Skip paid help under about 40 staff, or before you can pay: a free SBDC advisor or your state's MEP center is a fair first step.9,10

  • Under about 40 staff, or not ready to pay: start with a free Small Business Development Center (SBDC) advisor trained in AI.9 America's SBDC says its AI U program has coached 8,000+ businesses.9
  • You make things: call your state's Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) center first.10 Some help fund staff AI training, as Maryland's did for a family apple processor.32
  • Staff only need help with everyday writing: buy business seats from the table above and train people.12,22
  • One clear workflow between two systems: see our AI automation consulting guide, and keep the first project small enough to manage.14 A plan across several departments: see AI strategy consulting.
