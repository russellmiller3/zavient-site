What is AI implementation services, and what does it change?

AI implementation services get one AI use case you already chose working in your own systems, used by your own team.1,2 What changes: the pilot becomes daily work, with AI drafting, sorting or flagging while people keep every decision.11,3 System hookups and staff use make or break it.5,3

What should you walk away with?

You should leave with your chosen use case working in your systems, a named owner on your side, a starting number, and everything in your own accounts.2,15

  • A named owner on your side, with a monthly review of errors, overrides and use. A good handover names the team receiving the system and its support duties.2,11
  • A starting number for the task, measured before the build, and the number it should move. Microsoft's testing guide says to set a baseline before any go or no-go call.15,7
  • The logins, code, prompts, test results and a runbook (the how-to for running it), all in your accounts. Practitioners flag any vendor who can't say who owns the prompts and data at the end.3
  • A written line between what the AI may do on its own and what waits for a person. NIST, the US standards agency, asks firms to define and document human oversight.11,2
  • A team trained to work the new way, not just a working system. Regular AI use is sharply higher after five or more hours of training.9,10

Where does your team stay in charge?

A named person on your team signs off anything that reaches customers or moves money, and the AI only drafts, sorts or flags.11,3

  • The process owner, such as the ops lead, controller or claims lead, owns the workflow and its number. They decide which outputs a person checks.11,2
  • The AI drafts, sorts or flags; a named reviewer approves anything that reaches customers or moves money. Those review points are written down before launch.11,3
  • Someone other than the builder checks the test results. People can override the system or roll it back to the old process.11,12
  • An executive signs off on the risk, and the vendor never owns the decision. Two in three AI-using firms use it only to help staff with tasks.11,20

What shapes can the work take?

Start with one task in tools you pay for, use Microsoft's free guided rollout at 150+ licences, and connect your ERP for document-heavy work.19,17,5

1. Small team, about 40 to 120 staff: one task in tools you already pay for

Pick this for one repeat task, like drafting customer replies or summarizing intake forms, in Microsoft 365. Microsoft's free Success Kit has scenario guides and an adoption checklist.19

  1. Ops leadPick one weekly task and record today's time and error rate
  2. Process ownerWrite what the AI may draft and what a person must check
  3. IT leadTurn on Copilot for a pilot group and check who can see which files
  4. Named reviewerCheck every output while the old way still runs alongside
  5. Ops leadCompare with the starting number, decide go, fix or stop, then widen one team at a time

Cost: Microsoft 365 Copilot Business is $21 per user a month, paid yearly, or $18 in a first-year offer ending 31 Dec 2026.18 License the pilot group first, and buy any outside help at a fixed price and scope.

2. Mid-size firm on Microsoft 365 with 150 or more licences: free guided rollout plus your own review

Pick this if you hold 150 or more eligible Microsoft 365 licences and at least one Copilot licence.16 Microsoft's FastTrack team then guides the rollout at no extra cost.17

  1. IT leadSign up for FastTrack and fix files shared too widely in SharePoint first
  2. Ops leadPick 2 or 3 roles and record their starting numbers
  3. ManagersName a reviewer for each team and coach use every week
  4. Team leadsGive each person at least five hours of training built around their own tasks
  5. Ops leadTrack use and results monthly in Copilot Analytics
  6. COOWiden to the next team only if the numbers moved

Cost: FastTrack guidance is free; licences are the main cost, from $21 per user a month, or $32 bundled with Business Premium.17,18 Microsoft's Business plans cover up to 300 users.18

3. Document-heavy work: AI wired into your ERP or CRM

Pick this when invoices, orders, claims or contracts move between systems. Integration sets the timeline: Deloitte found three-month estimates can stretch to 18 months.5

  1. Controller or process ownerList what data actually exists, before anyone scopes the build
  2. BuilderConnect to a test copy of the ERP (finance system) or CRM (customer database)
  3. Process ownerLock a set of real past cases, messy ones included
  4. BuilderRun the AI in shadow mode, where it works alongside people but decides nothing
  5. Named reviewerCompare its output with the team's own decisions
  6. Ops leadGo live with one team, keeping a way back to the old process
  7. Process ownerSign off, then review errors and overrides every month

Cost: Quoted per job, because the price depends on the systems it touches.21 Run a short data audit first.

Why do most AI pilots stall?

Most AI pilots stall before daily use, usually at hooking into existing systems and at security review.5,7

  • Integration stretches the timeline: Deloitte found three-month estimates can stretch to 18 months.5 Fix: map every system and login the AI touches before you sign.2
  • Ideas die at IT and security review: in a study Adobe paid for, only about 15 of 100 reached production.7 Fix: bring IT and security in during week one, not at go-live.
  • Staff drift back to the old tools after the first error. Fix: pick work that fits their tools and get managers to back it; frequent use is 88% versus 55% when AI fits.8
  • The pilot ran on hand-picked clean data. Fix: test on real past cases, messy ones included; OpenAI warns against test sets unlike real traffic.14
  • Nobody owns it after the vendor leaves. Fix: name the owner before the build starts.2
  • No starting number, so no proof it worked. Fix: measure today's time and errors before any build, then make a go or no-go call.15,7

How do you judge any AI advisor?

Judge any AI advisor by who owns the system after handover, and whether they test on your real past cases against a starting number.2,14,15

  1. Write down today's number for the one task: time per case, error rate and cost per case.15
  2. Pull a set of real past cases, messy ones included, and lock them away before the vendor sees results.14
  3. Have someone other than the builder score the results against your team's own decisions.11,14
  4. Run it in shadow mode first: the AI works alongside your team, but people decide and nothing it outputs is used.12
  5. Give the shadow run a fixed end date. Amazon's shadow tests run 7 days by default, and 30 at most.13
  6. Score against people's judgement, not gut feel. OpenAI lists "it seems like it's working" as a mistake to avoid.14
  7. Compare with the starting number and make a written go, fix or stop call.15
  8. Go live with one team first, and keep a way back to the old process.12
  9. Price the lift in dollars: hours saved times loaded cost per hour (pay plus benefits), plus errors avoided times cost per error. Then subtract licences, upkeep and reviewer time.
  10. Name the owner and set a monthly review of errors, overrides and use.11

Questions buyers ask about AI implementation services

What does an AI implementation consultant actually do?

An AI implementation consultant takes a use case you've already picked and makes it work in your real systems, with your data, people and controls.3,2 That covers integration, testing, rollout, training and handover. Deciding what to do first is strategy work, a different purchase.2

How much does AI implementation cost?

For a small rollout in Microsoft 365, licences start at $21 per user a month, and guided setup is free at 150 or more licences.18,17 Custom work into an ERP or CRM is quoted per job. Our own paid work starts after a free 30-minute diagnostic, with a fixed scope agreed up front.21

What do AI implementation vendors promise, and what actually happens?

Most vendors promise training and support; what often happens is a late launch that staff abandon at the first error.22,5 Three-month estimates can stretch to 18 months once system hookups get hard.5 Few vendors publish a price, measure a starting number or name an owner on your side.22

How do you get the holdouts on your team to use it?

Separate people who can't yet from people who won't; each needs different help. Frequent use runs 88% where AI fits existing systems, against 55% where it doesn't; manager support shows a similar gap.8 Train people on their own tasks; use is sharply higher after five hours or more.9,10

How can we avoid being scammed on AI implementation?

Ask for a price, the working system you'll get, and who owns the prompts and data at the end. Ask how hard cases will be tested and who runs the system after handover.3

When should you not hire help?

Skip implementation help if you haven't picked a use case, qualify for Microsoft's free FastTrack, have someone in-house to run it, or only need writing help.2,17,19,18

  • You haven't picked the use case yet: get strategy help first. Implementation assumes the decision is already made.2
  • You hold 150 or more eligible Microsoft 365 licences and one Copilot licence: start with Microsoft's free FastTrack guidance.16,17
  • You have someone in-house to run it: use Microsoft's free Copilot Success Kit, with its scenario library and adoption checklist.19
  • Staff only need help writing and summarizing: buy AI seats and train people, with no implementation project.18,9
