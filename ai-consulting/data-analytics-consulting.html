Data analytics consulting: what should you walk away with?

Bottom line
  • Analytics should help managers decide faster, not decide for them. Start from one weekly decision.1,2
  • Fix the data first. Most firms don't measure data quality, and two-thirds of data staff don't fully trust it.3,4
  • Count weekly users, not dashboards built. Only a quarter of staff use business intelligence (BI) tools daily.5,6
  • A year of BI licenses for a 100-person firm often costs less than one build project.7,8
  • Add forecasts and scoring models only once the data is trusted. A named person owns each model's use.9,2

What is Data analytics consulting, and what does it change?

Data analytics consulting gets your numbers straight, then builds the dashboards, forecasts and models so managers decide from facts, not guesses.10,11 AI adds the forecasts and scores, on top of clean records.12 Trusted data and daily use make or break it.4,5

What should you walk away with?

Walk away with one weekly decision to improve, a data-quality score, agreed metric definitions, one dashboard used weekly, and a handover your team can run.1,14,4,5,15

  • You should leave with one named weekly decision, a data-quality score, agreed metric definitions, one dashboard used weekly and a handover your team can run.1,5
  • One named weekly decision, with an owner and today's number to beat. Firms that scale analytics start from the decision and work back to the data.1,13
  • A data-quality score on 100 of your own recent records. Most firms never measure it, and in one study 47% of new records had a critical error.3,14
  • One agreed definition per metric, signed off by the controller. Inconsistent definitions are a top barrier to quality data (45%).4,3
  • One dashboard used every week, tracked by active users. Average BI use is 25% of staff; smaller firms reach 44%.5,6
  • A handover your team can run: documents, training, and who refreshes what. Lack of proper training was the top obstacle to BI use (50%).15

Which kind of analytics do you need?

Start with clean data and one dashboard, then add sales analytics, demand forecasts or scoring models, picked by the decision you want to improve.1,5,12

Where does your team stay in charge?

The manager who owns each decision keeps it, a named business owner owns each metric, and the controller signs off any number that ties to the books.2,3

  • The manager who owns the decision (COO, ops lead or head of sales) keeps it; the dashboard or model is one input. NIST (the US standards agency) treats AI as an additional opinion and says human roles must be defined.2
  • A named business owner, not IT alone, owns each metric and its data. Gartner calls data quality a business discipline that needs owners.3
  • Someone independent challenges each forecast or score before use, with the standing to force changes. US bank regulators call this effective challenge, a useful standard for any firm.9,16
  • The controller signs off any number that ties to the books. Each model's users stay accountable for using it as intended and minding its known limits.17

What shapes can the work take?

Start with one dashboard for one weekly decision, join systems into one data store when numbers disagree, and add forecasts once the data is trusted.18,3,12

1. Small team, one decision (about 40 to 100 staff)

Pick this when one weekly decision runs on spreadsheets and the data sits in one or two systems. Price follows the number of sources and how clean they are.18,19

  1. Ops leadName the weekly decision and today's number
  2. IT leadConfirm who may see the data
  3. AnalystScore 100 recent records for errors
  4. ControllerAgree each metric's definition
  5. AnalystBuild one dashboard in a tool you already own
  6. Ops leadUse it weekly in one team or site for six weeks, counting active users

Cost: Two vendors' published guides put one dashboard at about $1,000 to $12,000, and a small reporting project at $5,000 to $20,000.18,19

2. Mid-size firm, several systems (about 100 to 300 staff)

Pick this when finance, sales and ops numbers disagree because they live in separate systems. Gartner names inconsistency across sources the hardest data-quality problem.3,4

  1. COOPick two or three decisions to support
  2. Data engineerPipe the ERP (finance system), CRM (customer database) and accounting data into one store
  3. IT leadSet who can see which data before anything is shared
  4. ControllerReconcile totals to the books
  5. Division headsOwn their metric definitions
  6. AnalystBuild one dashboard per decision

Cost: Vendor guides put a reporting suite at $12,000 to $30,000, and a data warehouse (one store for all your data) at $25,000 to $80,000.19,20 Clutch's published pricing data puts typical BI projects at $10,000 to $49,999.8

3. Forecasts and scores on top (once the data is trusted)

Pick this once a dashboard is used weekly and its numbers reconcile. Forecasts and recommendations are advanced analytics, built on clean records of what happened.12,10

  1. Division headDefine the decision and what a forecast must beat
  2. AnalystHold back recent data the model never sees
  3. AnalystBuild the forecast or score
  4. Independent reviewerChallenge the model before use
  5. Division headApprove use and set override rules

Cost: One vendor guide puts predictive or AI analytics at $40,000 to $150,000 or more.20 Vendors say most of any build's cost is data work, not charts.21,22

Why do most analytics projects stall?

Analytics projects stall when nobody uses the dashboard, the data is wrong and unchecked, or teams argue about whose number is right.5,14,4

  • Most stall because nobody uses the dashboard, the data is wrong, teams fight over definitions, or the budget goes to tools instead of daily use.5,3,1
  • Nobody uses the dashboard. BI use has sat near a quarter of staff for years, so start from one decision, train people and track weekly users.5,6
  • The data is wrong, and nobody checks. Score 100 recent records before building; in one study, only 3% of department scores met a basic standard.14,3
  • Teams argue about whose number is right. Write one list of agreed metric definitions and have the controller sign it; inconsistent definitions are a top barrier (45%).4,3
  • The budget goes to tools, not daily use. About 90% of breakaway firms spend over half their analytics budget on getting it into daily work, against 23% of others.1,34
  • Buying a tool without a real trial. Top BI performers ran a formal, competitive evaluation far more often (77% against 43%), so trial two tools on your own data.5
  • People ignore what they can't see into. A call-center recommendation engine went unused until it explained its reasons, so show why, not just what.35

How do you judge any analytics advisor?

Judge an advisor by whether they write down today's number first, score 100 of your records, track weekly users and reconcile totals to the books.1,14,5,3

  1. Judge them on your own numbers: today's figure for one decision, a data-quality score, and weekly use after launch.1,5
  2. Write down the one decision and today's number before work starts: cycle time, forecast error or hours spent building reports.1
  3. Score 100 recent records for critical errors, then score them again after cleanup.14
  4. Time how long it takes to answer the decision's question today, then after. In BARC's survey, 97% of users reported faster reporting or planning.5
  5. Count weekly active users at week 4 and week 12, against seats paid for. Compare with BARC's 25% average, or 44% at smaller firms.5,6
  6. For any forecast or score, hold back recent data the model never saw and compare it with your current rule. An independent reviewer signs off before use.9
  7. Reconcile dashboard totals to the books, and have the controller sign off.3
  8. Price the lift: hours saved times loaded cost, plus the value of the better decision. Pay is about 70% of what an employee costs a private employer.36
  9. Keep expectations real. Plants that adopted data-driven decisions were about 3% more productive, and only after adoption.37
  10. Pilot in one unit or site first. Roll out only when weekly use and the decision's number both move.37,5
  11. Ask for a fixed-scope first release, with the code, logins and accounts in your firm's name.18

Questions buyers ask about Data analytics consulting

What does a data analytics consultant actually do?

A data analytics consultant names the decision, gets the data clean and in one place, and builds reports and dashboards.11,10 Good help then adds forecasts or models and trains your team to run them.12,15

Is business intelligence consulting different from data analytics consulting?

Yes: business intelligence mostly shows what happened, while analytics also asks why, what will happen and what to do.12,10 Data strategy consulting picks which decisions and data come first.1

How much does data analytics consulting cost?

Clutch's published data puts typical BI and analytics projects at $10,000 to $49,999.8 Vendor guides price one dashboard at a few thousand dollars and a data warehouse at $25,000 to $80,000.18,19,20 Most of the cost is data work, not charts.21,22

Should we hire an analyst or a consultant?

Use a consultant for a time-boxed build, and hire when the work fills a full-time role for years. Median pay for data scientists, which includes BI analysts, was $120,230 in May 2025.38,39 With benefits, that is about $172,000 a year by our math, since wages are 70.0% of private employer pay costs.36

Which analytics consulting firms are the best?

The best fit is usually a certified partner of your BI tool, like a Microsoft Data & AI Solutions Partner for Power BI.40,41 Gartner's 2022 review of 18 providers, such as Accenture, Deloitte and IBM, picked firms built for enterprise scale and global reach.41 Whichever you pick, it should agree a fixed first release and a weekly-use target.1,5

Which kind of analytics do we need?

Start with clean data and one dashboard, since daily use drives most of the value.1,5 Then pick by decision: which accounts to call (head of sales), how much stock to hold (planner), or who will repay a loan (credit lead).12,10 Our related guides cover sales analytics, demand forecasting, scoring models, credit scoring and claims.

When should you not hire help?

Don't hire help when a free or low-cost tool like Power BI Desktop, Looker Studio or Metabase covers your reports, or when the work fills a full-time role for years.7,24,23,38

  • When you run Microsoft 365 and need a few reports: start with free Power BI Desktop. Pro is $14 a user a month, and Microsoft 365 E5 already includes it.7
  • Your data lives in Google tools: try Google Data Studio (Looker Studio), which has a free tier.24
  • You have one database and a technical person: self-host Metabase's free open-source version.23
  • The work fills a full-time role for years: hire an analyst. Median pay for the role was $120,230 in May 2025.38,39
