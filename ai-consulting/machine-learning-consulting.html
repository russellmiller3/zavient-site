Machine learning consulting: what should you get, and will it work?

Bottom line
  • Insist on a model your team uses weekly, not a demo. Half or more of models never reach use.1,2
  • Test models against today's rule on held-back data. Our benchmark's models caught 76 churners per 100 flagged; the rule, 44.3,4
  • Explainable models often match black-box accuracy on business data, so your team sees why each score.5,6
  • Budget for upkeep, not just the build. Most models lose accuracy over time and need monitoring and retraining.7,8
  • People keep the call: managers can adjust each score, and logged adjustments often improve results.9,10

What is machine learning consulting, and what does it change?

Machine learning consulting builds a model that scores each new customer, loan or order from your own history.3,5 What changes: your team calls the riskiest cases first; on our churn benchmark, 76 in 100 flagged customers left, against 44 for a contract-type rule.4 A named person still decides.

What should you walk away with?

You should hold one named decision with its money number, a scored baseline and held-back test set, a model in weekly use, readable reasons per score, and a handover.3,1,14

  • One named decision and the money number it moves, agreed before any build. Google's rules for machine learning say to design the metric first.3
  • Today's rule, scored, and a held-back test set that you keep. A simple first model sets the baseline; leaked test data makes weak models look strong.3,11
  • A model in your team's weekly routine, not a file on a laptop. Only 32% of analytics professionals surveyed say their models usually get deployed.1,2
  • The reasons behind each score, as charts your managers can read and correct. Glass-box models (ones whose logic you can read) are often as accurate as black boxes.12,13
  • A handover: code, data feed, monitoring and a retrain rule, with a named owner. Google's ML Test Score lists 28 checks to score it against.14,8

Where does your team stay in charge?

People keep the calls: the person acting on the score can override it, the domain lead reviews the model, an independent checker tests it, and credit officers own declines.9,15,16,18

  • The person who acts on the score, such as an account manager or demand planner, makes the call and can override it. Log overrides to see whether they help.9,10
  • The domain lead, such as the head of underwriting or customer success, reviews what the model learned before launch.15,13 One hospital model learned asthma lowers pneumonia risk, only because those patients got intensive care first.15
  • Someone who didn't build the model checks it: internal audit, a risk owner or an outside reviewer. Bank and insurance guidance calls this independent, effective challenge.16,17
  • In credit, the credit officer or underwriter owns every decline and its stated reasons. Reg B, the federal fair-lending rule, says 'failed our score' is not a reason.18

What shapes can the work take?

Start with a yes-or-no score from CRM data, add a model that drafts a weekly plan, and use a glass-box model for regulated decisions.19,10,17

1. One yes-or-no decision, data in your CRM (about 40 to 150 staff)

Pick this for churn or lead priority: one yes-or-no outcome, a few thousand past cases and a team that acts weekly.19 Check your CRM's (customer database's) built-in score first.20,21

  1. GM (general manager)Name the decision and the money number it moves
  2. Ops leadWrite down today's rule and how often it is right
  3. IT leadExport two to three years of history
  4. BuilderHold back the latest 3 to 6 months, unseen in training, then test the rule against two or three models
  5. Account managers in one team or regionWork the top 10% list weekly and log what they did
  6. Ops leadCompare saved revenue with a held-back control group after 60 to 90 days

Cost: Vendor guides put a trial build at about €5,000 to €15,000 (a European vendor) or $15,000 to $80,000.22,23 Zavient's fixed-scope 30-day Sprint delivers ranked bets, specs and a plan, not a model build, so not like for like.24

2. A model that drafts a weekly plan (about 100 to 500 staff)

Pick this for demand, scrap or staffing, when a planner already builds the number by hand. The model gives a first draft, and the planner adjusts it.10,19

  1. Division headPick the plan and what an error costs
  2. Demand plannerRecord today's forecast error for 8 to 12 weeks
  3. BuilderReplay past weeks, testing only on weeks the model never saw
  4. Demand plannerAdjust the draft and log every override
  5. ControllerCheck the inventory or labor cost effect monthly
  6. IT leadSet up monitoring and a retrain trigger (an accuracy drop that starts a refresh)

Cost: Vendor guides put a live model at €15,000 to €45,000 up to $80,000 to $350,000; forecasting at €50,000 to €150,000.22,25,19 They budget 15% to 30% of the build each year for upkeep.22,25

3. A regulated decision: credit, underwriting or insurance pricing

Pick this when a regulator, examiner or customer can ask why. Thirty US jurisdictions have adopted the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) bulletin, which asks insurers for validation and drift checks.17,26

  1. Head of lending or underwritingSet the policy and the reasons you must give
  2. BuilderFit a glass-box model and document its data and limits
  3. Independent validatorCheck the logic, test it on past outcomes and set a monitoring plan
  4. Compliance leadMap model reasons to the reasons on decline letters
  5. UnderwriterDecide edge cases and sign every decline
  6. Risk ownerReview drift (accuracy slipping as data changes) each quarter

Cost: We found no published price for regulated builds. Expect the ranges above, plus time for the validation and documentation that model risk guidance describes.16,27

Does an explainable churn model cost you accuracy?

No: we ran four approaches on IBM's public churn file of 7,032 customers, and the glass-box model matched the black box on unseen customers.4,6 Among the 100 riskiest of every 1,000 customers, each model found about 76 who really left. The one-line contract rule found about 44.

Why do most AI pilots stall?

Most pilots stall because the model never reaches the people who would use it, its accuracy decays without upkeep, or its test scores were too good to be true.28,7,11

  • Test scores that look too good. Leakage, where the model glimpses the answers, hit 294 studies in 17 fields; lock a recent holdout yourself before work starts.11
  • The model never reaches the people who would use it. Studies of Microsoft teams and of 18 ML engineers find deployment and upkeep are the hard part; name the user and screen first.28,29
  • Accuracy decays after launch. One study found decay in 91% of 128 model and dataset pairs; check live accuracy monthly and set a retrain trigger.7,30
  • The model learns the wrong lesson and nobody sees it. Start glass-box and have the domain lead review every chart; Google's rules say readable models are easier to debug.15,3
  • Scores used outside the range they were built for. Zillow wrote down $304 million of homes after its price forecasts missed; cap where scores apply and keep sign-off on big calls.31
  • A churn score is not a save plan: in two field experiments, the customers most likely to leave were not the best ones to target.35 Hold back a small random control group, so you measure customers saved, not just predictions made.

How do you judge any AI advisor?

Judge an advisor by whether their model beats your current rule on a holdout you locked, with reasons you can read and a handover your team can run.3,11,14

  1. Name one decision and the money number it moves, before any data work.3
  2. Write down today's rule and score it. Any model must beat it; our contract rule caught 44 churners per 100 flagged.3,4
  3. Count past outcomes, such as customers who left, not rows. Needed size depends on the number of outcomes and fields, not one rule of thumb.32
  4. Lock a holdout, past cases the model never trains on, before the advisor sees any results. Use your most recent months if you can.11,32
  5. Compare four approaches on that holdout: your rule, logistic regression, a glass-box model and a black box.5,3
  6. Score what your decision needs: real cases caught in the list you will work, and results by region or product. One Google system gained 1% overall while one country fell 50%.14
  7. Ask to see why for one real customer. In our benchmark, one customer's top reasons were one month of tenure, a month-to-month contract and fibre internet.12,4
  8. Price the lift: extra real cases in your list x the share you save x margin, minus the cost of acting, building and upkeep. By our math, the 32 extra churners per 1,000 paid about $28,600 a year: revenue reached, not saved.4
  9. Run it live in one team against a small random control group for 60 to 90 days before you scale.
  10. Check the handover against the ML Test Score's monitoring checks: code, data feed, monitoring, retrain rule and who holds the logins.14

Questions buyers ask about machine learning consulting

How much does machine learning consulting cost?

Vendor guides publish about $40,000 to $400,000 for a custom model, with a typical range of $55,000 to $250,000.23 Trial builds cost less, and vendors budget 15% to 30% of the build a year for upkeep.22,25

How long does a machine learning project take?

One vendor guide says 2 to 6 weeks to check the data and 1 to 3 months for a trial build.33 A first live model takes 4 to 6 months.33 Gartner's survey average was 8 months from prototype to production.2

Do we have enough data?

Only if you have enough outcomes, not rows: count how many customers left, loans went bad or orders slipped.32 Test it with a learning curve: fit on half the data, then all; if scores still climb, more data helps. With a few dozen cases, use a rule instead.

Does predictive modelling actually work, or is it hype?

Yes, predictive modelling works, but only when it beats your current rule on data it never saw.3 On our churn benchmark, models caught about 76 churners per 100 flagged, against 44 for a contract-type rule.4

Will we lose accuracy if the model has to be explainable?

No: on business data, explainable models often match black-box accuracy.5 InterpretML's own table puts its glass-box model at 0.852 on telecom churn, against 0.828 for a popular black box.6 Ours scored 0.849 against 0.837 for an untuned black box.4

Can our team keep the model running after the consultant leaves?

Only if the handover includes the data feed, monitoring, a retrain rule and a named owner.14,8

When should you not hire help?

Skip a consultant when your CRM already scores leads, when a rule or spreadsheet will do, or when an analyst can try InterpretML or Google's AutoML first.20,3,6,34

  • Your CRM already scores leads: turn on its scoring and test it on your holdout first.20,21 Salesforce's tool wants 1,000 leads in 200 days, with 120 converted.20
  • Too few cases, or one obvious driver: use a rule or a spreadsheet score. Google's rules say a simple rule can get you about half the gain.3
  • You have an analyst and one clean table: try free InterpretML or Google's AutoML first.6,34 Google lists AutoML training on tables at $21.252 per hour of computing.34
